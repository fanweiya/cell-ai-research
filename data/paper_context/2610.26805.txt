Error bars, 95% bootstrap CIs. 4.1 Report text adds prognostic signal that survives stage redaction Vision–language foundation models report better survival prediction than pure-vision models [ 4 , 5 ] , but reports state tumour stage, so the gain could merely restate it. The clean increment B ′ − A B^{\prime}-A was positive and significant in all six cohort–endpoint settings and pooled to + 0.019 +0.019 C-index for PFI and + 0.016 +0.016 for OS (Fig. 1 b). Stage does not explain it: both leakage contrasts were null, the redacted report alone was at least as prognostic as the slide embedding, and structured stage and grade (arm C) formed the weakest arm (Fig. 1 a). The increment is therefore not a restatement of the stage statement. It is information in the report that complements the slide, captured by a frozen text encoder rather than by a VLM, and redaction leaves stage-correlated findings such as tumour size in place. 4.2 Language pays through pretrained features, not prompts Language enters a contrastive VLM twice, as pretraining supervision and as zero-shot prompts; if predictive power lives in the features, a laboratory can replace prompts with a supervised classifier and avoid prompt sensitivity. Under identical supervised probes, all three language-aligned encoders outperformed Prov-GigaPath on both tasks (Fig. 1 c). A probe uses no prompt, so the advantage resides in the features; but the encoders also differ in backbone, data, dimension and aggregation, so it cannot be attributed to language. The within-encoder arm isolates the prompt: with CONCH v1 features and pooling fixed, zero-shot classification was statistically indistinguishable from the linear probe (balanced-accuracy difference − 0.020 -0.020 for NSCLC and − 0.019 -0.019 for RCC; Fig. 1 d). Top- k k zero-shot classification scored higher, but its margin vanished under shared pooling, an instance-selection rather than a prompt effect. With CONCH ranked first of 19 foundation models as a weakly supervised feature extractor in an independent benchmark [ 9 ] , the evidence indicates that, for in-distribution subtyping, vision–language pretraining pays, if at all, through the features it shapes rather than through prompts. Error bars, question-level bootstrap 95% CIs; asterisks, CI excludes zero; n.s., not significant. 4.3 Slide-level VQA accuracy does not depend on the slide A multiple-choice benchmark can be passed without looking, by exploiting answer distributions and option positions. SlideChat answered 74.82% of items correctly with the slide, and its text-only and mismatched-slide gaps were indistinguishable from zero, including on grading and staging questions; WSI-LLaVA lost a few points, significantly (Fig. 2 a,b). The prior is learned in training: Qwen2.5-7B, SlideChat’s own backbone, answered 43.52% correctly without the slide, close to the 40.7% letter baseline, whereas SlideChat, equally blind, answered 74.41%, so about 31 points come from regularities of its instruction-tuning data rather than from the slide. The assistants were instead sensitive to answer format: shifting option positions lowered accuracy significantly, and substituting NOTA for the correct option pushed SlideChat below the letter baseline, so neither model rejects the remaining options once the correct content is removed. 4.4 Rewording leaves accuracy intact but changes the answers Under paraphrase alone, the accuracy change was indistinguishable from zero for all four systems (Fig. 2 d), so we detect no accuracy cost. The answers, however, were unstable: 8–23% of items changed answer under paraphrase and 25% under paraphrase plus reframing, where consistency fell to 0.57. Even SlideChat, the most stable system, changed a quarter of its answers when only the framing sentence varied, and a single persona line shifted Qwen3-VL’s accuracy significantly, the only accuracy changes whose intervals excluded zero. Leaderboards cannot see this instability; in the clinic, where the same case must yield the same diagnosis, it is disqualifying. 4.5 Self-reported gains cannot yet be attributed Two further claims rest on self-reported evidence (tier 3; Fig. 2 c). Agentic navigation produces its largest margins where they are least separable. CPathAgent reaches 88.6% on PathMMU-HR 2 , 15.4 points above Gemini-2.5-Pro answering in a single pass, yet at slide level it leads ABMIL by 2.9 points on average and trails TITAN on CPTAC-Lung (88.1% versus 91.9%) [ 16 ] . The margin thus depends on scale, and only part of it is navigation: prompted to navigate in the same way, Gemini-2.5-Pro rises from 73.2% to 76.4%. With no intervals or seeds reported, neither component can be tested. Language priors in MIL earn smaller and more consistent margins: ViLa-MIL, VLEER and GMAT report few-shot gains over language-free aggregators while training fewer than 10 7 10^{7} parameters [ 34 , 35 , 36 ] , for example 1.7 and 5.9 AUC points for ViLa-MIL over CLAM-MB with 16 shots (Fig. 2 c). Every such comparison comes from the proposing paper without independent or leakage-controlled replication, so we grade this evidence as weakly supported. 5 Discussion What accuracy does not capture. Visibility reorders clinical risk: the hallucinations of generative systems surface as text that a reader can check, whereas score-only systems (contrastive encoders and VLM-augmented MIL) fail through correlational shortcuts, such as a frozen encoder passing a tissue-source-site signature onward [ 22 ] , and leave a confident, wrong prediction with no trace. Long-tail behaviour is rarely reported: among the catalogued systems, HiPath falls from 70.2% on head terms to 2.6% on tail terms [ 37 ] , MR-PLIP reaches 0.745 balanced accuracy on 30 EBRAINS brain-tumour subtypes [ 38 ] , CPathAgent scores 64.3% on three-class BRACS regions [ 16 ] , and paediatric tumours are assessed only by KEEP, on 816 WSIs from one hospital [ 39 ] . Calibration and abstention are absent: networks are poorly calibrated by default [ 40 ] , free text carries no calibrated confidence, selective prediction [ 41 ] goes unused, and none of the 51 systems reports calibrated confidence. Compute is reported sporadically and in incompatible units: a few papers give training budgets [ 42 , 37 ] , only LAMMI-Pathology reports a memory footprint [ 43 ] , and none compares runtimes across paradigms or estimates carbon cost [ 44 ] , although instruction-tuned assistants (7B–15B parameters) need roughly 10 4 10^{4} visual tokens and 40–80 GB of GPU memory per WSI at inference. Minimum controls. Each failure reported here was exposed by a cheap control. We propose that evaluations of pathology VLMs report, at a minimum: (i) for slide-conditioned VQA, text-only, mismatched-slide, option-shift and NOTA arms alongside the majority-answer baseline; (ii) answer-flip rates and consistency under paraphrase and reframing; (iii) stage-redacted and text-only arms for claims that text improves prognosis; (iv) within-encoder comparisons for claims about prompts; (v) CIs and seeds for every headline comparison, preferably with independent replication; and (vi) compute in six fields: GPU type and count, GPU-hours, peak training memory, peak inference memory, visual tokens per WSI, and end-to-end latency on a reference slide. For design, language at inference is best spent where it is irreplaceable (prognosis, interaction and reporting), and grounding should become a training objective: answers should cite tissue evidence, change when it changes, abstain when it is absent and stay invariant to paraphrase. None of this replaces clinical validation through multisite testing, subgroup reporting and prospective reader studies. Limitations. The probes cover released checkpoints on TCGA-derived data, two slide-level assistants, three language-aligned encoders and one pure-vision encoder; reasoning-tuned, agentic and MIL systems are not probed directly. In Probe 1, a null leakage contrast would also arise if the frozen text encoder were insensitive to stage statements, no arm combines vision with structured stage and grade, and the report text is a processed release. In Probe 2, the cross-encoder comparison is confounded, and zero-shot and supervised heads are compared under mean pooling only, without learned instance selection such as attention-based MIL. Probes 3 and 4 use multiple-choice items, so free-text generation may behave differently, and the navigation and few-shot grades rest on self-reported numbers. Limitations. Until grounding, paraphrase consistency and calibration are reported as routinely as accuracy, benchmark scores should be read as upper bounds on what a model reads from the slide, not as evidence that it does.
