Translated overlap sampling adds 5.70 5.70 mean BAcc points, whereas the rims add 0.48 0.48 points beyond the whole-cell pool. Quarter-stride sampling adds only 0.69 0.69 points while nearly quadrupling token count again. Thus, patch-phase coverage removes the main bottleneck, and contour-aware pooling provides a smaller refinement. The width-matched control assigns that refinement to distinct interior and rim supports rather than dimensionality. We isolate alignment through the mask study: a tight box changes BAcc by − 0.70 -0.70 points, whereas a four-pixel translation changes it by − 3.86 -3.86 points. Soft coverage therefore tolerates contour uncertainty but depends on registration, because translation moves both pools toward neighbouring tissue. Boundary imprecision preserves interior evidence, whereas translation redirects both supports to adjacent nuclei. This response matches our goal of assigning frozen features to the target cell. Our downstream tasks show the same organization. On CoNIC [ 25 ] and Lizard [ 24 ] , all six encoders improve under linear probing, and our readout remains ahead from one to 500 shots. At 500 shots, our margins remain 11.73 11.73 and 11.05 11.05 points. Retrieval gains are larger for macro averages, while the anchor analysis raises CONCH neutrophil AP from 0.481 0.481 with token mean and 0.503 0.503 with slide pooling to 0.756 0.756 with SRDP, concentrating similarity on matching lobulated cells. Our classification and retrieval results expose complementary effects of the same descriptor. A linear probe improves when cell-centred cues become separable, while direct retrieval improves only if those cues also reorganize neighbourhoods before fitting. The persistent few-shot margin confirms that descriptor geometry remains decisive as the label budget grows. Across datasets, gains follow the spatial evidence available to the readout. CoNIC and Lizard provide the clearest improvements, NuCLS [ 4 ] varies by encoder, and the four-label MoNuSAC [ 54 ] evaluation is nearly tied. Slide pooling encodes the image at once when possible; larger images are split into disjoint windows matching the encoder input and padded only at the borders. It averages native tokens intersecting each cell mask and combines their sums and counts across window boundaries. Because cells are not recentered in separate crops, the original patch alignment is preserved. SRDP uses Eq. 11 . Table 1 shows the clearest gains on CoNIC [ 25 ] and Lizard [ 24 ] . We observe this consistency across general-purpose and pathology-specific encoders, identifying the readout as the shared source of improvement. Translated sampling and fractional coverage prevent local nuclear cues from collapsing into a few mixed tokens, raising mean BAcc by 5.68 5.68 points over token mean across all settings. NuCLS [ 4 ] varies by encoder, whereas the four-label MoNuSAC [ 54 ] evaluation remains nearly tied across readouts. Translated overlap sampling adds 5.70 5.70 mean BAcc points, whereas the rims add 0.48 0.48 points beyond the whole-cell pool. Quarter-stride sampling adds only 0.69 0.69 points while nearly quadrupling token count again. Thus, patch-phase coverage removes the main bottleneck, and contour-aware pooling provides a smaller refinement. The width-matched control assigns that refinement to distinct interior and rim supports rather than dimensionality. We isolate alignment through the mask study: a tight box changes BAcc by − 0.70 -0.70 points, whereas a four-pixel translation changes it by − 3.86 -3.86 points. Soft coverage therefore tolerates contour uncertainty but depends on registration, because translation moves both pools toward neighbouring tissue. Boundary imprecision preserves interior evidence, whereas translation redirects both supports to adjacent nuclei. This response matches our goal of assigning frozen features to the target cell. Our downstream tasks show the same organization. On CoNIC [ 25 ] and Lizard [ 24 ] , all six encoders improve under linear probing, and our readout remains ahead from one to 500 shots. At 500 shots, our margins remain 11.73 11.73 and 11.05 11.05 points. Retrieval gains are larger for macro averages, while the anchor analysis raises CONCH neutrophil AP from 0.481 0.481 with token mean and 0.503 0.503 with slide pooling to 0.756 0.756 with SRDP, concentrating similarity on matching lobulated cells. Our classification and retrieval results expose complementary effects of the same descriptor. A linear probe improves when cell-centred cues become separable, while direct retrieval improves only if those cues also reorganize neighbourhoods before fitting. The persistent few-shot margin confirms that descriptor geometry remains decisive as the label budget grows. Across datasets, gains follow the spatial evidence available to the readout. CoNIC and Lizard provide the clearest improvements, NuCLS [ 4 ] varies by encoder, and the four-label MoNuSAC [ 54 ] evaluation is nearly tied. This pattern supports our premise: dense mask alignment exposes local evidence retained by a frozen backbone, with larger gains when that evidence matches the label semantics. 5 Conclusion We introduced SRDP to treat fine-grained cell representation as a geometry-aware readout problem over frozen pathology foundation models. Our dense grid resolves patch-phase ambiguity, soft coverage registers irregular masks, and inner rims retain contour morphology. Linear-probe, few-shot, retrieval, and ablation results consistently show that these components unlock cell-scale evidence mixed by native patch pooling. We thereby connect broad pathology pretraining to cell-centred analysis through a training-free readout that transfers across backbones and tasks. By separating representation learning from spatial assignment, SRDP lets one frozen encoder serve cell typing and direct retrieval under the same geometry. This establishes readout design as a practical axis for adapting PFMs to cellular analysis. Fixed pixel-space rims require scale normalization.
