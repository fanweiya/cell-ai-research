Averaging independent intensity measurements, called looks , reduces this noise. Under the Gamma model, the sum of L L looks satisfies S L ∣ X ∼ Gamma ( L , 1 / X ) , 𝔼 [ S L / L ∣ X ] = X , Var ( S L / L ∣ X ) = X 2 / L , S_{L}\mid X\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(L,1/X),\qquad\mathbb{E}[S_{L}/L\mid X]=X,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(S_{L}/L\mid X)=X^{2}/L, with shape L L and rate 1 / X 1/X for each pixel, where the clean intensity X X is called the reflectivity . We use three satellite radar acquisitions from Sentinel-1. Following Dalsasso et al. (2022) , we obtain two conditionally independent measurements with shape L obs = 1 / 2 L_{\mathrm{obs}}=1/2 from the squared real and imaginary components, each being a half-look . To reduce correlation in the speckle between neighboring pixels, we separate each image into four interlaced sublattices (Appendix H.4 ). N2N can supervise the predictions up to half a look, and the consistency loss supervises their continuation to larger L L . The network predicts a four-component inverse-Gamma mixture for the posterior of each pixel. For each jump size w w , we use the predicted expectation of e − w / X i e^{-w/X_{i}} to compute the jump rate. These predictions give the marginal posterior of each pixel. Because neighboring pixels of the clean image are dependent, they are also dependent in the joint posterior, and sampling each pixel from its marginal ignores this dependence. When we simulate the learned process, the prediction at each pixel depends on the whole current image, so the simulated process can reproduce it. We compare two samplers that start from the same observed half-look. The independent sampler draws a reflectivity at each pixel from its predicted marginal posterior, then draws an independent half-look conditional on these reflectivities. The joint sampler simulates the learned process from L obs L_{\mathrm{obs}} to 2 ​ L obs 2L_{\mathrm{obs}} , where its increment is a new half-look. We evaluate both samplers against the second half-look of held-out patches, without a clean reference. The two samplers give similar pixelwise coverage for the held-out view. To test spatial dependence, we sum the intensities over × 16 16\!\times\!16 blocks and compare predictive intervals for these sums with the held-out block sums. The variance of a sum includes the covariances between its pixels, which the independent sampler sets to zero. Noise2Noise regression supervises it up to the information level covered by the data, and the consistency relation determines its continuation toward clean images. In the CIFAR-10 and photon-counting microscopy experiments, training with the consistency loss improves sample quality when sampling continues above the information level of the training data. On radar data, the learned sampler captures posterior dependence between pixels, which independent per-pixel sampling misses. Our theory gives mild conditions under which this continuation is uniquely determined. Under the conditions of Section 3 , an exact minimizer of the training losses averaged over the true distribution of noisy measurements has the true predictions and therefore generates the true observation process. Limitations. The theory concerns exact minimizers and exact simulation, and our experiments depart from it in four ways. To reduce the cost per training step, the paths for the consistency loss are simulated with coarse steps. The networks enforce only some of the bounds of Assumption E.1 , the negative-binomial networks predict finitely many moments, and the radar model adds a regression on simulated states to stabilize its predictions. The resulting errors propagate along the continuation, and the stability estimate in Remark E.1 , which bounds this propagation, grows exponentially with the image size and the length of the continuation. In practice, continuing does not always help. At 180.1 180.1 binomial counts on CIFAR-10, the continued samples have a higher FID than samples stopped at the information level of the training data, and on radar data the learned process underestimates the spread of the intensities beyond 16 ​ L obs 16L_{\rm obs} . We have no criterion for how far a learned continuation should be run. Because we build the generative process from the measurement law, the method needs that law, including the information level of the data. Using the wrong law is costly: on the same Poisson data, Gaussian diffusion models give FIDs higher by 11.0 11.0 – 17.5 17.5 at the two higher photon counts. We also assume that the noise is independent across pixels given the clean image. Raw radar images do not satisfy this, since their speckle is correlated between neighboring pixels, and we split each image into four sublattices to reduce the correlation.
