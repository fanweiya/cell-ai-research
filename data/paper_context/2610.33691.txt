3.3 Language Model We adopt Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model as our MLLM decoder due to its capability of working with multi-modal data [ 33 ] . However, Vicuna [ 39 ] and LLama3 [ 34 ] have also been proven to be capable for this task [ 6 ] . Inspired from LLaVA-style training [ 20 ] , the training is performed in two stages. First, we train only the projection layer while keeping the MLLM backbone frozen for the purpose of semantic space alignment. Subsequently, we load the unfrozen MLLM using QLoRA [ 7 ] for parameter efficient visual instruction tuning and train both the projection layer and MLLM as an auto-regressive next token prediction task. The input to the model is a single unified prompt 𝒫 = [ 𝐯 ; 𝐤 ; 𝐩 ] \mathcal{P}=[\,\mathbf{v}\,;\,\mathbf{k}\,;\,\mathbf{p}\,] consisting of the visual tokens 𝐯 \mathbf{v} , knowledge tokens 𝐤 \mathbf{k} , and the instruction prompt 𝐩 \mathbf{p} . The confidence of the auxiliary classifier is explicitly stated in the instruction prompt 𝐩 \mathbf{p} . This prevents the MLLM from solely relying on the classifier output. The model is trained to predict the output y ^ \hat{y} . The unified prompt 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is masked to create the training labels, such that when predicting the y ^ \hat{y} , visual and knowledge tokens are attended. The training objective of cross entropy loss for the next token given 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is augmented with an adaptive weighting which prioritizes challenging examples (examples which score low in NLU metrics) by increasing their loss contribution while down-weighting trivial examples [ 2 ] . 3.5 Dataset and Training. We use the WSI-Bench dataset [ 17 ] , which was curated from TCGA [ 35 ] , as our primary dataset for the report generation pipeline. The report generation sub-task of this dataset includes 9749 training pairs and 208 test pairs, each consisting of a WSI and its corresponding pathology report. From that, a total of 9452 slides were used after removing slides which do not include microns per pixel value. For weakly supervised training of the auxiliary classifier, we derive histology labels from WSI reports using rule-based text processing and obtain primary-site metadata from TCGA. Extracted labels were cross-validated against labels available from the TCGA portal. The result is a supplementary dataset containing WSIs paired with primary site inputs and the histology classes as ground truth labels. 4 Experiments 4.1 Quantitative Results on WSI-Bench Dataset. We evaluate our method against existing WSI MLLMs under the report generation task of the WSI-Bench dataset [ 17 ] . As presented in Table 2 , PathKGen outperforms existing MLLMs including MI-Gen [ 3 ] , HistGen [ 9 ] , WSI-LLaVA [ 17 ] and WSI-Agents [ 26 ] , in multiple metrics. Specifically, our model achieves notable relative improvements across five standard NLU scores: BLEU-1 by 6.67%, BLEU-2 by 8.66%, BLEU-3 by 10.10%, BLEU-4 by 8.75%, ROUGE-L by 5.92%, and METEOR by 2.75%. Furthermore, as histopathology report generation is a domain characterized by complex and highly nuanced terminology, standard NLU metrics alone are inadequate to comprehensively assess report quality. To rigorously evaluate performance in this context, we employ an LLM-as-a-Judge metric: WSI-Precision introduced in [ 17 ] , which evaluates the accuracy of generated reports by verifying the model predictions against the ground truth claims. Our approach achieves a WSI-Precision of 0.480, outperforming the SOTA by 9.09%. These results demonstrate that PathKGen surpasses existing methods by significant margins. 4.2 Evaluation on Semantic Alignment We evaluated our model outputs using BERTScore, a deep learning based metric that measures the semantic alignment between the ground truth and the generated text [ 37 ] . PathKGen achieves a BERTScore F1 of 0.9081 with RoBERTa-large model [ 21 ] and 0.8836 with DistilBERT model [ 28 ] . Hallucinations or omissions of critical medical terms would be reflected by a significant decrease in BERTScore. Our consistently high BERTScore indicates strong semantic alignment with the ground truth and minimal factual distortion. In comparison, WSI-LLaVA achieves a BERTScore of 0.9068 for RoBERTa-large. These findings suggest that the MLLM is robust to uncertainty in the classifier outputs, with no observable degradation in report quality. 4.3 Qualitative Results Interpretation Fig. 5 shows selected outputs from the model for qualitative analysis. We observe that the key morphological descriptors of the carcinoma illustrated by the subgraph are reflected in the generated report. In Fig. 5 ( 𝐚 ) \mathbf{(a)} , the subgraph features and descriptors in the report show high overlap, meaning the MLLM faithfully adapts to the information provided by the knowledge path. As illustrated in Fig. 5 ( 𝐛 ) \mathbf{(b)} , the MLLM also learns novel connections absent from the knowledge graph (isolated green nodes), demonstrating the partial externalization of its internal knowledge. Since trivial characteristics are consistently supported by the knowledge path, the model parameters instead learn more complex relationships between visual descriptors and carcinomas. Furthermore, the MLLM learns negation rules, whereby the absence of specific morphological features serves as positive diagnostic evidence for certain histologies. This is confirmed by isolated red nodes in Fig. 5 ( 𝐜 ) \mathbf{(c)} , indicating that absent features remain disconnected from the corresponding histology node. Compared with the vision-only baseline, PathKGen improves BLEU-4 by 2.47%, METEOR by 2.52%, and WSI-Precision by 3.03% as shown in Table 3 . To determine whether these gains are merely from conditioning on the auxiliary classifier output or primary site metadata, we additionally evaluate variants where the knowledge pathway receives only the predicted histology or only the primary site. PathKGen consistently outperforms both baselines, indicating that the richer structured knowledge, including the subgraph relations and associated morphological features, provides complementary information beyond histology or site conditioning alone. Further, the counterfactual interventions in Table 5 show that oracle knowledge slightly improves performance, whereas random incorrect histology anchors substantially degrade WSI-Precision, confirming that the model meaningfully utilizes the supplied knowledge. To understand the amount of knowledge externalization handled by the knowledge path, we compare three model sizes with the same knowledge path architecture (Table 4 ). Lower-parameter models achieve performance comparable to 7B SOTA, supporting that visual discrimination logic and knowledge containment of the MLLM have become partially externalized to the knowledge path. Inevitably, scaling the model size gives a greater observable performance gain than from the knowledge path. However, partial externalization of domain knowledge not only improves the explainability but also increases the model performance at a given scale, by providing an external guidance to the MLLM. 4.1 Quantitative Results on WSI-Bench Dataset.
