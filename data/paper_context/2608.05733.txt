Methods Study design and participants This was a retrospective diagnostic accuracy study. Imaging and clinical data were collected privately for this study. Eligible patients had (i) histologically confirmed GBM, (ii) completion of standard-of-care therapy comprising maximal safe resection followed by concurrent chemoradiotherapy, (iii) development of a new contrast-enhancing lesion within the radiation field on follow-up MRI within six months of completing therapy, and (iv) availability of both anatomical and DCE-MRI sequences together with MGMT methylation status. Eighty-two patients met these criteria (49 men, 33 women; mean age 58.4 years, SD 8.9). The reference-standard diagnosis was established histopathologically from repeat surgical resection or stereotactic biopsy tissue where available (52 patients; TP defined as > > 25% malignant features on histology, n = 31 n=31 ; PsP as < < 25% malignant features, n = 21 n=21 ), and by modified Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (mRANO) criteria, based on serial imaging follow-up, for the remaining 30 patients without accessible tissue (TP n = 22 n=22 ; PsP n = 8 n=8 ). The final cohort comprised 53 patients with TP and 29 with PsP (Figure 1). MGMT promoter methylation status was determined for every patient and categorised as unmethylated ( n = 41 n=41 ), methylated ( n = 23 n=23 ), or indeterminate ( n = 18 n=18 ). Image acquisition All patients underwent MRI on a single Siemens Healthineers (Erlangen, Germany) 3-Tesla MAGNETOM Trio whole-body scanner with a 12-channel phased-array head coil. The neuroimaging protocol comprised T1-weighted magnetisation-prepared rapid acquisition of gradient echo (T1-MPRAGE: repetition time 1820 ms, echo time 3.88 ms, flip angle 15 ∘ , slice thickness 1 mm, bandwidth 190 Hz/pixel, field of view 256 × 256 256\times 256 mm 2 with 75% phase field of view, 192 slices, one average), axial T2-fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (T2-FLAIR: repetition time 10,030 ms, echo time 137 ms, flip angle 150 ∘ , slice thickness 3 mm, bandwidth 227 Hz/pixel, field of view 240 × 240 240\times 240 mm 2 with 75% phase field of view, 60 slices, one average), and a dynamic contrast-enhanced gradient-recalled-echo sequence (repetition time 3.23 ms, echo time 1.12 ms, flip angle 23 ∘ , slice thickness 3.5 mm, field of view 220 × 220 220\times 220 mm 2 , in-plane acquisition matrix 256 × 256 256\times 256 , 28 slices, one average, pixel bandwidth 210 Hz/pixel, acquisition time 3 min 33 s, temporal resolution 7 seconds per frame, 30 sequential dynamic measurements) acquired following a weight-based (0.07 mmol/kg) intravenous bolus of gadobenate dimeglumine (MultiHance, Bracco Imaging, Milan, Italy) delivered by power injector (Medrad, Indianola, PA, USA). Pharmacokinetic modelling and parsimonious parametric map construction Signal-intensity time courses were converted to contrast-concentration time courses using fixed longitudinal relaxation times (1400 ms tissue, 1600 ms blood) and a contrast-agent relaxivity of 4.3 L mmol -1 s -1 . A vascular input function was defined for each patient from a manually placed region of interest over the superior sagittal sinus. At every voxel, the concentration-time curve was fitted independently to five PK models spanning a hierarchy of physiological assumptions: the standard (non-linear) Tofts model, the extended Tofts model (ETM), the shutter-speed model (SSM, which relaxes the fast-water-exchange assumption), a two-compartment exchange model (2CXM), and a three-site two-exchange model (3S2X). Because K t ​ r ​ a ​ n ​ s K^{trans} and V e V_{e} are common to all five models, the parsimonious map for each was constructed by aggregating the voxel-wise best-fit values across all candidates. V p V_{p} , present only in ETM, 2CXM and 3S2X, and τ i \tau_{i} , present only in SSM and 3S2X, underwent an analogous but restricted voxel-wise selection among their respective eligible models. The extended Tofts and two-compartment exchange models track the tissue signal most closely during the rapid contrast-uptake phase, whereas the shutter-speed and three-site two-exchange models yield smoother but slightly attenuated peak-enhancement estimates. Model discrepancy is greatest in the early dynamic phase and converges at later time points, motivating the voxel-wise, best-fit (parsimonious) selection strategy used throughout this study rather than a single fixed model. Tumour segmentation and radiomic feature extraction Tumours were segmented into contrast-enhancing, non-enhancing/necrotic, and oedematous subregions using an automated deep-learning segmentation network (nnU-Net) trained and validated on the BraTS (2018–2021) public benchmark, achieving a test Dice score of 88.95%, with segmentations reviewed for anatomical plausibility. Post-contrast T1 and T2-FLAIR volumes were co-registered to the DCE-MRI parametric maps by rigid-body affine transformation. Total tumour volume was computed by summing enhancing and non-enhancing voxels across all co-registered slices. From the enhancing subregion of each parsimonious parametric map ( K t ​ r ​ a ​ n ​ s K^{trans} , V e V_{e} , V p V_{p} , τ i \tau_{i} ), first-order intensity statistics, three-dimensional shape descriptors, and five texture-matrix families (Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix, Gray Level Run Length Matrix, Gray Level Size Zone Matrix, Neighbouring Gray Tone Difference Matrix, Gray Level Dependence Matrix) were extracted using PyRadiomics, yielding an initial feature space of 1,073 variables. The true-progression case shows more extensive, spatially confluent regions of elevated K t ​ r ​ a ​ n ​ s K^{trans} and V e V_{e} , consistent with disorganised, hyperpermeable neovasculature, whereas the PsP case shows a more diffuse and rim-distributed permeability pattern consistent with treatment-related vascular injury. Feature selection Dimensionality reduction proceeded in two stages. First, each of the 1,073 candidate features was assessed independently for its ability to discriminate TP from PsP using the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test, on the grounds that radiomic feature distributions routinely violate normality assumptions; features with p < 0.05 p<0.05 were retained (reducing the set to 86 features). Second, Elastic Net regression – a regularised linear model combining L 1 L_{1} and L 2 L_{2} penalties, which tends to retain or discard correlated features jointly rather than arbitrarily selecting one from a correlated cluster – was applied, with the regularisation strength and L 1 L_{1} / L 2 L_{2} mixing parameter optimised by five-fold stratified cross-validated grid search, to obtain the final, compact feature subset used for model development. Class balancing, model development and validation Because PsP cases were less frequent than TP, reflecting the underlying clinical prevalence, Adaptive Synthetic Sampling (ADASYN) was applied within the training partition only, concentrating synthetic minority-class generation near the decision boundary; the held-out test partition was never resampled. Five candidate classifiers – Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), and XGBoost – were trained within an unstratified five-fold cross-validation framework, with hyperparameters selected to maximise mean validation AUC across folds. Following hyperparameter selection, classification thresholds were tuned on out-of-fold predictions across the full training set by maximising the F1 score over the interval [0.2, 0.6], avoiding any threshold optimisation on test data. The final models were trained on the complete resampled training set under the selected hyperparameter configuration and evaluated once on the untouched test partition. To assess the independent and combined contributions of parsimonious pharmacokinetic modelling and molecular data, four progressively enriched configurations were compared: (i) conventional extended-Tofts-derived radiomic features with MGMT status; (ii) T1-post-contrast structural radiomic features with MGMT status; (iii) parsimonious DCE-MRI radiomic features without MGMT status; and (iv) parsimonious DCE-MRI radiomic features with MGMT status. All experiments were repeated across multiple independent random seeds governing data splitting, resampling, fold assignment and model initialisation, with performance reported as mean (SD). Outcome measures and statistical analysis The primary outcome was test-set AUC; secondary outcomes were sensitivity, specificity, precision and F1 score, all derived from the confusion matrix at the tuned classification threshold. Feature-level contributions to the optimal classifier were quantified using Gini importance. All feature-selection and modelling procedures were implemented in Python using scikit-learn, imbalanced-learn and XGBoost; pharmacokinetic model fitting was implemented in MATLAB; radiomic extraction used PyRadiomics. Results Cohort characteristics The final cohort comprised 82 patients (53 TP, 29 PsP; 49 men, 33 women; mean age 58.4 years, SD 8.9). MGMT promoter status was unmethylated in 41 patients (TP=27, PsP=14), methylated in 23 (TP=12, PsP=11), and indeterminate in 18 (TP=14, PsP=4). Contribution of parsimonious pharmacokinetic modelling Across all five candidate PK models, the voxel-wise parsimonious selection consistently achieved lower mean AIC and higher mean R 2 R^{2} than any single fixed model, both within the enhancing and non-enhancing tumour subregions, confirming that no single kinetic model was uniformly optimal across the heterogeneous vascular microenvironment of GBM. Using the conventional, single-model extended-Tofts approach, the best-performing classifier (Random Forest) achieved a test AUC of only 0.68 (SD 0.13), with wide instability in specificity across repeated runs (0.38, SD 0.12), a pattern consistent with systematic model misspecification degrading downstream radiomic feature quality. Replacing the single-model approach with parsimonious, voxel-wise best-fit parametric maps – without any change to the molecular covariates – raised Random Forest test AUC to 0.84 (SD 0.09) and sensitivity to 0.93 (SD 0.09), a sixteen-percentage-point absolute improvement in discrimination attributable entirely to more physiologically faithful feature extraction. Contribution of MGMT methylation status Incorporating MGMT promoter methylation status alongside the parsimonious DCE-MRI radiomic feature set produced the best-performing model in the entire study. The Random Forest classifier achieved a test AUC of 0.89 (SD 0.07), sensitivity 0.93 (SD 0.09), specificity 0.76 (SD 0.19), precision 0.88 (SD 0.10), and F1 score 0.90 (SD 0.07). The improvement in specificity over the MGMT-free parsimonious configuration – from 0.57 (SD 0.31) to 0.76 (SD 0.19) – was the single largest incremental gain observed across all four configurations, indicating that molecular status substantially reduced false-positive calls of TP, the misclassification with the greatest potential for unwarranted treatment escalation. Discussion The sixteen-percentage-point absolute improvement in Area Under the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic Curve achieved through voxel-wise adaptive pharmacokinetic model selection—from 0.68 to 0.84—establishes, prior to the integration of molecular covariates, that model choice is a first-order determinant of downstream radiomic feature quality and classification accuracy in heterogeneous tumours. This finding challenges the widespread convention of imposing a single extended-Tofts kinetic model across entire tumour volumes and suggests that such uniform parameterisation may systematically degrade the quantitative biomarkers on which numerous prior radiomics studies have relied. The mechanism is consistent with established biological understanding: GBM vasculature is abnormally tortuous and variably permeable both within and between tumours, yet conventional single-model approaches force uniform parameterisation across this heterogeneity. Voxel-wise AIC-guided selection, by contrast, permits model adaptation to local tissue properties and produces physiologically faithful estimates of vascular permeability, extravascular volume, plasma volume, and intracellular water exchange time. The subsequent integration of MGMT promoter methylation status yielded a further, clinically important improvement concentrated specifically in specificity. This pattern is mechanistically plausible: MGMT-methylated tumours are both more responsive to alkylating chemotherapy and more likely to manifest PsP rather than TP following treatment, so methylation status functions as a genuinely independent prior on the outcome rather than a proxy for information already captured by the imaging features. Because MGMT testing is already standard practice in GBM management, incorporating it into an imaging-based classifier requires no additional invasive procedure, laboratory assay, or deviation from existing clinical workflows. The resulting model (Area Under the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic Curve 0.89, sensitivity 0.93, specificity 0.76, F1 score 0.90) compares favourably with previously published approaches, which have generally relied on single-model DCE-MRI parameters or T1-post-contrast structural radiomics and have reported accuracies in the 70–85% range. [ 8 , 9 ] The feature-importance analysis reveals mechanistically grounded insight: the dominance of K t ​ r ​ a ​ n ​ s K^{trans} shape descriptors—particularly least-axis length (importance 0.254) and flatness (0.166)—over mean K t ​ r ​ a ​ n ​ s K^{trans} values indicates that spatial disorganisation of vascular permeability, rather than mere elevation, constitutes the principal imaging correlate distinguishing active tumour angiogenesis from radiation-induced vascular injury. This finding aligns with established histopathological evidence that TP manifests as chaotic, tortuous microvascular proliferation, whereas PsP presents with comparatively uniform vascular injury lacking organised neovascularisation. Radiomic texture and shape descriptors, by capturing spatial disorganisation, thus achieve superior fidelity to underlying pathological processes relative to summary statistics. Limitations This single-centre retrospective study establishes proof-of-concept that voxel-wise adaptive pharmacokinetic model selection substantially outperforms conventional single-model approaches in the discrimination of TP from PsP. The 21-percentage-point absolute improvement in Area Under the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic Curve over the extended-Tofts baseline (0.89 versus 0.68) is sufficiently large to justify the substantial investment required for prospective validation in independent multicentre cohorts acquired across different scanner platforms and acquisition protocols. Radiomic features are known to be sensitive to acquisition and reconstruction parameters; such external validation will be essential to characterise scanner robustness and assess true generalisation. Reference-standard labels were derived from a mixture of histopathology (52 of 82 patients) and mRANO imaging criteria (remainder), though histopathological confirmation was prioritised wherever tissue was available. The analysis employed a single post-treatment imaging timepoint rather than longitudinal radiomic trajectory; however, all patients had confirmatory follow-up imaging at median 8 weeks establishing stable or progressive disease status. Fixed T1 relaxation values (1850 ms) were used for signal-to-concentration conversion rather than voxel-wise T1 mapping - a widely accepted simplification in clinical DCE-MRI but one that may introduce parameter bias in tissue with atypical baseline T1. Complementary sequences such as dynamic susceptibility contrast perfusion and diffusion tensor imaging were unavailable and represent avenues for future multiparametric integration. Conclusion This work establishes two interdependent principles. First, voxel-wise pharmacokinetic model selection substantially outperforms single-model convention in heterogeneous tumours, with model choice alone accounting for 76 per cent of the total improvement in Area Under the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic Curve, and warranting adoption as a methodological baseline in future DCE-MRI radiomics research. Second, the integration of routinely available molecular data i.e MGMT status with adaptive imaging meaningfully improves clinical classification beyond imaging features alone. The resulting classifier (Area Under the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic Curve 0.89, sensitivity 0.93, specificity 0.76, F1 score 0.90) substantially exceeds performance of conventional single-model (0.68) and structural radiomics baselines (0.72). Pending rigorous external validation and prospective evaluation, this non-invasive, workflow-compatible approach could support earlier, more confident post-treatment decision-making in GBM whilst reducing both unnecessary treatment escalation and delayed intervention.
