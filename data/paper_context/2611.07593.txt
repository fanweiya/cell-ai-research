2 Method We introduce TACT-KV, a calibration-free method for compressing the visual portion of the KV cache held by a decoder-only vision–language model during generation. For a backbone with L L transformer layers and n h n_{h} attention heads per layer, a single sequence at layer ℓ \ell produces a key tensor K ( ℓ ) K^{(\ell)} and a value tensor V ( ℓ ) V^{(\ell)} with per-head feature dimension d d . In a vision–language model, tokens originate from two sources, a vision encoder and a text tokenizer. When the input is a volumetric medical scan or a video, visual tokens dominate the count and inherit a known three-dimensional grid S × H × W S\times H\times W from the vision encoder, where S S is the slice or temporal axis token count and H , W H,W are the spatial pooling dimensions. We exploit this structural prior by allocating the per-layer bit budget for the visual KV cache in a frequency-domain representation of this grid, retaining the information required by downstream tasks at low bit precision. The pipeline introduces no learned parameters and runs once per layer per sequence in a single forward pass. The value tensors V v , V t V_{v},V_{t} are defined analogously. The grid parameters ( S , H , W ) (S,H,W) are emitted by the vision encoder for each input and are read directly by our pipeline. We do not estimate any per-sample structural quantity. Uniform allocation also degrades both models, showing that the transform and adaptive allocation each contribute to prediction fidelity. 4 Discussion and Conclusion We presented TACT-KV, a transform-coding pipeline for compressing the visual KV cache of vision–language models on volumetric token grids. TACT-KV reshapes the cache into a ( S , H , W ) (S,H,W) tensor, applies a separable three-axis DCT-II for spatial decorrelation, allocates a per-layer bit budget by reverse water-filling, and quantizes each frequency bin with a scalar codebook. The pipeline is calibration-free and adds no learned parameters. The volumetric prior we exploit is absent from the text and natural-image settings that prior KV compressors target, and we prove that the distortion gain of reverse water-filling over uniform allocation has a closed form, 1 / γ 1/\gamma , in the spectral flatness of the transformed cache. Across six medical VLMs on two 3D CT benchmarks, TACT-KV achieves the lowest 1-bit flip rate on eleven of twelve model and dataset pairs in our main comparison. The method also transfers to four general-purpose VLMs on Video-MME, and its gain in maximum decoding batch size at equal HBM increases with model scale, reaching 8 × 8\times on Hulu-Med 32B. The margin between TACT-KV and 1D quantizers on these benchmarks also measures how much volumetric structure scalar codebooks alone cannot recover, and that margin widens as the input becomes more spatially coherent. Integrating TACT-KV with attention-aware eviction on the text portion of the cache is a natural next step and orthogonal to the bit-allocation savings reported here. Limitation. The closed-form prediction of Theorem 1 holds in the high-rate regime in which every frequency bin receives non-trivial bit allocation, and the lowest-rate operating points on the smaller Hulu-Med variants fall outside this regime (Section 3.3 ). Our pipeline also compresses the visual portion of the cache only, leaving text tokens at bf16, so the savings reported here are tied to inputs in which visual tokens dominate. The volumetric prior is validated on chest CT and head CT only, and whether it transfers to other 3D modalities such as MRI, PET, and 3D ultrasound is not measured. Although the same pipeline transfers to Video-MME without modification, the temporal axis differs in origin from the slice axis, and the gains we observe there are empirical rather than predicted by the volumetric assumption that motivates the design. Broader Impact. Lower memory requirements can broaden access to volumetric medical VLMs at clinical sites with limited GPU capacity Lan et al. (2026) . Prediction changes introduced by compression could lead to missed findings or unnecessary follow-up in clinical use. Evaluation on local data should therefore examine per-label prediction changes and diagnostic accuracy, especially for rare findings that aggregate metrics may obscure.
