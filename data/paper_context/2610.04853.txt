Full settings are provided in Appendix A.1 . 4.2 Main Result: Performance across Backbones and Aggregators Table 1 summarizes the performance of DI-MIL across three frozen backbones and two aggregators while holding downstream architectures constant within each paired comparison. Replacing conventional single-embedding bags with DI-MIL yields performance improvements in 67 out of 72 evaluation settings. Aggregate improvements are consistently positive across datasets, ranging from +1.10 to +3.21 percentage points. Fold-paired analyses in Appendix A.6 further characterize the variability of these gains across matched folds. The mean performance gains are most pronounced in liquid-based and Respiratory cohorts, Serous Fluid and Respiratory , where rare diagnostic cells are diluted by abundant background. Decoupling instance granularity enables slide attention to focus on evidence-enriched sub-tile clusters. Across feature extractors, mean performance gains are highest under Smart-CCS (+3.64 points), followed by UniCAS (+1.59 points) and DINOv3 (+0.51 points). This trend indicates that DI-MIL leverages fine-grained representations more effectively when dense tokens capture domain-specific cytomorphological details. We further analyze this backbone-dependent behavior through quantitative and qualitative dense-token analyses in Appendix A.2 . First, we vary the number of decomposed instances k k to assess granularity sensitivity. Second, with k k fixed, we compare feature-based clustering against random and spatial grouping to isolate the effect of the grouping criterion. Lastly, we compare post-encoding decomposition with direct smaller tiling. The full ablation suite is evaluated on both Oral2026 and Respiratory . 4.4.1 Number of decomposed instances We first vary k ∈ { 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 } k\in\{1,2,3,4,5\} on Oral2026 and Respiratory using Smart-CCS with ACMIL while holding all other settings fixed. As shown in Table 3 , k = 4 k=4 provides the most balanced performance across the two datasets: it achieves the highest AUC and macro-F1 on Oral2026 and the highest ACC and macro-F1 on Respiratory , while k = 5 k=5 yields the highest Oral2026 ACC and Respiratory AUC. The non-monotonic trend indicates that simply increasing the number of instances is not sufficient. We therefore use k = 4 k=4 as a practical fixed default rather than claiming a universal optimum. Importantly, the k = 1 k=1 baseline serves as a control that isolates the feature representation change from the multi-instance decomposition effect by collapsing dense tokens into a single instance. As shown in Appendix A.4 , k = 4 k=4 consistently outperforms both the original [CLS] baseline and this k = 1 k=1 control across all datasets. Table 5 compares DI-MIL with progressively finer tiling on Oral2026 and Respiratory . Thus, its gains cannot be attributed solely to a larger number of MIL instances. Replacing conventional single-embedding bags with DI-MIL yields performance improvements in 67 out of 72 evaluation settings. Aggregate improvements are consistently positive across datasets, ranging from +1.10 to +3.21 percentage points. Fold-paired analyses in Appendix A.6 further characterize the variability of these gains across matched folds. The mean performance gains are most pronounced in liquid-based and Respiratory cohorts, Serous Fluid and Respiratory , where rare diagnostic cells are diluted by abundant background. Decoupling instance granularity enables slide attention to focus on evidence-enriched sub-tile clusters. Across feature extractors, mean performance gains are highest under Smart-CCS (+3.64 points), followed by UniCAS (+1.59 points) and DINOv3 (+0.51 points). Right: Relative tile count versus MIL feature-instance count. 5 Discussion and Limitations Conventional WSI MIL operationally treats each encoded tile as an indivisible unit of slide-level aggregation. This formulation is particularly restrictive in cytopathology, where a single microscopic field may contain sparse diagnostic cells intermixed with abundant benign and non-diagnostic content. DI-MIL addresses this fundamental domain mismatch by decoupling visual encoding context from slide aggregation granularity, enabling attention-based aggregators such as ABMIL and ACMIL to assign independent importance weights to localized, discriminative cellular patterns. Qualitative inspection of high-attention instances is consistent with this intended granularity effect; examples are provided in Appendix A.5 . On the other hand, DI-MIL has two main limitations. First, its fixed k k and non-differentiable clustering do not adapt to variations in tile cellularity or spatial heterogeneity. Second, it treats decomposed instances as an order-agnostic set and therefore does not explicitly model spatial relationships among intra-tile clusters. Potential extensions include adaptive or differentiable decomposition, spatially aware grouping, and task-guided interactions among decomposed instances. 6 Conclusion We revisited the conventional paradigm in weakly supervised cytopathology that couples tile-level encoding context to MIL aggregation granularity. By decomposing dense tokens from foundation models within each tile into independently weighted instances post-encoding, DI-MIL mitigates the intra-tile evidence dilution that can arise when heterogeneous cytopathology tiles are compressed into single global representations. Extensive evaluations across diverse cytopathology cohorts, foundation backbones, and aggregators demonstrate that DI-MIL consistently outperforms both single-embedding baselines and direct fine-grid tiling. These results demonstrate that the tile-level instance is not always the most effective choice for cytopathology MIL and establish instance construction as a meaningful direction for future study.
