Pathology foundation models. Large-scale self-supervised pretraining has produced histology FMs such as UNI [ 2 ] , UNI2 [ 19 ] , CONCH [ 18 ] , and Virchow2 [ 35 ] . Their tile embeddings transfer across tasks and organs with little or no fine-tuning. Recent work has further leveraged pretrained pathology representations for weakly supervised segmentation through structural distillation [ 15 ] , while disentangled stain and morphological representations have been explored for robust histopathology analysis under limited annotations [ 21 ] . Diffusion models have been used to extend this capability to generation, learning to render a realistic tile from a foundation model embedding [ 10 , 32 ] . PixCell [ 31 ] scales up the diffusion training to cover multiple cancer types, and learns to decode UNI2-h embeddings [ 19 ] into H&E tissue images. Gestalt learns the missing map from spatial gene expression into this ‘renderable’ embedding space, allowing the pretrained image representation and decoder to be reused. 3 Method Figure 1 : Gestalt . A synthetic field must represent variation between fields, spatial correlation among nearby cells, and realistic expression sparsity. We model these properties using a shared field state, spatially correlated cell residuals, and a detected-gene-count model. The generator works in two stages. After reducing cells of one type to 100 100 principal components, it represents each real tile by the mean of its cells. For a synthetic field, it draws a shared tile state s s from a Gaussian mixture fitted to these means, then draws each cell state z i z_{i} around it: s ∼ ∑ m = 1 M π m ​ 𝒩 ​ ( μ m , Σ m ) , z i = s + ϵ i , \displaystyle s\sim\sum_{m=1}^{M}\pi_{m}\mathcal{N}(\mu_{m},\Sigma_{m}),\quad z_{i}=s+\epsilon_{i}, (9) Cov ⁡ ( ϵ i , ϵ j ) = exp ⁡ ( − ∥ r i − r j ∥ 2 ρ ) ​ Σ ϵ . \displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert r_{i}-r_{j}\rVert_{2}}{\rho}\right)\Sigma_{\epsilon}. (10) Here r i r_{i} is cell i i ’s position, Σ ϵ \Sigma_{\epsilon} is the residual covariance in PCA space, and ρ \rho is a spatial range fitted from the data. The number of mixture components M M is chosen over 1 1 – 20 20 by the Bayesian information criterion (BIC). This is a random-effects model with an exponential covariance function [ 5 ] . The number of cells is drawn from the observed type-specific distribution, and their positions follow a hard-core point process [ 6 ] , whose minimum separation adapts to that count. After mapping z i z_{i} back to gene space, the number of detected genes is regressed on the cell state to preserve realistic sparsity. The mixture components produce the different appearances. BIC selects 16 16 components for C1 melanoma and 10 10 for C1 keratinocytes, against 3 3 for C2 keratinocytes and 2 2 for C2 T cells. A single state reproduces a type’s mean expression but not the variation between its fields. Limitations. Synthetic fields are less variable than real tissue. On C1 melanoma, the mean pairwise cosine among 40 40 predicted field embeddings is 0.82 0.82 , compared with 0.66 0.66 among 40 40 real tiles of the same type. Replacing sampled states with those measured from real tiles leaves this value unchanged, so the missing variation comes from structure within each field, which the Gaussian model does not capture. We therefore perform the main ablations under the cross-condition HEST lung setting of Sec. 4.3 , on the healthy holdouts NCBI884 and NCBI875 ( Table S5 ). Does expression conditioning matter? Keeping the decoder fixed, we replace the predicted embedding with a constant, a seeded permutation of the predictions, or the average real FM embedding of the ten training tiles with the most similar expression. On both healthy holdouts, Gestalt outperforms the constant and permutation controls on both paired metrics, and the retrieval control on all three image metrics. Permutation preserves the generated image set and therefore leaves FID unchanged, while degrading SSIM and FeatDist. This shows that the results depend on tile-specific expression rather than PixCell alone or retrieval of similar training examples ( Appendix S4 ). Which mapper components matter? On the same holdouts, we ablate the positional embedding, graph-attention blocks, spatial pathway, and per-cell encoder. Removing either positional encoding or graph attention worsens FID and FeatDist on both patients, while replacing the full spatial pathway with mean pooling degrades FID further. Overall, Gestalt leads four of six comparisons and remains close to the best baseline on the other two, while achieving the best FeatDist on both patients. Full results are in Appendix S4 . Separating molecular prediction from rendering allows mapper and decoder errors to be analyzed separately, while statistically generated expression enables paired synthetic expression-image generation. Our generalization experiments show that the formulation remains informative across multiple shifts, though performance degrades under harder cohort changes. Expression-conditioned histology synthesis remains an open problem, but these results establish foundation-model embedding prediction as a viable path toward further advances in molecular encoding, spatial modeling, and rendering.
