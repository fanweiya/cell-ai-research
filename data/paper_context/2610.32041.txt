2 Methods 2.1 Motivation Data in Fourier space displays a wide variety of structures which are ill-captured by traditional ViT tokenization. As a motivating and important example, the variance of many natural signals is heavily concentrated in low spatial frequencies (Figure 1 ). With the standard discrete Fourier transform (DFT) convention that places the origin at the center of the Fourier image, most of the data variation is concentrated around the center. Additionally, the variance of many natural signals scales as a power law in frequency, such that low-frequency variances are orders of magnitude greater than high-frequency variances ( Field, 1987 ; Van der Schaaf and van Hateren, 1996 ) . Standard ViT tokenization partitions an image into square patches on a uniform Cartesian grid. In Fourier space, this is poorly justified, since each square patch spans a mix of spatial frequencies rather than aligning with concentric frequency bands from the spectrum. Moreover, for many data modalities, this effectively undersamples the information-dense low-frequency components of a Fourier-space signal and oversamples the high-frequency periphery. NUViT addresses both issues by tokenizing on a non-uniform polar grid that allocates more samples to the low-frequency center (Figure 2 ). 2.2 NUViT 2.2.1 Tokenization To address the sampling mismatch described above, we represent the Fourier data on a polar grid with N r N_{r} radial shells and N ϕ N_{\phi} angular samples per shell, yielding coordinates { ( r i , ϕ j ) : r i = i / N r , ϕ j = π j / N ϕ } \{(r_{i},\phi_{j}):r_{i}=i/N_{r},\,\phi_{j}=\pi j/N_{\phi}\} for i = 1 , … , N r i=1,\ldots,N_{r} and j = 1 , … , N ϕ j=1,\ldots,N_{\phi} . We use the NUFFT to evaluate the Fourier transform at these non-uniform coordinates, producing a N r × N ϕ N_{r}\times N_{\phi} grid in polar space. This grid is treated as a regular 2D image and partitioned into square patches of size P × P P\times P using the standard ViT patching procedure. Inductive bias Compared to a standard ViT, NUViT introduces additional inductive biases. An important one for machine learning is the assumption that the low spatial frequencies of the input data carry information content that is better sampled with a polar grid. This assumption is appropriate whenever the data obeys the Fourier power law, as is the case for natural images, MRI images, cryo-EM images, and many other modalities. The use of a polar grid also introduces a bias towards isotropy, which is common in many types of Fourier data, including Fourier power law data. The polar grid also respects the spectral autoregression structure of Fourier data: if we group pixels into Fourier shells [ g 1 , g 2 , … ] [g_{1},g_{2},\ldots] by distance from the origin, the subsequences [ g 1 ] , [ g 1 , g 2 ] , … [g_{1}],[g_{1},g_{2}],\ldots encode the image at monotonically increasing resolution. Each ring of patches on the polar grid corresponds naturally to a frequency shell, so the model can attend to coarse-to-fine information in a structured way. 3 Results We evaluate NUViT in five settings. To validate our design choices against simpler baselines, we test (1) Fourier-domain natural image classification and (2) 2D crystal lattice identification. To demonstrate NUViT’s utility for different imaging modalities, we test (3) supervised MRI classification, (4) supervised cryo-EM image classification, and (5) heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction, which tests whether NUViT can serve as an effective encoder in a downstream generative model. 3.1 Natural Image Classification Setup We first use Fourier-domain classification of natural images as a simple setting to evaluate NUViT and ablate its design choices. Input RGB images are converted to grayscale and Fourier transformed. We evaluate on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 ( Krizhevsky et al., 2009 ) using top-1 accuracy. We measure robustness by performing test-time inference on CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C ( Hendrycks and Dietterich, 2019 ) . As Fourier-space baselines, we use a FIT ( Buchholz and Jug, 2022 ) -derived model and a ViT-S ( Dosovitskiy et al., 2020 ) with a Hartley transform representation. Results NUViT achieves the best overall performance on both datasets (Table 2 ). FIT performs poorly, likely due to its one-token-per-pixel design, which produces extremely long sequences even for modestly sized images, leading to slow convergence. To test whether NUViT generalizes to the opposite case, we benchmark on hyperuniform patterns, whose power spectra rise with frequency because they suppress fluctuations at large spatial scales ( Torquato and Stillinger, 2003 ) — as observed in cosmological superhomogeneity ( Gabrielli et al., 2002 ) and crystal lattices. While perfect crystals do not follow a power law, imperfect crystals with random perturbations approximately follow S ⁡ ( k ) ∝ k α S(k)\propto k^{\alpha} with α = 2 \alpha=2 ( Gabrielli, 2004 ; Kim and Torquato, 2018 ; Klatt et al., 2020 ) . We generate a dataset of such imperfect crystals with four types of symmetry, randomly displacing each lattice point (Figure S1 ). As Fourier-space baselines, we use an MLP ( Zhong et al., 2020 ) and a ViT-S ( Dosovitskiy et al., 2020 ) , each with real and Hartley transform representations. Results In Table 2 , we find that NUViT substantially outperforms alternative encoders on 2D crystal lattice classification. Because every encoder reaches 100% train accuracy, this gap reflects generalization rather than capacity. Each lattice class can be uniquely identified by its Bragg peaks, which are a set of peaks in the power spectrum of each crystal. However, identification is complicated by random perturbations, which dampen the peaks by the Debye-Waller factor e − σ 2 ​ k 2 e^{-\sigma^{2}k^{2}} , where σ \sigma is the noise and k k is the frequency ( Ashcroft and Mermin, 1976 ) . Therefore, Bragg peaks at higher frequency are more dampened than those at lower frequency. The higher test accuracy of NUViT compared to baseline methods provides evidence that our polar grid tokenization better matches the geometry of the spectrum than standard ViTs, capturing subtle peak information. 3.3 MRI Classification Setup To evaluate NUViT on MRI data, we use the OASIS Alzheimer’s Detection Multi-Class dataset ( Mohanty, 2025 ) , which contains 2D brain MRI images labeled by Alzheimer’s disease severity. To increase realism, we add MRI-specific corruptions and noise at a realistic signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), adapted from fastMRI ( Zbontar et al., 2018 ) . The task is challenging because it requires fine-grained distinctions between adjacent severity levels. Because these labels are ordinal, each encoder is paired with a CORAL head and trained with a class-weighted CORAL loss ( Cao et al., 2020 ) . We evaluate models with quadratic weighted kappa (QWK), which measures agreement with the ground truth relative to chance while penalizing errors in proportion to the squared distance between predicted and true labels. Because MRI data is acquired directly in the frequency domain, these results suggest that NUViT could operate on raw MRI data without first reconstructing an image, which we leave to future work. 3.4 Cryo-EM Image Classification Setup We next evaluate NUViT on cryo-EM heterogeneity classification, where the goal is to classify noisy 2D projection images of dynamic proteins by their underlying 3D state. Cryo-EM images are characterized by extremely low signal-to-noise ratios and are commonly represented in the Fourier domain, making this a natural application for NUViT. We compare against two baselines corresponding to encoders used in cryo-EM reconstruction: the cryoDRGN ( Zhong et al., 2020 ; Zhong et al., 2021a ) MLP encoder and a ViT-S ( Dosovitskiy et al., 2020 ) encoder, each tested with both real-space and Hartley transform representations. Hartley transform values are the default for many cryo-EM models ( Zhong et al., 2020 ; Zhong et al., 2021a ; Zhong et al., 2021b ; Levy et al., 2022 ; Gu et al., 2026 ) . The synthetic datasets are from CryoBench ( Jeon et al., 2024 ) : IgG-1D and IgG-RL contain 100 conformational states of the IgG antibody, while Ribosembly and Tomotwin-100 contain 16 and 100 distinct biomolecular species, respectively. Setup We next evaluate whether NUViT can serve as an effective encoder in a downstream generative model for cryo-EM reconstruction. Specifically, we replace the MLP encoder in the cryoDRGN ( Zhong et al., 2020 ; Zhong et al., 2021a ) model with either a ViT (Hartley) encoder or a NUViT encoder, keeping the neural field decoder and all other components fixed. Beyond standard reconstruction quality, we test whether NUViT can enable amortized inference in cryo-EM. Amortization We next evaluate each encoder on held-out test images. These images are passed through the encoder to generate latent embeddings (Figure 4 ), which are then passed through the decoder to reconstruct volumes that are compared with ground truth maps via FSC AUC \mathrm{FSC}_{\mathrm{AUC}} (Table 7 ). The MLP encoder shows the largest train-test gap, with FSC AUC \mathrm{FSC}_{\mathrm{AUC}} dropping from 0.346 to 0.139 on Tomotwin-100 . ViT encoders improve amortization substantially, and NUViT improves further, achieving the best test performance on all three datasets. The latent space visualizations in Figure 4 are consistent with these findings: NUViT’s latent space is better organized on both train and test images, whereas the MLP encoder fails to disentangle different conformations or compositions on test images. The improved amortization is plausibly because NUViT’s polar grid tokenization concentrates capacity on the low-frequency components that carry the most structural information, making the encoder less susceptible to overfitting to the high-frequency noise that dominates individual training images. Motivated by the Fourier power law, NUViT introduces the idea of sampling and tokenizing on a non-uniform polar grid, providing stronger inductive biases such as better sampling of important low-frequency regions of Fourier space that are undersampled by a traditional ViT. Extensive experiments show that NUViT is a more expressive, generalizable, and robust encoder for Fourier data. Despite these encouraging results, NUViT has the limitation that it is more computationally expensive than ViTs. This may be addressed by using more performant NUFFT libraries, such as the one proposed in ( Lingsch et al., 2023 ) , as well as reducing the oversampling rate in the NUFFT ( Beatty et al., 2005 ) . This work could have long-term societal benefits through improved image reconstruction and analysis techniques for applications like cryo-EM and MRI, contributing to deeper insight into biological and physical systems. Dashes indicate that there is no prior evaluation of that baseline on the given dataset. Latent space quality To assess the latent spaces of Figure 4 quantitatively, we use two sets of metrics. To evaluate continuous heterogeneity with a known groundtruth coordinate structure, such as IgG-1D, we evaluate trustworthiness ( Venna and Kaski, 2001 ) and continuity ( Venna and Kaski, 2006 ) , two neighborhood-preservation metrics designed to assess continuous latent spaces such as IgG-1D’s ring latent space. Both metrics take values in [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] with higher values being better. We compute these metrics between a latent space and an idealized ring structure. Table S5: Embedding quality.
