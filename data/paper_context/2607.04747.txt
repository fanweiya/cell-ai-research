3 Methodology 3.1 An Overview of MergeSurv MergeSurv is a CL framework for survival analysis using WSIs. Given a stream of N N datasets 𝒟 = { D t } t = 1 N \mathcal{D}=\{D_{t}\}_{t=1}^{N} corresponding to N N tasks, MergeSurv trains each survival task D t D_{t} using the WSI model f 𝒮 ​ ( ⋅ , θ 𝒮 ) f_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot;\theta_{\mathcal{S}}) with a Cox regression head H ⁡ ( ⋅ , θ H ) H(\cdot;\theta_{H}) to obtain the task-specific optimal parameters θ 𝒮 , t ∗ \theta^{*}_{\mathcal{S},t} and θ H , t ∗ \theta^{*}_{H,t} . Subsequently, all parameters { θ 𝒮 , t ∗ } t = 1 N \{\theta^{*}_{\mathcal{S},t}\}_{t=1}^{N} are sequentially merged into unified parameters, resulting in the merged parameters Θ ~ 𝒮 , 1 : N \tilde{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S},1:N} . Finally, only f 𝒮 ( ⋅ ; Θ ~ 𝒮 , 1 : N ) f_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot;\tilde{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S},1:N}) and two risk prediction strategies to estimate survival risk scores are used for inference on all N N tasks learned so far, as illustrated in Fig. 1 . Two inference strategies are introduced: One-for-All (OFA) with a single Cox head, and Voting-Expert Aggregation (VEA) with multiple task-specific heads. 3.2 Ad Hoc Fine-Tuning Fine-tuning Process First, whenever a new WSI survival task t t is defined, the model f 𝒮 f_{\mathcal{S}} is fine-tuned from the prior parameters θ prior \theta_{\text{prior}} using the training samples of D t D_{t} to optimize the objective function ℒ \mathcal{L} over E E epochs. θ prior \theta_{\text{prior}} is initialized from the pretrained weights of the TITAN foundation model [ 6 ] . As TITAN is a pathology-specific foundation model trained on large-scale unlabeled pathology images in a self-supervised manner, it effectively captures tissue structures in the embedding space. The predicted risk score for the i i -th sample is denoted by r ^ i \hat{r}_{i} . The Cox loss learns the relative risk ranking among patients, assigning higher risk scores to patients with earlier events. 3.3 Model Merging Operation We follow OPCM [ 18 ] for the model merging operations of the WSI model parameters f 𝒮 f_{\mathcal{S}} . Unlike existing rehearsal-based continual learning approaches, MergeSurv avoids storing previous WSIs by independently fine-tuning task-specific models and sequentially merging their parameters into a unified model. We further investigated two inference strategies, namely One-for-All (OFA) and Voting-Expert Aggregation (VEA), for continual survival risk prediction across multiple cancer cohorts. Experimental results on four TCGA datasets demonstrated that MergeSurv effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while achieving competitive survival prediction performance compared with regularization-based and replay-based continual learning methods. In particular, MergeSurv-VEA achieved strong risk stratification performance and consistently low forgetting across sequential survival tasks. Overall, the results suggest that model merging is a promising direction for scalable and privacy-preserving continual learning in computational pathology.
