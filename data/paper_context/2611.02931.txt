2 Materials and methods 2.1 Dataset and preprocessing Prostate biopsy blocks from the STHLM3 trial [ 8 ] were re-sectioned and sequentially scanned at three processing stages: raw, deparaffinized (DP) and H&E-stained. The original ISUP grades were retained as reference. The resulting stain-free histopathology (STHLM3-SFH) dataset comprises paired scans for training the generative models (250 raw/H&E and 117 DP/H&E slide pairs) and a test split of 79 slides from 67 patients scanned at all three stages (Table 1 ). WSIs were tiled (1024 × \times 1024 px, 0.5 μ \mu m/px) and to correct minor spatial shifts from sequential scanning, raw and DP images of the training set were each registered to their H&E counterparts in Fiji by template matching [ 9 ] on random patch pairs. Implementation: https://github.com/JAMielcarz/training-time-domain-transfer 2.3 Diagnostic model The model follows a prior multiple instance learning approach [ 11 ] , adjusted to benign vs. malignant classification: an EfficientNet-V2-S feature extractor followed by gated attention [ 12 ] . It was trained on 37,447 H&E WSIs from three cohorts (STHLM3; Stavanger University Hospital, SUH; S:t Göran Hospital, STG), either directly (H&E benchmark and inference-time virtual staining), or after virtual unstaining to each unstained stage (TTDT), with each model trained solely on synthetic data (Table 1 ). Ten cross-validation models were ensembled, and the threshold was optimized for F1 on a multi-cohort tuning set (STHLM3; Karolinska University Hospital, KUH; Radboud University Medical Center, RUMC). Diagnostic training and tuning cohorts (details in [ 11 ] ) share no patients with the STHLM3-SFH test set. 2.4 Evaluation Five scenarios were compared: real H&E (benchmark), synthetic H&E from raw or DP tissue (virtual staining), and real raw or DP tissue (TTDT). Each approach was compared with the H&E benchmark by DeLong’s test [ 13 ] on AUCs against the pathologist’s diagnosis, and by Cohen’s κ \kappa and Gwet’s AC1 [ 14 ] (robust to the high malignancy prevalence). 3 Results Evaluated against the pathologist on the test set (n=79), real H&E achieved the highest AUC for cancer detection (0.970; Table 2 , Fig. 2 ). Virtual staining reached AUCs of 0.932 (DP) and 0.859 (raw), and TTDT reached 0.922 and 0.904. Only virtual staining from raw input had a significantly lower AUC (p=0.016) than the benchmark and a low sensitivity (0.44). TTDT showed higher concordance with the H&E benchmark AI than virtual staining did for both raw ( κ \kappa =0.59 vs. 0.37; AC1=0.73 vs. 0.29) and DP input ( κ \kappa =0.66 vs. 0.61; AC1=0.73 vs. 0.64). Unlike domain adaptation [ 15 ] , which typically requires target domain data to train the task model, our diagnostic model receives none at any stage, making the transfer zero-shot. The generative model needs paired data, but once trained it enables the diagnostic model training at scale from vast source domain archives. The viability of this paradigm rests on the hypothesis that fewer paired samples are needed to train a sufficient generative model than real target samples to train a diagnostic model from scratch. As in sim-to-real learning, synthetic training data need not perfectly reproduce the target domain [ 2 ] , only capture sufficient structure for the downstream task. Pixel-wise metrics such as SSIM or PSNR are sensitive to misalignment and may not reflect the preservation of diagnostically relevant features. Thus, rather than evaluating visual similarity alone, we focus on diagnostic utility of synthetic images as training data. Across both tissue processing stages, the generator-free approach showed higher agreement with the H&E benchmark than virtual staining did, suggesting that avoiding inference-time generation may improve diagnostic consistency. Limitations of the study include data from a single laboratory and reference grading from the original collection; we therefore focused on concordance with the H&E benchmark AI. Closing the remaining sim-to-real gap through larger datasets or improved generators remains future work. In conclusion, training-time domain transfer removes generative AI from the diagnostic inference pipeline and enables zero-shot deployment on emerging modalities, where large training datasets are infeasible to collect.
