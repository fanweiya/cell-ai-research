Each rate applies to its own configuration’s covered task set, so comparing configurations would need a common set of reviewed tasks. 7 Discussion and conclusion 2D-FET-Bench V2 provides 128 executable layout tasks built on microscopy-derived flake contours. It lets researchers test whether language-model agents can build flake-specific device layouts from task text and contour coordinates, and how reliably they do so across five attempts on the same fixed input. Because each task gives only text and coordinates, success requires spatial reasoning from a textual device specification to flake-specific layout geometry. Across the 12 evaluated configurations, agents solve 117 of the 128 tasks at least once. GPT5.6-Luna solves 103 tasks at least once and 56 in all five attempts, and the six-model panel covers 105 tasks. Within GPT5.6-Luna, ReAct-3/C0 exceeds PaE by 27.0 pass@1 points at 2.46 times the recorded tokens. The expert audit adds a device-level check: across the five audited configurations, the expert accepts 56.4%–63.5% of sampled verifier-passing layouts, or 260 of 436 in total. The results suggest two ways to improve layout agents: match gate and contact construction to the transistor demand on each flake, and revise layouts without breaking constraints that already hold. The agentic workflow also shows potential for research acceleration: with eight tasks running in parallel, GPT5.6-Luna with ReAct-3/C0 completes one attempt on all 128 tasks in about 1.6 hours, the longest of the eight parallel first-attempt runs in our records. Based on our prior layout-design experience, we estimate that manually constructing layouts for all 128 tasks would take about 20 hours. This suggests that agents can build device layouts about ten times faster, if their layouts are as valid as manual ones at the device level. Such a speedup would shorten device design cycles and speed up 2D-material device research.
