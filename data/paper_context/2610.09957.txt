Diffusion models. Denoising diffusion models [ 35 , 12 ] and the related score-based formulation [ 39 ] are the de-facto generators for high-resolution image synthesis [ 5 , 26 ] . Sampling is accelerated by denoising diffusion implicit model (DDIM) [ 38 ] . It is made tractable at high resolution by latent diffusion [ 31 ] . It is most commonly conditioned via classifier-free guidance [ 13 ] . Fidelity and diversity in generative evaluation. Distributional metrics such as FID [ 11 ] and KID [ 1 ] combine sample quality and diversity into a single score. Even within their original natural-image setting, Inception-based scores do not consistently track human judgments of image quality [ 25 ] . This has motivated metrics that explicitly separate these factors, including precision and recall [ 19 ] , density and coverage [ 23 ] , and the reference-free Vendi score [ 8 ] . Our diagnostic follows this decomposition: inter-similarity to real images captures realism, while intra-similarity within generated samples captures diversity. Unlike previous metrics, we study their behavior in mask-conditioned biomedical diffusion, where realism often saturates while diversity collapses. The two axes must therefore be interpreted jointly and calibrated against a domain-specific reference distribution rather than ImageNet-based defaults [ 18 , 40 ] . 3 Method We evaluate a mask-conditioned diffusion model ( Section 3.1 ) using a diversity-aware diagnostic ( Section 3.2 ). When conditioning carries most of the spatial information, as in mask-conditioned diffusion, the remaining appearance degrees of freedom (texture, background, brightness) can collapse onto low-risk attractors while preserving the mask-defined structure. Late in training, we observe this as convergence toward a narrow color palette: the anti-correlation between color distance and inter-similarity ( r = − 0.71 r=-0.71 , Fig. 7 ) indicates that increasing similarity is achieved at the expense of appearance diversity. A related mechanism explains early collapse, where samples remain close to the dataset mean while still occupying the reference distribution in DINOv2 space. We expect this failure mode to extend beyond microtubule microscopy to other spatially conditioned generators, including pixel-space diffusion and small-domain conditional generative adversarial networks (cGANs) such as SPADE [ 28 ] . The aggregation strategy in Eq. 3 reflects this observation. A geometric mean prevents a strong score on one axis from compensating for collapse on another. The baseline-aware interpretation of mask fidelity highlights a broader issue in evaluating synthetic biomedical images. For thin filaments, segmentation itself is ambiguous: experts agree only to SKIoU 0.80 on real images. Therefore, geometric fidelity must be interpreted relative to this achievable ceiling. The comparison in Appendix A shows that a synthetic system can exceed this ceiling without necessarily producing more realistic images. Limitations. Our evaluation is based on a relatively small dataset ( 309 309 labeled 512 × 512 512\times 512 crops) from a single biomedical domain. Although DiffuMT shows downstream utility, the train-on-synthetic experiment uses real expert masks as conditioning, whereas the parametric baseline relies on procedural masks; thus, differences in mask distribution may contribute alongside appearance realism. Separating these two effects would require a controlled comparison on matched conditioning masks, which we leave to future work; likewise, generating synthetic masks that capture realistic microtubule geometry, so that the pipeline no longer depends on real expert masks at inference time, is nontrivial and is itself a direction for future work. The hyperparameter-transfer experiment demonstrates improved adaptation over the parametric synthetic baseline, but does not establish superiority over tuning directly on an equally sized real subset, since DiffuMT itself is generated from a model trained on real microscopy. Finally, while the mechanism likely extends to other spatially conditioned generators, demonstrating the same failure mode across additional biomedical domains such as CT and MRI remains future work. Unlike standard generative metrics, our diversity-aware diagnostic identifies the collapse of unconstrained appearance features that can occur while realism scores remain high. The selected model produces synthetic images that domain experts cannot reliably distinguish from real recordings in a 2AFC study and generates training data that transfers to real segmentation: a model trained only on DiffuMT reaches segmentation performance comparable to a real-trained model and substantially exceeds the parametric baseline. Beyond this dataset, our diagnostic provides a general evaluation tool for mask-conditioned diffusion models, where realism and diversity must be assessed jointly rather than through a single distributional score. Sixteen triplets, each row left to right: a SynthMT parametric sample, our DiffuMT sample on the same synthetic mask, and the real image most similar to it (nearest neighbor, k = 1 k\!=\!1 ). Dataset composition. Each released DiffuMT triplet pairs a binary mask with its real microscopy patch and one mask-conditioned synthetic sample ( Section 3.1 ). A key advantage of pixel-space mask conditioning is that the release is not limited to a fixed tiling of the annotated data: because every labeled 512 × 512 512\times 512 crop is larger than the 256 × 256 256\times 256 model resolution, we sample arbitrarily many random 256 × 256 256\times 256 views and synthesize a fresh conditional counterpart for each, turning 309 309 hand-annotated crops into a much larger triplet corpus at no additional labeling cost. Views are drawn only where the mask contains foreground (so every triplet shows filaments), and the 75 75 source recordings are partitioned into disjoint train/validation/test groups before cropping, so no recording—and therefore no underlying filament geometry—appears in more than one split. The public release contains 2000 2000 training, 400 400 validation and 400 400 test triplets built this way; each synthetic image is generated with DDIM sampling from the diagnostic-selected checkpoint (epoch 315), and filenames are anonymized to a running index so no acquisition metadata leaks. Reproducibility. Random seeds were not fixed across the stochastic components (initial U-Net weights, forward noise, the initial sampling noise x T x_{T} ; the DDIM reverse process itself is deterministic), though reading the diagnostic over 200 200 samples per checkpoint keeps the per-axis numbers stable to this, and every trend reported in the paper holds across all eight hyperparameter configurations ( Section 4 ). On publication we will release configuration files that fix the seeds and reproduce the numbers in Table 2 , together with the diagnostic implementation, the 2AFC analysis code, and the trained checkpoint.
