3 Methodology A schematic illustration of our proposed MRPT model is shown in Fig. 2 . The multi-resolution representations are learned in a hierarchical manner, progressing from the cell-level to the patch-level, then to the region-level, and finally to the WSI-level. At each level, multi-resolution SSL is employed to learn rich, fine-grained, and coarse-grained representations. To address the first challenge, we adopt a hierarchical WSI structure inspired by HIPT [ chen2022scaling ] . We extend the ViT architecture for multi-resolution WSI representation learning by employing a nested aggregation of visual tokens that are recursively decomposed into smaller units. To address the second challenge, we propose a multi-resolution ViT that incorporates an efficient Consecutive Cross-Resolution Attention (CCRA) mechanism to effectively integrate information across consecutive resolutions. Our proposed MRPT follows a multi-resolution hierarchical structure ( M → { R i } i = 1 m → { P i j } j = 1 256 → { C i , k j } k = 1 256 \textbf{M}\rightarrow\{\textbf{R}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\rightarrow\{\textbf{P}^{j}_{i}\}_{j=1}^{256}\rightarrow\{\textbf{C}^{j}_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{256} ) (Fig. 6 ). At the lowest hierarchical level, a ViT module aggregates cell-level features across consecutive resolutions using the CCRA mechanism. This design enables MRPT to capture hierarchical and multi-resolution structures of WSIs and to model clinically meaningful relationships across resolutions. The pre-training of MRPT is performed in three distinct stages. In Stage 1 (Fig. 2 (a)-(b)), we pre-train cell-level features { C i , k j } k = 1 256 \{\textbf{C}^{j}_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{256} using multi-resolution SSL paradigm, where both teacher and student networks leverage a CCRA-enabled ViT architecture, referred to as mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} . Here, C ∈ C i , k j \textrm{C}\in\textbf{C}_{i,k}^{j} represents multi-resolution cell-level tokens extracted from patches { P i j } j = 1 256 \{\textbf{P}_{i}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{256} . In Stage 2 (Fig. 2 (c)), the weights of mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} are frozen and re-used as an embedding layer for patch-level pre-training using another ViT module, ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} , where P ∈ P i j \textrm{P}\in\textbf{P}_{i}^{j} represents multi-resolution patch-level tokens. ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} aggregates patch-level embeddings to learn region-level representations R i \textbf{R}_{i} . In Stage 3 (Fig. 2 (d)), the weights of ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} are frozen and re-used as a backbone for WSI-level pre-training using ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} , where R ∈ { R i } i = 1 m \textrm{R}\in\{\textbf{R}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m} denotes multi-resolution region-level tokens. Each pre-training stage of MRPT is detailed in the following sections. 3.2.1 Multi-Resolution Cell Aggregation ( mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} ) The aggregation of multi-resolution cell-level tokens within P i j \textbf{P}_{i}^{j} is performed using the proposed mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} (Figs. 2 (a)–(b)). This module employs the CCRA mechanism to integrate information across consecutive resolutions. To obtain the learnable embeddings [mCLS] P i j \textrm{[mCLS]}_{\textbf{P}_{i}^{j}} , the cell-level representations across all resolutions are aggregated along the token sequence. Multi-resolution SSL paradigm is employed in which local views are processed by the student network and global views are processed by the teacher. Both teacher and student learn to align the multi-resolution representation. Below, we discuss the general architecture of mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} , which is the same for both student and teacher. Given P i j \textbf{P}_{i}^{j} , mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} decomposes it into r r sequences, each of length 256, corresponding to non-overlapping c r × c r c_{r}\times c_{r} pixel tokens at different resolutions r ∈ { 10 × , 20 × , 40 × } r\in\{10\times,20\times,40\times\} , where c r c_{r} denotes the cell-level patch size at r r . For each resolution, a linear projection layer with positional embedding is applied to obtain a sequence embedding S r \textbf{S}_{r} , which includes an appended [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} token. A transformer ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} is then pre-trained independently to learn resolution-specific embeddings. The number of encoder layers in each ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} is adjusted according to the resolution to balance computation. Finally, the outputs of ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} from two consecutive resolutions are fused via the proposed CCRA mechanism to yield the multi-resolution representation. mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} Architecture: mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} consists of a stack of Q Q hierarchical fusion blocks. Each block contains three branches that process tokens from the three resolutions and fuse their outputs using the CCRA mechanism. As illustrated in Fig. 2 (a), the architecture is composed of Q Q multi-resolution transformer encoders, where each encoder comprises: (1) a 10 × \times branch handling coarse-grained features ( 8 × 8 8\times 8 patch size) with L L transformer layers, (2) a 20 × \times branch processing medium-grained features ( 16 × 16 16\times 16 patch size) with M M layers, and (3) a 40 × \times branch focusing on fine-grained features ( 32 × 32 32\times 32 patch size) with N N layers. The three branches are fused Q Q times through CCRA layers that enable the [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens of all branches to exchange contextual information across resolutions. Consecutive Cross-Resolution Attention (CCRA): The proposed CCRA module (Fig. 2 (b)) fuses information between two consecutive resolutions by averaging their [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens and appending them to the patch tokens of the finer sequence. This design allows efficient and interpretable cross-resolution information exchange. Specifically, the [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens from both sequences serve as information carriers between resolutions and are back-projected to their respective branches. Because [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens encode abstract-level semantics of their sequences, interacting with patch tokens of other resolutions enriches both coarse- and fine-grained contextual representations. The process continues across CCRA layers, where [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens iteratively refine their own sequence representations, thereby enhancing semantic alignment across resolutions. This mechanism efficiently fuses coarse and fine features at the cell level while maintaining semantic consistency. While performing cross-attention between consecutive resolutions, we preserve the hierarchical diagnostic logic used by pathologists: 10 × \times magnification provides a global overview of tissue architecture, 20 × \times reveals local glandular and stromal organization, and 40 × \times exposes nuclear and cellular morphology. Pathologists examine WSIs sequentially across resolutions rather than skipping scales, as intermediate resolutions contextualize cellular detail within broader architecture. Inspired by this workflow, MRPT restricts cross-attention to consecutive resolution pairs (10 × ↔ 20 × \times\leftrightarrow 20\times , 20 × ↔ 40 × \times\leftrightarrow 40\times ), ensuring semantic continuity and minimizing misalignment across distant representations. This design produces biologically interpretable and diagnostically meaningful embeddings ( see ablation ). In Stage 1 (Fig. 2 (a)-(b)), we pre-train cell-level features { C i , k j } k = 1 256 \{\textbf{C}^{j}_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{256} using multi-resolution SSL paradigm, where both teacher and student networks leverage a CCRA-enabled ViT architecture, referred to as mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} . Here, C ∈ C i , k j \textrm{C}\in\textbf{C}_{i,k}^{j} represents multi-resolution cell-level tokens extracted from patches { P i j } j = 1 256 \{\textbf{P}_{i}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{256} . In Stage 2 (Fig. 2 (c)), the weights of mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} are frozen and re-used as an embedding layer for patch-level pre-training using another ViT module, ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} , where P ∈ P i j \textrm{P}\in\textbf{P}_{i}^{j} represents multi-resolution patch-level tokens. ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} aggregates patch-level embeddings to learn region-level representations R i \textbf{R}_{i} . In Stage 3 (Fig. 2 (d)), the weights of ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} are frozen and re-used as a backbone for WSI-level pre-training using ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} , where R ∈ { R i } i = 1 m \textrm{R}\in\{\textbf{R}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m} denotes multi-resolution region-level tokens. ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} aggregates the region-level embeddings to produce the final WSI-level representation. Both mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} and ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} operate with a sequence length of 256 tokens, while ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} processes approximately m ≈ 256 m\approx 256 region tokens per resolution. When small ViT backbones are employed at each stage, MRPT contains fewer than 12M parameters, making it computationally efficient and trainable on standard GPU workstations. Each pre-training stage of MRPT is detailed in the following sections. 3.2.1 Multi-Resolution Cell Aggregation ( mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} ) The aggregation of multi-resolution cell-level tokens within P i j \textbf{P}_{i}^{j} is performed using the proposed mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} (Figs. 2 (a)–(b)). This module employs the CCRA mechanism to integrate information across consecutive resolutions. To obtain the learnable embeddings [mCLS] P i j \textrm{[mCLS]}_{\textbf{P}_{i}^{j}} , the cell-level representations across all resolutions are aggregated along the token sequence. Multi-resolution SSL paradigm is employed in which local views are processed by the student network and global views are processed by the teacher. Both teacher and student learn to align the multi-resolution representation. Below, we discuss the general architecture of mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} , which is the same for both student and teacher. Given P i j \textbf{P}_{i}^{j} , mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} decomposes it into r r sequences, each of length 256, corresponding to non-overlapping c r × c r c_{r}\times c_{r} pixel tokens at different resolutions r ∈ { 10 × , 20 × , 40 × } r\in\{10\times,20\times,40\times\} , where c r c_{r} denotes the cell-level patch size at r r . For each resolution, a linear projection layer with positional embedding is applied to obtain a sequence embedding S r \textbf{S}_{r} , which includes an appended [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} token. A transformer ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} is then pre-trained independently to learn resolution-specific embeddings. The number of encoder layers in each ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} is adjusted according to the resolution to balance computation. Finally, the outputs of ViT r \textrm{ViT}_{r} from two consecutive resolutions are fused via the proposed CCRA mechanism to yield the multi-resolution representation. mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} Architecture: mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} consists of a stack of Q Q hierarchical fusion blocks. Each block contains three branches that process tokens from the three resolutions and fuse their outputs using the CCRA mechanism. As illustrated in Fig. 2 (a), the architecture is composed of Q Q multi-resolution transformer encoders, where each encoder comprises: (1) a 10 × \times branch handling coarse-grained features ( 8 × 8 8\times 8 patch size) with L L transformer layers, (2) a 20 × \times branch processing medium-grained features ( 16 × 16 16\times 16 patch size) with M M layers, and (3) a 40 × \times branch focusing on fine-grained features ( 32 × 32 32\times 32 patch size) with N N layers. The three branches are fused Q Q times through CCRA layers that enable the [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens of all branches to exchange contextual information across resolutions. Consecutive Cross-Resolution Attention (CCRA): The proposed CCRA module (Fig. 2 (b)) fuses information between two consecutive resolutions by averaging their [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens and appending them to the patch tokens of the finer sequence. This design allows efficient and interpretable cross-resolution information exchange. Specifically, the [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens from both sequences serve as information carriers between resolutions and are back-projected to their respective branches. Because [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens encode abstract-level semantics of their sequences, interacting with patch tokens of other resolutions enriches both coarse- and fine-grained contextual representations. The process continues across CCRA layers, where [ CLS ] r [\textrm{CLS}]_{r} tokens iteratively refine their own sequence representations, thereby enhancing semantic alignment across resolutions. This mechanism efficiently fuses coarse and fine features at the cell level while maintaining semantic consistency. While performing cross-attention between consecutive resolutions, we preserve the hierarchical diagnostic logic used by pathologists: 10 × \times magnification provides a global overview of tissue architecture, 20 × \times reveals local glandular and stromal organization, and 40 × \times exposes nuclear and cellular morphology. Pathologists examine WSIs sequentially across resolutions rather than skipping scales, as intermediate resolutions contextualize cellular detail within broader architecture. Inspired by this workflow, MRPT restricts cross-attention to consecutive resolution pairs (10 × ↔ 20 × \times\leftrightarrow 20\times , 20 × ↔ 40 × \times\leftrightarrow 40\times ), ensuring semantic continuity and minimizing misalignment across distant representations. This design produces biologically interpretable and diagnostically meaningful embeddings ( see ablation ). Training Details: We pre-train MRPT on 36,000 WSIs in total, comprising 30,000 WSIs from diverse TCGA cancer types [ hutter2018cancer ] and 6,000 WSIs from CPTAC [ Edwards2015CPTAC ] . MRPT is pre-trained on 624M patches using mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} , 2.4M regions using ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} , and 36K WSIs using ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} . For mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} , we employ three resolution-specific ViTs with L = 2 L=2 , M = 4 M=4 , and N = 4 N=4 encoder layers (see Table 1 ), using an embedding dimension of 384 for ViT 10 ⁣ × \textrm{ViT}_{10\times} and 768 for both ViT 20 ⁣ × \textrm{ViT}_{20\times} and ViT 40 ⁣ × \textrm{ViT}_{40\times} . We use K = 1 K=1 CCRA layer and a total of Q = 4 Q=4 blocks in mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} . For ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} , we set n = 4 n=4 encoders, h = 6 h=6 heads, and d = 192 d=192 embedding dimension; for ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} , we use n = 2 n=2 , h = 3 h=3 , and d = 192 d=192 , following HIPT [ chen2022scaling ] . We train mViT P ​ - C \textrm{mViT}_{\textbf{P}}\textrm{-}\textrm{C} for 250K iterations using AdamW [ adam2014method ] with batch size 256 and base learning rate 0.0007; the first 20 epochs are warm-up to the base rate, followed by cosine decay [ loshchilov2016sgdr ] . ViT R ​ - P \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{R}}\textrm{-}\textrm{P} and ViT M ​ - R \textrm{ViT}_{\textbf{M}}\textrm{-}\textrm{R} are each trained for 200K iterations. In DINO [ caron2021emerging ] , the teacher is updated via EMA of the student. For MRPT-LLaVA , stage 1 uses learning rate 0.001 and batch size 64; only a two-layer projection head is trained to align WSI-text features. We apply LoRA with rank 128 and α = 256 \alpha=256 for efficient tuning. All models are trained on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Patch-level Tasks: We evaluate patch-level classification and VQA across 17 datasets. Patch-level classification uses 13 independent datasets: WSSS4LUAD [ han2022wsss4luad ] , SICAP [ silva2021self ] , DigestPath [ da2022digestpath ] , CRC100K [ Kather2018_colorectal ] , RenalCell [ brummer2022integrative ] , MHIST [ wei2021petri ] , PCam [ veeling2018rotation ] , Osteo [ arunachalam2019viable ] , BACH [ iciar2018grand ] , SkinCancer [ kriegsmann2022deep ] , LC-Lung [ Borkowski2019_LC25000 ] , LC-Colon [ Borkowski2019_LC25000 ] , and DataBiox [ bolhasani2020histopathological ] , with both zero-shot and linear-probe settings. Zero-shot patch-level VQA is conducted on five datasets including QuiltVQA [ seyfioglu2024quilt ] , PathVQA [ He2020_PathVQA ] , PMC-VQA [ zhang2023pmcvqa ] , and PathMMU [ Sun2024_PathMMU ] . WSI-level Tasks: We evaluate WSI-level classification, VQA, captioning, and report generation across 17 datasets. WSI-level classification uses 10 external datasets: PANDA [ Bulten2022_PANDA ] , HunCRC [ pataki2022huncrc ] , CPTAC-NSCLC [ Edwards2015CPTAC ] , Brains30 [ roetzer2022digital ] , BRACS [ brancati2022bracs ] , TCGA-RCC [ hutter2018cancer ] , DHMC-RCC [ zhu2021development ] , Camelyon17 [ bandi2018detection ] , and UBC-OCEAN [ UBC-OCEAN ] , with zero-shot, WSMIL, and FSWL evaluations. WSI-level VQA and captioning are evaluated on SlideBench-Caption [ Chen_2025_CVPR ] , SlideBench-VQA (TCGA) [ Chen_2025_CVPR ] , SlideBench-VQA (BCNB) [ Chen_2025_CVPR ] , WSI-VQA [ Chen2025_WSI-VQA ] , and WSI-Bench [ Liang2024_WSI-LLaVA ] . WSI-level report generation uses HistGen [ guo2024histgen ] and WSI-Bench (Report) [ Liang2024_WSI-LLaVA ] .
