IV-A Architecture and Preprocessing Before classification, individual pollen grains are isolated using a U 2 U^{2} -Net detection pipeline. The network processes 320x320 monochrome images to generate a salient object probability map P ⁡ ( x , y ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] P(x,y)\in[0,1] . This map is binarized using an adaptive threshold T = μ ⁡ ( P ) + k ⋅ σ ⁡ ( P ) T=\mu(P)+k\cdot\sigma(P) , and refined via 3x3 morphological operations. Extracted contours are filtered by area (5000–120,000 px) and circularity ( C ≥ 0.3 C\geq 0.3 ). Localized crops are then classified using a DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone. Crucially, a normalized Slate Gray background (monochrome 128) acts as a zero-activation mask, bypassing computationally expensive masked pooling (Fig. 4 ). By normalizing the gray background (128), the resulting pixel values are almost zero. This means that the background does not trigger the neural network, eliminating the need for computationally expensive masked pooling. IV-E Embedding Model To construct a robust embedding space, AnalogyNet utilizes a DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone, which offers a global receptive field and universal feature extraction via self-supervised pre-training. Despite its smaller footprint (21M parameters vs. ResNet-50’s 25.5M), this Transformer-based architecture provides superior efficiency and emergent segmentation capabilities. The pipeline (Fig. 4 in Supp. material) processes a 252 × 252 252\times 252 monochrome image into an 18 × 18 18\times 18 grid of 324 patch tokens (plus one CLS token) with a hidden dimension of 384. These propagate through 12 encoder blocks featuring multi-head self-attention (6 heads) and SwiGLU activations. To derive the final representation, the network applies mean pooling over the spatial patch tokens, yielding a 384-dimensional vector. This vector is passed through a three-layer MLP Projection Head to reach a 128-dimensional latent space. Finally, L 2 L_{2} normalization projects the embedding onto a unit hypersphere ( S 127 S^{127} ), ensuring that the squared Euclidean distance is monotonically equivalent to cosine similarity: ‖ a − b ‖ 2 2 = 2 ​ ( 1 − cos ⁡ ( a , b ) ) \|a-b\|_{2}^{2}=2(1-\cos(a,b)) This geometric constraint allows the model to optimize taxonomic similarities with high precision and consistency. Concerning the operational efficiency, traditional melissopalynological analysis is a highly labor-intensive and operator-dependent task, where the manual identification of a single sample under a microscope can take from several hours to a full day, depending on pollen diversity and preservation [ 37 ] . Specifically, manual counts of merely 600 pollen grains can take between 2 and 4 hours for an expert palynologist [ 38 ] . Given that the Chilean standard NCh2981.Of2005 requires a count of at least 1,200 grains to validate the botanical origin of honey [ 18 , 39 ] , a human expert would need an estimated 4 to 8 hours per sample. In stark contrast, our automatic classifier, operating at an average of 126.0 ms per image, processes the 1,600 images required to reach the 1,200-grain target in approximately 3.36 minutes of pure computational time. Even when accounting for the mechanization of scanning displacement control, which may scale this overall time by a maximum of ∽ \backsim 30 minutes per sample coverslip, the proposed solution still vastly exceeds the 6-fold acceleration threshold compared to manual labor, effectively resolving the temporal bottleneck inherent in traditional analytical laboratories. VI Conclusion By coupling a controlled, automated brightfield microscopy platform with a specialized Deep Metric Learning pipeline, this work effectively resolves the primary operational bottlenecks inherent to traditional melissopalynology. The proposed system delivers a processing speedup of at least 6x-8x over manual analysis while maintaining expert-level accuracy and providing visually explainable diagnostic features. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that the optimal configuration utilizes a DINOv2 backbone trained via a Multi-Similarity Loss. This architecture, combined with a deterministic preprocessing strategy using a normalized Slate Gray background, efficiently maps targets to a highly separable 128-dimensional unit hypersphere ( 𝕊 127 \mathbb{S}^{127} ). This approach establishes a highly efficient, scalable, and interpretable framework that outperforms traditional CNN baselines across global latent space metrics. Future research will expand the scope of these models to include new pollen datasets and human cell analysis. Specifically, we aim to implement automatic morphological classification of red blood cell (RBC) deformations induced by conditions such as sickle cell disease and various anemias [ 31 , 32 , 33 ] . Furthermore, given that erythrocyte mechanical alterations are closely linked to glucose levels and gestational diabetes [ 34 , 35 , 36 ] , achieving high-accuracy automated diagnosis remains a critical priority for clinical decision-making.
