Traditional SVD Methods Singular Value Decomposition decomposes a matrix 𝐀 ∈ ℝ m × n \mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} as 𝐀 = 𝐔 ​ 𝚺 ​ 𝐕 ⊤ \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top} , where 𝐔 ∈ O ⁡ ( m ) \mathbf{U}\in\mathrm{O}(m) and 𝐕 ∈ O ⁡ ( n ) \mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{O}(n) are orthogonal matrices and 𝚺 \mathbf{\Sigma} contains ordered singular values Golub and Van Loan (2013) . The full SVD has cubic time complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( m , n ) ⋅ m ​ n ) \mathcal{O}(\min(m,n)\cdot mn) via Householder bidiagonalization and QR iteration Golub and Reinsch (1970) , which impose sequential dependencies for batched matrix factorisations on modern parallel hardware and thus limit the throughput Lahabar and Narayanan (2009) . The computational burden is further compounded when moving to high-order arrays, where Tensor SVD Zhang and Xia (2018) encounters a fundamental statistical-computational gap that renders optimal recovery NP-hard under moderate signal-to-noise ratios. Approximation methods like Truncated SVD Hansen (1987) ; Hansen (1990) ; Stoll (2012) and Randomised SVD Halko et al. (2011) ; Musco and Musco (2015) reduce complexity but retain sequential bottlenecks that prevent efficient GPU parallelisation. Rank–revealing QR still hinges on pivoted column selection heuristics that require dependent branching and synchronization Chan (1987) ; Gu and Eisenstat (1996) . Nyström methods only postpone the problem by reducing the effective matrix size Drineas et al. (2006) ; Musco and Musco (2017) , but the subsequent SVD on the reduced core is still sequential and remains the runtime limit. As alternatives, Streaming SVD updates decomposition dynamically as new data arrives Brand (2006) and k-SVD employs an iterative framework to learn sparse representations by optimizing dictionary atoms Aharon et al. (2006) ; Scetbon et al. (2021) . Despite their sophistication, these methods do not resolve the core structural barrier, which is to map cleanly to highly parallel computation architectures. Learning-Based Approaches Recent work has explored neural networks for SVD-related tasks. SV-Learn Xu et al. (2022) uses MLPs to predict singular values but suffers from numerical instability due to soft constraints and cannot recover singular vectors. Dictionary - learning methods such as Deep K - SVD Scetbon et al. (2021) produce sparse representations via iterative dictionary updates which rely on solvers with loop - heavy control flow that resists the parallelism targeted here. Operator-SVD Ryu et al. (2024) learns spectral decompositions of linear operators through nested low-rank approximation, achieving theoretical elegance but at significant computational cost unsuitable for real-time imaging. Beyond imaging, SVD also underlies spectral clustering layers, though the high computational cost of the decomposition often necessitates its replacement with dual autoencoder architectures to maintain end-to-end efficiency Yang et al. (2019) . In the large language model (LLM) domain, SVD has attracted attention for activation whitening Yuan et al. (2023) , low-rank weight approximation Wang et al. (2026) , and truncation-aware model compression Yuan et al. (2023) ; Wang et al. (2025) , underscoring the broad demand for efficient factorisation. More broadly, end-to-end (E2E) methods learn a desired reconstruction mapping directly from data offering fast inference capabilities for images, as for example Learned SVD (L-SVD) Boink and Brune (2019) or SVD-inspired filter networks Cole et al. (2022) included as learning-based baselines. In contrast, the proposed study retains an explicit, mathematically principled matrix-factorisation structure while approximating the decomposition to achieve the throughput required for real-time imaging. 3 Proposed method This work targets an efficient, learnable SVD approximation that preserves reconstruction and downstream accuracy while minimizing GPU wall - clock latency and keeping training cost practical. The Quasi - SVD framework comprises a spectrum of module variants (illustrated in Fig. 1 ): analytic closed - form, unconstrained neural net (UNN), Lie - informed parametrizations, and end - to - end prediction where each trades off accuracy, latency, and training effort. End-to-end (E2E) learning Many applications of the SVD involve reconstructing a filtered version 𝐌 \mathbf{M} of an input matrix 𝐀 \mathbf{A} . For instance, the Lu-Chipman decomposition in Mueller matrix imaging produces 𝐌 = 𝐔𝐃𝐕 ⊤ \mathbf{M}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{V}^{\top} after factorisation where 𝐃 = diag ⁡ ( 𝐝 ) \mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{d}) and 𝐝 ∈ { − 1 , 1 } m \mathbf{d}\in\{-1,1\}^{m} Lu and Chipman (1996) . The polarimetric dataset requires performing a very large number of small decompositions, whereas the ultrasound dataset involves fewer but substantially larger matrices and the MNIST dataset lies inbetween. Together, these cases capture the range of memory and performance trade-offs targeted by the proposed method. Polarimetric data MMP is an imaging technique with growing diagnostic relevance in medical imaging Sampaio et al. (2023) ; Moriconi et al. (2024) . For visualization, the Mueller matrix is typically decomposed via SVD following Lu and Chipman Lu and Chipman (1996) , enabling interpretation of polarimetric features. MNIST denoising data As a controlled benchmark with known ground truth, a denoising task is constructed from MNIST handwritten digits ( 28 × 28 28\times 28 grayscale). A 10 × 10 10\times 10 tile grid assembles 100 randomly permuted digits into a single 280 × 280 280\times 280 matrix, providing an intermediate scale regime. Each tile receives independent Gaussian noise ( σ = 0.2 \sigma=0.2 ), with per-epoch D4 dihedral transforms and a random background offset drawn from [ 0 , 0.5 ] [0,0.5] applied to clean tiles before noise injection; tile order is shuffled per sample to prevent memorisation and penalise all-zero matrices. Supp. Fig. details how this advantage grows with matrix and batch size. 4.1 Datasets and assessment The selected datasets represent complementary SVD workload regimes. The polarimetric dataset requires performing a very large number of small decompositions, whereas the ultrasound dataset involves fewer but substantially larger matrices and the MNIST dataset lies inbetween. Together, these cases capture the range of memory and performance trade-offs targeted by the proposed method. Polarimetric data MMP is an imaging technique with growing diagnostic relevance in medical imaging Sampaio et al. (2023) ; Moriconi et al. (2024) . For visualization, the Mueller matrix is typically decomposed via SVD following Lu and Chipman Lu and Chipman (1996) , enabling interpretation of polarimetric features. In neuropathology, the per-pixel i i azimuth angle φ i = 1 / 2 ​ tan − 1 ⁡ ( M i ( 2 , 4 ) / M i ( 4 , 3 ) ) \varphi_{i}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\tan^{-1}(\nicefrac{{M_{i}^{(2,4)}}}{{M_{i}^{(4,3)}}}) derived from 𝐌 i \mathbf{M}_{i} in Eq. ( 11 ) encodes fiber orientation, a biomarker for tumor identification Hahne et al. (2025a) . Although near real-time MMP has been demonstrated Moriconi et al. (2024) , instant visualization of Lu–Chipman features remains challenging. The proposed Quasi-SVD model is evaluated on the NeuroPathoPol (NPP) dataset, with the public mouse uterine cervix (MUC) data Novikova et al. (2024) ; Pogudin (2024) reserved for domain-transfer analysis. NPP comprises 388 × 516 388\times 516 -pixel images, each pixel associated with a 4 × 4 4\times 4 Mueller matrix 𝐁 i = B i ( u , v ) \mathbf{B}_{i}=B_{i}^{(u,v)} . Training and validation use 12 tumor and 9 non-tumor brain samples, while testing employs 5 tumor and 3 healthy samples. For each pixel i i , a normalised input matrix 𝐀 i ∈ ℝ 3 × 3 \mathbf{A}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3} is derived from 𝐁 i \mathbf{B}_{i} as its bottom-right 3 × 3 3\times 3 block scaled by the ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) entry, 𝐀 i = 𝐁 i ( 2 : 4 , 2 : 4 ) / B i ( 1 , 1 ) \mathbf{A}_{i}=\mathbf{B}_{i}^{(2:4,\,2:4)}/B_{i}^{(1,1)} . Data augmentation through random rotation and flipping increases the diversity of training samples Hahne et al. (2025b) . MNIST denoising data As a controlled benchmark with known ground truth, a denoising task is constructed from MNIST handwritten digits ( 28 × 28 28\times 28 grayscale). A 10 × 10 10\times 10 tile grid assembles 100 randomly permuted digits into a single 280 × 280 280\times 280 matrix, providing an intermediate scale regime. Each tile receives independent Gaussian noise ( σ = 0.2 \sigma=0.2 ), with per-epoch D4 dihedral transforms and a random background offset drawn from [ 0 , 0.5 ] [0,0.5] applied to clean tiles before noise injection; tile order is shuffled per sample to prevent memorisation and penalise all-zero matrices. Ultrasound data Many imaging applications employ the SVD for background removal Xin et al. (2015) . In ULM, the SVD is a key processing step to suppress static reflectors (e.g., bone) and isolate microbubble flow, commonly realised through the Karhunen–Loève transform (KLT) Demené et al. (2015) ; Baranger et al. (2018) ; Heiles et al. (2022) . Given a Casorati matrix 𝐁 c ∈ ℝ h × n \mathbf{B}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times n} constructed from n n frames with h h pixels, each 𝐁 c \mathbf{B}_{c} forms the Hermitian matrix 𝐀 = 𝐁 c ⊤ ​ 𝐁 c \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}_{c}^{\top}\mathbf{B}_{c} and uses its singular vectors for subspace denoising. Evaluation uses publicly available in vivo ULM data from the PALA study Heiles et al. (2022) , comprising rat brain perfusion sequences acquired with a 15.6 MHz linear probe (128 elements, 0.1 mm pitch) at 1 kHz frame rate with multi-angle plane waves. B-mode data from rat 18 is employed, containing 250 sequences of 500 frames each. The first 20 sequences are used for training and validation (0.9 split), and the final 5 for testing. Each B-mode image contains h = 143 × 167 h=143\times 167 pixels, yielding 𝐀 ∈ ℝ 500 × 500 \mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{500\times 500} from n = 500 n=500 stacked frames. Metrics Reconstruction quality and decomposition accuracy are reported using both perceptual and numerical metrics. The structural similarity index (SSIM) measures local luminance, contrast, and structure using an 11-pixel Gaussian window. Comparison methods The proposed framework is evaluated against classical and learning-based SVD techniques: (i) Exact SVD, a reference implementation via cuSOLVER NVIDIA Corporation (2026) using one-sided Jacobi SVD for small matrices ( m , n ≤ 32 m,n\leq 32 ) and traditional decomposition otherwise Golub and Reinsch (1970) , serving as the numerical baseline for accuracy; (ii) SV-Learn, an MLP-based predictor of singular values only, without singular vector reconstruction Xu et al. (2022) ; (iii) end-to-end (E2E) networks, as in L-SVD Boink and Brune (2019) ; Cole et al. (2022) , trained to directly reconstruct SVD-filtered outputs; and (iv) rSVD, a stochastic low-rank approximation with three power iterations, implemented in PyTorch Halko et al. (2011) , whose oversampling parameter q q is set to the validation-optimal truncation rank of classical SVD. For ablation, each learned model is extended with an RNN, and the analytical and UNN variants are included.
