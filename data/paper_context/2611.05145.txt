4 Results All 1,341 study runs and 1,580 follow-up tasks completed, none diverged, and every result matched its registered split hash. Table 2 lists all registered tests. 4.1 Crop-level splitting inflates both benchmarks On SIPaKMeD, accuracy was 97.14% under P1 and 96.18% under P2 (H1 = = 0.97 pp, 0.59–1.39), with all four sensitivity analyses at p p < < 0.001. Every architecture scored higher under P1, by 0.66–1.44 pp (Fig. 2 a): crop-level splitting hides a quarter of the errors. Merging near-duplicate-linked parents changed nothing (P2 − - P3: 0.03 pp, −0.49 to 0.56), so shared parents, not stray duplicates, carry the effect, and the architecture ranking was largely preserved (Kendall τ = 0.82 \tau=0.82 ). On CRIC, with about 29 mixed-class cells per slide, accuracy fell from 90.48% to 78.40% (H2 = = 12.08 pp; 11.6–12.8 pp per architecture; Fig. 2 b) and balanced accuracy from 78.53% to 58.89%; a size-matched crop-level control gave the same gap (12.11 pp). That control fixes training size, not slide composition, and the factorial below was run on SIPaKMeD only, so the CRIC gap is consistent with acquisition leakage but not causally decomposed. Our CRIC inputs are 100 × 100 100\times 100 windows around the published nucleus coordinates, so absolute accuracies are not comparable with other CRIC results. 4.2 On SIPaKMeD, the inflation is leakage, not diversity At fixed crop count, field count and allocation, adding the test fields’ siblings raised accuracy on the common test set by 1.73 pp (0.95–2.51) in every architecture at both diversity levels, while 43% more fields without leakage did nothing (0.00 pp, −0.65 to 0.64; interaction −0.20 pp, p = 0.60 p=0.60 ; Fig. 2 c). The leak replicated on fresh seeds (1.47 pp) and persisted with every background masked in training and testing (F3: 1.86 pp). In an exploratory split it was as large on test crops sharing no pixel with any training crop (1.68 pp) as on overlapping ones (1.78 pp): what helps is a seen field, not memorised pixels. Reducing the linearly decodable parent code did not move the gap either (Fig. 3 a). Rank-256 LEACE cut R R from 25.1% to 5.4%, removing 82% of its above-floor value (class-only floor 1.2%), in all twelve architectures at a 0.17-pp cost in P2 accuracy, and the gap stayed at 0.93–0.95 pp (F5); the linear adversary passed its check in all three architectures with no change (F7); pooled over 15 cells the slope was 0.000 (−0.005 to 0.005; F8), so a 20-pp loss of R R moves the gap by at most 0.1 pp. Grad-CAM put 21.8% of attribution outside the cell in leak and clean models alike (difference −0.09 pp, −0.57 to 0.39; Fig. 3 c), although its maps passed the randomised-head check (median Spearman ρ = 0.08 \rho=0.08 ). On CRIC, seen slides pulled minority-class cells away from the slide majority (F1: −4.29 pp), which argues against a simple slide-majority prior. Only color transfer moved the leak. Matching each test cell’s color statistics to an unseen field of its own class cut the leak from 1.72 to 0.46 pp (F2: 1.27 pp, 0.38–2.16), whereas flattening illumination (1.30 pp) or removing the background (1.17 pp), at comparable accuracy cost, did not (Fig. 3 b); strong HED jitter in training left it intact (1.71 vs. 1.47 pp; F4). Because the transfer is label-conditioned and shifts the test distribution, it implicates field-specific appearance without identifying what the networks learned. 5 Discussion and conclusion Crop-level splitting inflates both public cervical cytology benchmarks we trained on. On SIPaKMeD the inflation is leakage: it appears when a test cell’s acquisition siblings are in training, not when training merely gains parent diversity. On CRIC, with 29 mixed-class cells per slide instead of four single-class crops per field, the gap is twelve times larger; without a factorial there, it is consistent with, not attributed to, sibling leakage. A benchmark’s exposure can be computed from the closed forms of Sec. 2 before any model is trained. Rankings largely survived on SIPaKMeD, but accuracies under crop- and parent-level splits are not comparable: on CRIC the protocol gap exceeds the spread between architectures. The mechanism is narrowed, not identified. The advantage survives background masking, does not depend on shared pixels, does not follow a simple slide-majority prior and is unaffected by reducing the linear parent code, while label-conditioned color transfer implicates field-specific appearance; nonlinear or spatially encoded parent information may still carry it. The methodological lesson holds regardless: models whose measured parent code was largely erased, and whose saliency matched that of clean models, still benefited from leakage. Probes establish availability, not use [ 5 , 18 ] , and neither probes, saliency nor augmentation can stand in for a grouped split. Limitations. Parents are the deepest released units, not patients: parent-disjoint evaluation removes the leakage measured here, but patient-level grouping could reveal further dependence that these experiments cannot bound. The factorial trains on 1,100 crops rather than 3,239, establishing the leak’s existence and direction more firmly than an exact decomposition of H1. One training recipe was used, erasure acts on linearly readable pooled features, and the follow-up, registered after the Study 2 results, is confirmatory only for its own questions. Recommendations. Releases should label every observation with its parent, and its patient where permitted; evaluations should split at the deepest released unit, publish hashed fold manifests and report the fraction of test observations with a training sibling. Code, provenance maps, split manifests, run records and a risk calculator are at https://github.com/optimisticexquisite/cytofair .
