III Methodology III-A Cell Identification After each WSI or ROI is divided into patches, nuclei are detected within each patch to obtain cell-level spatial locations. This step provides the cell centroids required for constructing patch-level graphs. We use a pretrained StarDist2D model [ 19 ] for nuclei detection in this work. The detected nuclear centroids are then used as graph nodes, enabling spatial graph construction at the patch level. III-B Patch-level Graph Within each image patch p p , the nuclei form a cell graph G p = ( V p , E p ) {G}_{p}=({V}_{p},{E}_{p}) representing local cellular organization, where V p {V}_{p} denotes the identified cells and E p {E}_{p} their spatial connections. Since cell-to-cell interactions cannot be reliably determined from the image alone, graph connectivity is defined with a proximity rule. Specifically, two cells i i and j j are connected if their Euclidean distance δ p ​ ( i , j ) \delta_{p}(i,j) is smaller than a predefined radius r p r_{p} . The adjacency matrix A p {A}_{p} is therefore defined as A p ​ ( i , j ) = { 1 , if ​ δ p ​ ( i , j ) < r p , 0 , otherwise . {A}_{p}(i,j)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\delta_{p}(i,j)<r_{p},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} (1) The resulting graph summarizes local tissue organization, including cell clustering, crowding, and isolation. An example of the patch-level cell graph is shown in Fig. 1 (a). We then extract 18 18 graph-based descriptors from each patch to quantify these local spatial patterns, as summarized in Table I [ 18 ] . For large GCN-based classifiers, repeatedly solving the inner problem and directly computing Hessian-related terms is impractical. We therefore adopt a DARTS-style one-step unrolled approximation [ 14 ] . Experiments IV-A Dataset We evaluate the proposed framework on three histopathology datasets: Dataset I, the Extended CRC dataset [ 20 ] ; Dataset II, the colon cancer dataset [ 23 ] ; and Dataset III, the melanoma dataset [ 11 ] . Dataset I, from the University of Warwick, contains 300 300 H&E-stained histology images extracted at 20 × 20\times magnification. The image resolutions are approximately 5000 × 7300 5000\times 7300 and 4548 × 7520 4548\times 7520 pixels. It contains three diagnostic categories: 120 120 normal, 120 120 low-grade, and 60 60 high-grade images. Dataset II contains 717 717 ROIs from Zhejiang University, including 355 355 cancer images and 362 362 normal images. The images are acquired at 40 × 40\times magnification, with an average spatial resolution of approximately 10000 × 10000 10000\times 10000 pixels. Dataset III, from NYU Langone Health, consists of 153 153 H&E-stained WSIs of metastatic melanoma tissue from 72 72 patient cases. The slides are digitized at either 20 × 20\times or 40 × 40\times , and all 40 × 40\times WSIs are downsampled to 20 × 20\times for consistency. The dataset includes 1072 1072 pathologist-annotated ROIs with an average resolution of 4190 × 4240 4190\times 4240 pixels, comprising 544 544 lymphocyte-rich and 528 528 tumor-rich regions. IV-B Implementation For patch extraction, we evaluate patch sizes of 512 × 512 512\times 512 , 768 × 768 768\times 768 , and 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 pixels with a fixed stride of 256 256 pixels. Based on the grid search, Datasets I and II are partitioned into overlapping patches of size 768 × 768 768\times 768 pixels, whereas Dataset III uses 512 × 512 512\times 512 -pixel patches. For Dataset I, we adopt the three-fold cross-validation scheme established in prior studies [ 21 , 20 ] . In each run, one fold is held out for testing, while the remaining two folds are partitioned into 80 % 80\% training and 20 % 20\% validation sets. Performance metrics are averaged across the three folds. For Dataset II, a subset of normal images is excluded because they contain too few cells to construct valid cell graphs. This leaves 669 669 images ( 355 355 cancer and 314 314 normal) for evaluation. After exclusion, we apply five-fold cross-validation, holding out one fold for testing and splitting the remaining four folds into 80 % 80\% training and 20 % 20\% validation sets. For Dataset III, we divide the ROIs into five patient-disjoint folds. Following a similar evaluation protocol, one fold is held out for testing while the remaining four are split into 80 % 80\% training and 20 % 20\% validation sets, with final results averaged across all five folds. The proposed framework is implemented in PyTorch. For patch-level cell graphs, we follow previous studies [ 21 , 25 ] and set the neighborhood threshold to r p = 64 r_{p}=64 pixels for all datasets. Both the classifier and the adjacency generator are optimized using Adam, with learning rates selected through grid search. For Dataset I, their learning rates are 10 − 3 10^{-3} and 10 − 2 10^{-2} , respectively; for Datasets II and III, they are 10 − 4 10^{-4} and 10 − 3 10^{-3} . The unrolled inner-step learning rate is set to 10 − 4 10^{-4} , and the finite-difference radius is set to 10 − 2 10^{-2} . We use a batch size of 20 20 for all experiments. The Gumbel-Sigmoid temperature is initialized at 1.0 1.0 to encourage early exploration, annealed by a factor of 0.98 0.98 , and bounded below at 0.1 0.1 to retain moderate stochasticity. Training is performed until validation convergence, and we use the same model architecture for training all three datasets. The combined classifier and adjacency generator contain approximately 0.86 ​ M 0.86M trainable parameters. The method combines fixed patch-level graphs with a learnable image-level graph to model local cellular organization and adaptive tissue interactions. By formulating image-level graph learning as a second-order bilevel problem, G 2 ∗ G_{2}^{*} -Net separates graph connectivity learning from classifier optimization and incorporates second-order hypergradients via a one-step unrolled approximation. Experiments on three histopathology datasets demonstrate strong and competitive performance against CNN- and GCN-based baselines.
