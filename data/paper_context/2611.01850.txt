Generative microscopy and biological evaluation. Generative models have been applied to microscopy synthesis and to modeling cellular phenotypes and responses to experimental perturbations [ 19 , 26 , 16 ] . Their evaluation has accordingly expanded beyond generic measures of visual fidelity and distributional similarity to morphological descriptors, phenotype distributions, perturbation recovery, and downstream biological tasks [ 20 , 4 , 26 ] . These approaches establish whether particular biological properties are preserved, but generally evaluate them individually. Dataset and biological representations. We use LIVECell [ 5 ] , a phase-contrast microscopy dataset comprising eight cell types (A172, BT-474, BV-2, Huh7, MCF7, SH-SY5Y, SkBr3, and SK-OV-3), more than 5,000 images acquired at 4-h intervals, and over 1.6 million annotated cells. Images are divided into six 260 × 236 260\times 236 tiles, padded to 260 × 256 260\times 256 , and center-cropped to 256 × 256 256\times 256 for training. 4 Results Figure 2 shows representative outputs of T2Joint across cell types and biological conditions. The model jointly produces microscopy images and structured instance masks from the requested phenotype descriptions, with the overlay illustrating their spatial correspondence. The requested and realized phenotype strata further provide a qualitative view of how the generated state reflects the conditioning signal. We next evaluate these relationships quantitatively, beginning with conventional generative performance before examining biological congruence across representations and empirical context. FID, precision, and recall expose a distributional cost associated with joint generation, but this cost is not proportionally reflected in the recoverability of cell type or acquisition time. Conversely, preservation of these global attributes cannot establish whether the richer biological state remains coherent across conditioning, image structure, instance annotations, and the empirical population. 4.2 Is biological information preserved consistently across perspectives? This asymmetry does not establish a causal or informational hierarchy, but shows that the generated population particularly fails to reproduce how morphology varies within its surrounding phenotype context. Because morphology is represented by a single area-to-convex-area descriptor, this recurrent failure may reflect limitations of the phenotype definition as well as the generator. Together, these results expose three patterns: consistent preservation across representations and population context for density and confluency, cross-perspective dissociation for NND, and recurrent disagreement for morphology. These distinctions cannot be recovered from any single metric or representation relationship. At the structural level, generated image–mask pairs achieve PQ = 0.459 =0.459 , compared with 0.577 0.577 for genuine LIVECell image–annotation pairs under the same independent evaluator, indicating substantial but imperfect instance-level correspondence. 4.3 What happens when control conflicts with empirical biology? The traversal experiment reveals an unexpected relationship between explicit phenotype control and empirical biological support (Fig. 4 ). As requested phenotype combinations become increasingly distant from states observed in matched real data, directional success of the manipulated attribute increases monotonically from 0.702 0.702 below the 80th distance percentile to 0.941 0.941 beyond the 99th percentile. Increasingly unsupported requests therefore do not make the target phenotype less responsive to conditioning; instead, it follows the requested direction more consistently. These results expose a tension that target adherence alone cannot capture. Under increasing biological conflict, the generator can follow the requested direction of the manipulated phenotype while reorganizing untargeted attributes toward states with greater empirical support. Evaluating the complete generated state is therefore necessary to determine whether successful conditioning preserves, disrupts, or reorganizes its surrounding biological context. 5 Discussion and Limitations Our experiments are limited to LIVECell, which was selected because its scale, multiple cell types, temporal sampling, and instance annotations enable conditioning, image appearance, instance structure, and population context to be evaluated within the same biological system. This allows a controlled multi-perspective analysis, but whether the observed patterns generalize to other microscopy datasets, modalities, or biological systems remains to be established. Biological congruence depends on which biological properties are represented and how their relationships are evaluated. Morphology illustrates this dependence: the recurrent disagreement observed across perspectives concerns the area-to-convex-area descriptor used in our experiments and does not imply that morphological information in general cannot be preserved. Likewise, the Prompt–Image, Prompt–Mask, and Image–Mask relationships used here reflect our experimental setting and should not be interpreted as a universal evaluation protocol. Our evaluation also relies on domain-specific proxies where synthetic ground truth is unavailable. microSAM representations and the LIVECell segmentation model provide independent references for information expressed in generated images, but remain imperfect estimators. We partially account for this limitation by training image probes only on real data and by calibrating image–mask phenotype disagreement against evaluator errors observed on genuine microscopy. Finally, empirical support should not be interpreted as biological validity. The conflict experiment measures proximity to phenotype configurations observed in matched real data, not whether a generated state is biologically correct or mechanistically feasible. The observed reorganization of untargeted phenotypes therefore motivates further investigation of learned biological dependencies rather than evidence that the generator understands or corrects biological state. More generally, biological congruence is intended as a relational evaluation principle whose representations, biological properties, and empirical references should be chosen according to the scientific information a generative model is expected to preserve. 6 Conclusion We introduced biological congruence as an evaluation principle for examining whether different representations of a generated sample provide coherent descriptions of its biological state and remain consistent with the empirical context they aim to represent. Using joint text-conditioned microscopy image and instance-mask generation as an experimental system, we showed that conventional generative quality, prompt adherence, cross-representation agreement, and population fidelity capture complementary rather than interchangeable properties. Across phenotypes, density and confluency show consistent preservation, NND reveals that image–mask agreement can coexist with weaker conditioning and population fidelity, and morphology exhibits recurrent disagreement across perspectives. Under increasing conflict with empirical support, target controllability can also increase while untargeted phenotypes reorganize toward states better represented in real data. These findings motivate evaluating biological generative models beyond aggregate quality or controllability metrics by considering the relationships among the biological information they are expected to preserve. Model and Training Details T2Joint uses a frozen RAE with a ViT-MAE-base encoder and a fine-tuned ViT-XL decoder. Microscopy images and structured instance masks are independently encoded into 768 × 16 × 16 768\times 16\times 16 latent representations using the same encoder and separate latent normalization statistics. The representations are concatenated channel-wise into a 1536 × 16 × 16 1536\times 16\times 16 joint latent, jointly denoised, and split before independent decoding. Image and mask denoising losses are equally weighted. The stage-2 denoiser uses DiTwDDTHead with encoder and decoder widths of 384 384 and 2048 2048 , 12 encoder blocks, 2 decoder blocks, and 6/16 attention heads. Text is encoded with a frozen E5-base-v2 encoder and projected to the denoiser representation. Token-level text cross-attention is applied every other encoder block, resulting in six cross-attention layers, with no additional text cross-attention in the decoder. Cross-attention residual gates are initialized to 0.1 0.1 . The class-conditioning pathway is disabled so that conditioning is provided exclusively through text.
