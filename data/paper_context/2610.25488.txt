II-A Efficient Medical Image Segmentation Architectures Encoder design is a central consideration in medical image segmentation architectures [ 18 , 19 ] . Traditional convolutional networks [ 20 , 5 , 2 ] aggregate local context, whereas Transformer-based models [ 21 , 22 ] and state-space models [ 4 , 23 ] capture long-range dependencies. Hybrid designs, including TransUNet [ 3 ] , nnWNet3D [ 24 ] , CROWn [ 25 ] , and PnPNet [ 26 ] , integrate convolutional processing with broader contextual interactions. Efficiency-oriented designs, such as 3D UNETR++ [ 7 ] , LightM-UNet [ 10 ] , and TfeNet [ 27 ] , reduce computational requirements through lightweight feature-extraction and mixing modules. However, reducing the cost of individual blocks does not by itself establish whether their spatial-mixing operations are necessary at each network stage. Another line of research examines the contribution of the token mixer to model performance. MetaFormer [ 8 ] shows that the general Transformer architecture, rather than the specific token mixer, accounts for much of the performance: pooling, random, or even identity mixers remain competitive on image classification. SlimFormer [ 17 ] reaches a similar conclusion for 3D medical image segmentation, where replacing the token mixer changes accuracy only slightly. However, comparisons between separately trained networks do not directly quantify the contribution of token mixing at individual stages within a fixed trained model. Where operators do vary across depth, their placement follows design heuristics: the hybrid MetaFormer variants and CoAtNet [ 28 ] place pooling or convolution before attention, and SwinUNETR-V2 [ 22 ] adds stage-wise convolutions to a Swin Transformer encoder. III-C Micro Architecture of Token Mixers The micro design sets the gates g b g_{b} in ( 1 ) stage by stage and specifies the Selective Mixing (SM) and Augmented Mixing (AM) mixers. Token mixing in S1 adds regional context to high-resolution local features, whereas bottleneck mixing relates distant locations in the input volume. S1 context propagates through the encoder, and decoder fusion combines the skip features of S2 and S3 with bottleneck context. These complementary paths motivate the expectation of limited additional mixer contributions in S2 and S3. Halving each spatial dimension reduces the token count eightfold between stages, so S1 favors lightweight mixing with cost linear in token count, whereas the bottleneck can afford stronger remote interaction. IV-B Validity of CKA and ARD as Design Diagnostics Fig. IV-B supports using CKA and ARD as complementary diagnostics to guide subsequent macro and micro design analyses. CKA assesses stage-level redundancy, whereas ARD assesses the contribution of each stage’s token mixers. Higher CKA corresponds to smaller Dice decreases after stage removal, whereas higher ARD corresponds to larger decreases after mixer bypass. Stage removal skips an entire stage; mixer bypass disables only its token mixers, preserving residual paths and channel MLPs. Removing any encoder stage substantially reduces Dice, S1-S3 all show large Dice decreases following stage removal, despite differences in CKA. While BN has substantially higher CKA and a smaller Dice decrease. In contrast, mixer bypass produce the highest ARD and the largest Dice decrease at the bottleneck and the smallest at S2/S3. S1 shows negligible Dice loss, but its ARD exceeds those of S2/S3 by more than an order of magnitude. An encoder stage can therefore remain essential even when its token mixers contribute little. Aggregate Dice can mask losses on challenging classes through gains on easier ones. We therefore analyze the gallbladder and duodenum, with the pancreas and liver as references (Fig. ). We examine the effects of stage-wise token-mixer bypass from two complementary perspectives: changes in segmentation results, shown by newly incorrect predictions alongside ARD residual heatmaps, and changes in feature space, visualized using PaCMAP [ 48 ] embeddings of the bypassed features. Bypassing the bottleneck mixer produces widespread normalized ARD residuals ( D ARD ≈ 0.1 D_{\mathrm{ARD}}\approx 0.1 ), but new errors concentrate in challenging classes: 2,680 gallbladder pixels are misclassified as background and 709 duodenum pixels as stomach, whereas the liver remains largely correct. S1 bypass produces scattered errors ( D ARD ≈ 10 − 6 D_{\mathrm{ARD}}\approx 10^{-6} ), whereas S2/S3 bypass yields at most 18 newly misclassified pixels in one slice ( D ARD ≈ 10 − 10 D_{\mathrm{ARD}}\approx 10^{-10} ). PaCMAP [ 48 ] shows the same ordering: misclassified tokens cluster densely after bypassing the bottleneck mixer, scatter after S1 bypass, and are nearly absent after S2/S3 bypass. The compared networks differ in the number and composition of blocks per stage, so we compare them at the shared stage granularity. Fig. IV-C1 reports stage-averaged CKA, defined as the mean whole-stage input–output CKA across the encoder stages and bottleneck, excluding the stem. Pruning lowers stage-averaged CKA in all three baselines, with the largest reduction in nnUNet and smaller changes in TransUNet and SlimFormer. Its stage-averaged CKA of 0.441 is lower than those of unpruned TransUNet (0.463), nnUNet (0.517), and SlimFormer (0.594). It requires 189.3 GFLOPs, 4.7% fewer than the next-lowest model on this axis, SlimFormer (198.6 GFLOPs), while using 65.6% fewer parameters and achieving a mean Dice 3.81 pp higher (87.84% versus 84.03%). Its peak memory of 0.43 GB is 29.5% lower than that of nnUNet (0.61 GB), the next-lowest model. For latency and energy consumption, nnUNet ranks first (7.33 ms and 1.93 J), followed by SlimFormer++ (12.99 ms and 2.08 J per volume), although nnUNet requires 1,065.8 GFLOPs, 5.6 times that of SlimFormer++. The latency gap reflects memory-access overhead in the SM scan and AM remote gathers, whose kernels are less optimized than dense cuDNN convolutions. Fused kernels for scan and gather operations may help translate lower GFLOPs into faster inference. V Conclusion Redundancy analysis guided block removal and stage-wise token-mixer allocation for 3D medical image segmentation. CKA identified redundant blocks and informed the hybrid macro architecture, whereas ARD located the strongest mixer contribution at the bottleneck and separated S1 from S2/S3 across the tested mixers, motivating AM at the bottleneck, SM in S1, and residual channel MLPs in S2/S3. Across eight public CT, MRI, and ultrasound datasets, SlimFormer++ achieved the highest mean Dice (87.84%) with 5.98 million parameters. Its latency still exceeds that of nnUNet despite lower GFLOPs (Section IV-F ); future work will incorporate measured latency into the design criterion and develop hardware-efficient mixer kernels.
