3 Method 3.1 Axis-Decoupled Block FAD-ViT uses a three-stage convolutional-tokenization hierarchy but, unlike spatial and dual-axis backbones, assigns spatial extraction and global mixing to two purpose-matched , disjoint operators rather than folding both into spatial attention. At stage s s , let the feature map be 𝐗 ( s ) ∈ ℝ B × H s × W s × C s \mathbf{X}^{(s)}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H_{s}\times W_{s}\times C_{s}} , reshaped to tokens 𝐗 tok ( s ) ∈ ℝ B × N s × C s \mathbf{X}^{(s)}_{\mathrm{tok}}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times N_{s}\times C_{s}} with N s = H s ​ W s N_{s}=H_{s}W_{s} . Each block applies the two decoupled operations below; for the controlled study we also form a spatial-axis counterpart by swapping only the global operator while holding tokenization, capacity, and schedule fixed. Spatial Feature Extraction (SFE). A depthwise convolution refines local structure while preserving the spatial grid, 𝐔 ( s ) = DWConv ⁡ ( 𝐗 ( s ) ) , \mathbf{U}^{(s)}=\mathrm{DWConv}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(s)}\big), (1) with stage-dependent kernel and stride. The convolution is depthwise by design, not for efficiency : because it applies an independent kernel per channel, all spatial mixing is confined to this branch and no channels are combined here, so spatial modeling is cleanly separated from feature modeling. Local spatial structure is thus routed by SFE and only by SFE, which keeps spatial contiguity for dense prediction and avoids premature spatial–feature entanglement. Feature Self-Attention (FSA). Global interaction runs along the feature axis. Queries, keys, and values are produced by a depthwise projection that acts on each channel independently, so channels are never linearly combined before attention; the resulting affinity is therefore computed between unmixed channel identities, in contrast to the pointwise (channel-mixing) query/key/value projections of cross-covariance attention [ 10 ] . The tensor is then flattened and transposed from token layout to feature layout, B × C s × H s × W s → flatten B × N s × C s → transpose B × C s × N s , B{\times}C_{s}{\times}H_{s}{\times}W_{s}\;\xrightarrow{\text{flatten}}\;B{\times}N_{s}{\times}C_{s}\;\xrightarrow{\text{transpose}}\;B{\times}C_{s}{\times}N_{s}, (2) so that the C s C_{s} channels become the tokens and the N s N_{s} spatial positions become the feature dimension. Each channel token is thus the flattened H s × W s H_{s}{\times}W_{s} response map of a single channel, i.e. a complete view of the image, so the full 2D layout is carried inside every token and is restored exactly when the output is transposed back; attention reweights whole-image channel responses and never collapses spatial structure. Single-head attention over this layout, FSA ⁡ ( 𝐐 , 𝐊 , 𝐕 ) = Softmax ⁡ ( 𝐐𝐊 ⊤ N s ) ​ 𝐕 , 𝐐 , 𝐊 , 𝐕 ∈ ℝ B × C s × N s , \mathrm{FSA}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\mathrm{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{N_{s}}}\right)\mathbf{V},\qquad\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C_{s}\times N_{s}}, (3) produces a C s × C s C_{s}{\times}C_{s} feature-affinity map, in contrast to the N s × N s N_{s}{\times}N_{s} token-affinity map of spatial attention. The map recombines the per-channel value maps, so each output token is a data-dependent reweighting of whole-image channel responses rather than of spatial locations. The result is transposed back to B × N s × C s B{\times}N_{s}{\times}C_{s} and updated with residual connection and feed-forward mixing. Because a feature token already aggregates the full spatial field, partitioning it across attention subspaces buys little and a single head suffices, as in the channel-token single-head attention of DaViT [ 7 ] and the single-head macro design of SHViT [ 28 ] ; unlike DaViT’s grouped variant we keep one global head over the full channel set. Together SFE and FSA partition the two forms of interaction into disjoint operators: SFE mixes space but not channels, FSA mixes channels but does not collapse space, and no operator in the block does both. This clean separation is what makes the global axis a variable we can set and study in isolation. 3.2 Staged Backbone, Spatial Tail, and Complexity The backbone stacks three stages with progressive downsampling and channel expansion ( H 1 , W 1 , C 1 ) → ( H 2 , W 2 , C 2 ) → ( H 3 , W 3 , C 3 ) (H_{1},W_{1},C_{1})\!\to\!(H_{2},W_{2},C_{2})\!\to\!(H_{3},W_{3},C_{3}) , C 1 < C 2 < C 3 C_{1}{<}C_{2}{<}C_{3} . The final stage appends an optional, lightweight spatial-attention tail (a few multi-head spatial-token blocks) on top of the feature-axis backbone. This tail is a minor refinement rather than a co-equal mixer; Sec. 6 shows that removing it costs only ≈ \approx 1 Dice, so feature-axis attention performs the overwhelming majority of global mixing and the backbone remains channel-primary by design. The two axes have complementary cost. Spatial attention scales as 𝒪 ⁡ ( N s 2 ​ C s ) \mathcal{O}(N_{s}^{2}C_{s}) , quadratic in the token count N s = H s ​ W s N_{s}=H_{s}W_{s} and therefore in input resolution; feature attention scales as 𝒪 ⁡ ( C s 2 ​ N s ) \mathcal{O}(C_{s}^{2}N_{s}) , quadratic in width C s C_{s} but only linear in N s N_{s} . The feature-axis cost per position is thus fixed by architecture and independent of resolution, whereas the spatial-axis cost grows quadratically once tokens outnumber channels ( N s > C s N_{s}>C_{s} ). At the moderate token counts of this backbone ( N s ≈ C s N_{s}\!\approx\!C_{s} at deep stages) the two are comparable in FLOPs; the feature axis’s decisive advantage here is parametric: FAD-ViT reaches its transfer accuracy with 23.1 23.1 M parameters against 90.5 ​ – ​ 90.6 90.5\text{--}90.6 M for the ViT/DINOv2 decoders ( Sec. 5 ), while its resolution-independent global cost avoids the spatial axis’s blow-up at the high native resolutions common in medical acquisition. For segmentation we attach an identical U-Net decoder to the staged features across all encoders, so that any downstream difference is attributable to the encoder rather than the decoder. 4 Experimental Setup Figure 2 : Study design. Under a fixed protocol (convolutional tokenization, matched ∼ \sim 23M-parameter capacity, shared U-Net / linear-head decoders, identical schedule, and ImageNet-1K pretraining with a from-scratch control) we vary only the global-mixing operator across four backbone families: a spatial-axis baseline (ViT/DINOv2), two channel-hybrids (XCiT, DaViT), and the strictly decoupled feature axis. Transfer is evaluated on classification, segmentation, and low-data probing and attributed with transfer-gap, CKA, modality, and multi-seed analyses. Protocol. To isolate encoder representation quality we fix everything downstream: identical ImageNet-1K pretraining (except DINOv2, LVD-142M [ 17 ] ), identical optimizer, schedule, augmentation, input resolution, and, for segmentation, an identical U-Net decoder across encoders ( Fig. 2 ). This controls for pipeline effects so that differences reflect the backbone. Datasets. We evaluate on 23 medical datasets/tasks: (i) classification on 10 MedMNIST-2D datasets [ 26 ] plus BUSI [ 1 ] and dataset B [ 27 ] breast ultrasound; (ii) segmentation on BUSI lesion and 10 MSD-derived 2D tasks [ 3 ] ; (iii) low-data linear probing on 8 MedMNIST datasets at 1%, 10%, and 100% labels. For the MSD-derived 2D tasks the 70 / 15 / 15 70/15/15 split is made at the volume (patient) level before slicing, so all slices of a volume stay in one partition and no patient spans splits; BUSI is split at the image–mask-pair level. Baselines. We compare against spatial-attention ViTs (ViT-S/16, ViT-B), convolutional ViT (CvT-13 [ 23 ] ), a convolutional U-Net encoder (ResNet50), other CNNs (VGG16), a state-space model (Vim-S), and a large self-supervised encoder (DINOv2-B/14 [ 17 ] ). For the axis study specifically we compare feature-axis (ours) against a matched-capacity spatial ViT (ViT-S/16-UNet, 22.9 22.9 M) and against the canonical channel-axis and dual-axis designs XCiT [ 10 ] and DaViT [ 7 ] within the same protocol. Implementation. Classification models are fine-tuned end-to-end; segmentation encoders share a U-Net decoder and are fine-tuned; linear probing freezes the backbone and trains a linear head. Training uses AdamW with cosine scheduling and mixed precision; input is 224 × 224 224{\times}224 for classification and 256 × 256 256{\times}256 for segmentation. We report Accuracy/AUC (classification), Dice/mIoU/HD95 (segmentation), and linear-probe accuracy at each label fraction. Full preprocessing per modality (intensity windowing, resampling, normalization) and all hyperparameters are given in the supplementary material. We transfer the two closest channel-aware designs, XCiT [ 10 ] and DaViT [ 7 ] , under the identical U-Net protocol. In both the axes stay entangled (pointwise Q / K / V Q/K/V pre-mix channels; spatial mixing persists), so pretraining cannot be attributed to one axis; FAD-ViT, with depthwise-only Q / K / V Q/K/V and no auxiliary spatial modules, is the minimal design that removes this confound. All three channel-aware backbones beat the pure spatial-axis ViT-UNet on the held-out ultrasound test by + 22 +22 to + 25 +25 Dice ( Tab. 5 ; extended to all eleven tasks in the supplement), so feature-axis interaction drives the gain. The three are close (within 2.6 2.6 Dice) yet invert the natural-image ordering: XCiT and DaViT match or beat FAD-ViT on ADE20K but only tie it under medical transfer, where FAD-ViT reaches the strongest coupled design’s accuracy ( 77.7 77.7 vs. 77.8 77.8 Dice) at the fewest parameters ( 23.1 23.1 vs. 27.1 27.1 and 34.4 34.4 M). That FAD-ViT matches rather than beats these coupled designs supports our thesis: the benefit comes from the shared feature axis, not from any one architecture. FAD-ViT is simply its efficient, attributable endpoint, transferring at least as well as heavier coupled designs without their machinery. Moving global interaction from spatial to feature tokens converts identical ImageNet pretraining into several times larger downstream gains at matched capacity, outperforming spatial backbones across diverse segmentation tasks at a quarter of the parameters while reusing more pretrained early and mid-level structure, the signature of a transfer prior rather than a capacity effect. The gain is largest on low-contrast ultrasound and smaller but consistent on MRI and CT. Feature-centric global attention is thus a cheap, principled design choice for medical backbones; natural next steps are stronger pipelines (nnU-Net), other architecture families, and self-supervised pretraining.
