Framework Overview The Morph-ISR framework, as illustrated in Figure. 2 (a), aims to establish a continuous and structure-preserving mapping between the LR observation I l ​ r ∈ ℝ B × 3 × W l ​ r × H l ​ r I_{lr}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 3\times W_{lr}\times H_{lr}} and the high-fidelity output I s ​ r ∈ ℝ B × 3 × W h ​ r × H h ​ r I_{sr}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 3\times W_{hr}\times H_{hr}} . Here, B B denotes the batch size, the three channels correspond to RGB, and ( W l ​ r , H l ​ r ) (W_{lr},H_{lr}) and ( W h ​ r , H h ​ r ) (W_{hr},H_{hr}) denote the LR and HR spatial dimensions, respectively. We first obtain the bicubic baseline I u ​ p ∈ ℝ B × 3 × W h ​ r × H h ​ r I_{up}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 3\times W_{hr}\times H_{hr}} by upsampling I l ​ r I_{lr} with scale s s . The reconstruction process begins by projecting I l ​ r I_{lr} into a latent representation f ∈ ℝ B × C × W l ​ r × H l ​ r f\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C\times W_{lr}\times H_{lr}} , where C C denotes the number of feature channels. The local encoder is a lightweight feature extractor composed of stacked 3 × 3 3\times 3 convolutional layers with GELU activations. To mitigate fixed-grid artifacts, SR is formulated as a continuous coordinate query task. Let x h ​ r = { x h ​ r , i } i = 1 N ∈ ℝ N × 2 x_{hr}=\{x_{hr,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 2} denote the HR query grid, where x h ​ r , i = ( u i , v i ) x_{hr,i}=(u_{i},v_{i}) , u i u_{i} and v i v_{i} denote its horizontal and vertical coordinates, respectively, and N = W h ​ r × H h ​ r N=W_{hr}\times H_{hr} is the number of HR queries. Each query coordinate x h ​ r , i x_{hr,i} is projected back into the LR feature space, yielding implicit position-aware features that establish a precise geometric linkage between the discrete LR representation and the continuous HR domain. This process resolves three key geometric descriptors: the continuous center coordinate x c , i x_{c,i} , the sub-pixel phase shift x s ​ p , i x_{sp,i} , and the global normalized coordinate x n ​ c , i x_{nc,i} for Fourier encoding (Li et al. 2021 ) , whose derivation is given in Eq. ( 1 ): { x c , i = ( x h ​ r , i + 0.5 ) / s − 0.5 , x s ​ p , i = x c , i − ⌊ x c , i ⌋ , x n ​ c , i = 2 ⋅ x h ​ r , i + 0.5 ( W h ​ r , H h ​ r ) − 1 . \begin{cases}x_{c,i}=(x_{hr,i}+0.5)/s-0.5,\\ x_{sp,i}=x_{c,i}-\lfloor x_{c,i}\rfloor,\\ x_{nc,i}=2\cdot\frac{x_{hr,i}+0.5}{(W_{hr},H_{hr})}-1.\end{cases} (1) where s s denotes the upsampling scale factor, set to 4 by default, and ⌊ ⋅ ⌋ \lfloor\cdot\rfloor denotes the floor operation. The Fourier positional feature is defined as x p ​ e , i = γ ​ ( x n ​ c , i ) ∈ ℝ P x_{pe,i}=\gamma(x_{nc,i})\in\mathbb{R}^{P} , where γ ​ ( ⋅ ) \gamma(\cdot) denotes Fourier positional encoding and P P is its output dimension. Instead of performing direct RGB regression, the IPKG module first reconstructs each query point through spatially adaptive convolution kernels. To synthesize the image content, each query index i ∈ { 1 , … , N } i\in\{1,\dots,N\} is traversed. For each projected coordinate x c , i x_{c,i} , the local K × K K\times K pixel neighborhood vector V i ∈ ℝ K 2 × 3 V_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{2}\times 3} is retrieved from I l ​ r I_{lr} , where K K denotes the kernel size used for local neighborhood filtering. The predicted kernel weights w i ∈ ℝ K 2 w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{2}} are then applied to perform local filtering. As shown in Figure. 2 , this operation generates a base prediction p i p_{i} . To explicitly encourage the recovery of high-frequency details, we model this prediction as a residual correction r i r_{i} relative to the bicubic baseline value I u ​ p ​ ( y i ) I_{up}(y_{i}) , where y i y_{i} denotes the HR-grid location corresponding to query coordinate x h ​ r , i x_{hr,i} when indexing I u ​ p I_{up} . Implicit Position-aware Kernel Generator Module To address the geometric discontinuity challenge inherent in cross-scale reconstruction, the IPKG module is established as a gated multi-head kernel generator that dynamically synthesizes spatially adaptive resampling kernels for each continuous query coordinate. For the i i -th query point, IPKG takes three position-aware inputs defined in the previous subsection: the center feature f c ​ e ​ n , i f_{cen,i} sampled from the LR latent feature map at x c , i x_{c,i} , the Fourier positional feature x p ​ e , i = γ ​ ( x n ​ c , i ) x_{pe,i}=\gamma(x_{nc,i}) , and the sub-pixel phase shift x s ​ p , i x_{sp,i} . These features jointly describe local texture, global coordinate position, and sub-pixel alignment. To accommodate the morphological diversity of pathological tissue, ranging from dense chromatin to sparse stroma, a gated multi-head architecture is adopted. A shared lightweight MLP Φ ​ ( ⋅ ) \Phi(\cdot) maps the composite query feature v q , i ∈ ℝ C + P + 2 v_{q,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{C+P+2} into N h N_{h} pairs of raw kernel and gate logits, where N h N_{h} denotes the number of kernel heads. The kernel logits 𝒲 r ​ a ​ w , i ( h ) ∈ ℝ K 2 \mathcal{W}_{raw,i}^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{2}} parameterize the K × K K\times K filter of the h h -th head, while 𝒢 r ​ a ​ w , i ( h ) ∈ ℝ \mathcal{G}_{raw,i}^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R} represents its confidence for the current query. Experiments Experiment Settings Datasets and Implementation: The experiments use 150 H&E-stained TCGA WSIs, comprising 50 randomly selected cases from each of the LUAD, KIRC, and LIHC cohorts (Weinstein et al. 2013 ) . These WSIs are divided into mutually exclusive training, validation, and test sets using a 7:1:2 ratio. CLAM (Lu et al. 2021 ) further extracts 34,495 HR patches of 512 × 512 512\times 512 pixels at 40 × 40\times magnification, which are bicubically downsampled by 4 × 4\times to produce paired 128 × 128 128\times 128 LR inputs. External generalization is assessed by directly applying the TCGA-trained model to the independent SurGen cohort of 46 colorectal cancer patients (Myles et al. 2025 ) , which is excluded from training and model selection but undergoes the same preprocessing. Under this experimental protocol, the framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU for 120 epochs. Optimization uses AdamW with a batch size of 4, a learning rate of 3 × 10 − 4 3\times 10^{-4} , and a weight decay of 10 − 4 10^{-4} . Automatic mixed precision and gradient clipping at 1.0 are applied throughout training. Meanwhile, the gating and kernel temperatures ( τ 1 , τ 2 ) (\tau_{1},\tau_{2}) are progressively annealed from ( 4.0 , 1.0 ) (4.0,1.0) to ( 1.2 , 0.6 ) (1.2,0.6) to stabilize dynamic kernel learning. Further details regarding the datasets and implementation are provided in Supplementary Appendix A and B. Competing Methods and Metrics: To comprehensively evaluate Morph-ISR, the baselines comprise Bicubic interpolation, deterministic regression methods SHISRCNet (Xie et al. 2023 ) and SwinIR (Liang et al. 2021 ) , generative reconstruction methods ESRGAN (Wang et al. 2018 ) , SinSR (Wang et al. 2024 ) , STAR-RL (Chen et al. 2024 ) , UPSR (Zhang et al. 2025 ) , and SuperDiff (Xu et al. 2025 ) , and the continuous SR method LIIF (Chen et al. 2021 ) . For quantitative validation, LPIPS and ST-LPIPS (Zhang et al. 2018 ) assess perceptual and structural consistency, while PSNR and SSIM (Wang et al. 2004 ) measure full-reference fidelity. Complementing the accuracy evaluation, edge-oriented deployment efficiency is characterized by parameters, FLOPs, throughput, and inference jitter following (Pang et al. 2025 ) . To determine whether the SR preserves pathology-relevant structures, downstream nuclear analysis is further conducted on representative images from TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-KIRC, TCGA-LIHC, and SurGen. A fixed H&E Cellpose cyto3 segmentation pipeline (Stringer and Pachitariu 2025 ) , independent of the HoVer-Net supervision employed in MFP, generates nuclear instances from SR and the corresponding HR images. Using the HR-derived segmentations as references, Nuclear Mask Dice and Detection F1 evaluate segmentation consistency, while Circularity and Eccentricity Fidelity quantify morphological preservation. This inference-only analysis requires no additional training or parameter updates. Further dataset, implementation, and computational details are provided in Supplementary Appendix C. Results and Discussion Quantitative Analysis of SR : Table 1 shows that Morph-ISR achieves the best LPIPS and ST-LPIPS across all three TCGA cohorts, with the largest margins on TCGA-LUAD: reductions of 38.37% and 39.55% relative to the second-best method, STAR-RL. These gains hold across deterministic and generative baselines as well as the continuous SR method LIIF, indicating robustness across reconstruction formulations. Figure 3 shows the same trend. Bicubic interpolation and deterministic baselines blur nuclear boundaries and attenuate stromal textures, while several generative methods produce less stable local morphology. In contrast, Morph-ISR better preserves nuclear contours, chromatin patterns, and fine tissue structures without conspicuous artificial textures. The independent SurGen evaluation further supports external-domain generalization. Morph-ISR obtains the best LPIPS and ST-LPIPS scores of 0.1721 and 0.1724, surpassing the second-best UPSR by 0.12% and 0.63%, respectively. It also achieves the best PSNR across the three TCGA cohorts, the best SSIM on TCGA-LUAD and TCGA-KIRC, and the second-best SSIM on TCGA-LIHC, while ranking second in both metrics on SurGen. Although Bicubic leads these pixel metrics on SurGen, its substantially poorer perceptual scores reveal the limitations of pixel fidelity alone. Morph-ISR further uses 0.8291 M parameters and 189.2 G total FLOPs, achieves the highest throughput of 11.89 FPS, and records 7.153 ms inference jitter, supporting edge-oriented deployment. Overall, Morph-ISR combines perceptual and structural fidelity with external generalization and deployment efficiency. Experiment Settings Datasets and Implementation: The experiments use 150 H&E-stained TCGA WSIs, comprising 50 randomly selected cases from each of the LUAD, KIRC, and LIHC cohorts (Weinstein et al. 2013 ) . These WSIs are divided into mutually exclusive training, validation, and test sets using a 7:1:2 ratio. CLAM (Lu et al. 2021 ) further extracts 34,495 HR patches of 512 × 512 512\times 512 pixels at 40 × 40\times magnification, which are bicubically downsampled by 4 × 4\times to produce paired 128 × 128 128\times 128 LR inputs. External generalization is assessed by directly applying the TCGA-trained model to the independent SurGen cohort of 46 colorectal cancer patients (Myles et al. 2025 ) , which is excluded from training and model selection but undergoes the same preprocessing. Under this experimental protocol, the framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU for 120 epochs. Optimization uses AdamW with a batch size of 4, a learning rate of 3 × 10 − 4 3\times 10^{-4} , and a weight decay of 10 − 4 10^{-4} . Automatic mixed precision and gradient clipping at 1.0 are applied throughout training. Meanwhile, the gating and kernel temperatures ( τ 1 , τ 2 ) (\tau_{1},\tau_{2}) are progressively annealed from ( 4.0 , 1.0 ) (4.0,1.0) to ( 1.2 , 0.6 ) (1.2,0.6) to stabilize dynamic kernel learning. Further details regarding the datasets and implementation are provided in Supplementary Appendix A and B. Competing Methods and Metrics: To comprehensively evaluate Morph-ISR, the baselines comprise Bicubic interpolation, deterministic regression methods SHISRCNet (Xie et al. 2023 ) and SwinIR (Liang et al. 2021 ) , generative reconstruction methods ESRGAN (Wang et al. 2018 ) , SinSR (Wang et al. 2024 ) , STAR-RL (Chen et al. 2024 ) , UPSR (Zhang et al. 2025 ) , and SuperDiff (Xu et al. 2025 ) , and the continuous SR method LIIF (Chen et al. 2021 ) . For quantitative validation, LPIPS and ST-LPIPS (Zhang et al. 2018 ) assess perceptual and structural consistency, while PSNR and SSIM (Wang et al. 2004 ) measure full-reference fidelity. Complementing the accuracy evaluation, edge-oriented deployment efficiency is characterized by parameters, FLOPs, throughput, and inference jitter following (Pang et al. 2025 ) . To determine whether the SR preserves pathology-relevant structures, downstream nuclear analysis is further conducted on representative images from TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-KIRC, TCGA-LIHC, and SurGen. A fixed H&E Cellpose cyto3 segmentation pipeline (Stringer and Pachitariu 2025 ) , independent of the HoVer-Net supervision employed in MFP, generates nuclear instances from SR and the corresponding HR images. Using the HR-derived segmentations as references, Nuclear Mask Dice and Detection F1 evaluate segmentation consistency, while Circularity and Eccentricity Fidelity quantify morphological preservation. This inference-only analysis requires no additional training or parameter updates. Further dataset, implementation, and computational details are provided in Supplementary Appendix C. Results and Discussion Quantitative Analysis of SR : Table 1 shows that Morph-ISR achieves the best LPIPS and ST-LPIPS across all three TCGA cohorts, with the largest margins on TCGA-LUAD: reductions of 38.37% and 39.55% relative to the second-best method, STAR-RL. These gains hold across deterministic and generative baselines as well as the continuous SR method LIIF, indicating robustness across reconstruction formulations. Figure 3 shows the same trend. Bicubic interpolation and deterministic baselines blur nuclear boundaries and attenuate stromal textures, while several generative methods produce less stable local morphology. In contrast, Morph-ISR better preserves nuclear contours, chromatin patterns, and fine tissue structures without conspicuous artificial textures. The independent SurGen evaluation further supports external-domain generalization. Morph-ISR obtains the best LPIPS and ST-LPIPS scores of 0.1721 and 0.1724, surpassing the second-best UPSR by 0.12% and 0.63%, respectively. It also achieves the best PSNR across the three TCGA cohorts, the best SSIM on TCGA-LUAD and TCGA-KIRC, and the second-best SSIM on TCGA-LIHC, while ranking second in both metrics on SurGen. Although Bicubic leads these pixel metrics on SurGen, its substantially poorer perceptual scores reveal the limitations of pixel fidelity alone. Morph-ISR further uses 0.8291 M parameters and 189.2 G total FLOPs, achieves the highest throughput of 11.89 FPS, and records 7.153 ms inference jitter, supporting edge-oriented deployment. Overall, Morph-ISR combines perceptual and structural fidelity with external generalization and deployment efficiency. Conclusion This work presents Morph-ISR, a morphology-aware implicit framework balancing pixel-level accuracy and structural fidelity in pathological image SR. In this framework, IPKG formulates reconstruction as continuous coordinate querying and generates spatially adaptive kernels for heterogeneous tissue structures, while the training-only MFP imposes region- and boundary-aware constraints without inference overhead. Evaluations on three TCGA cohorts show leading perceptual and structural fidelity alongside strong pixel-level accuracy. Zero-shot results on SurGen support cross-dataset generalization, while downstream nuclear analysis supports improved segmentation consistency and morphological preservation. Compact parameterization and favorable throughput further support efficient deployment in resource-constrained settings.
