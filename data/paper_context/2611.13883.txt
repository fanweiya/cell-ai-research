Methods Ethical considerations The patient sample collection was approved by the Bioethical Committee of Politechnika Bydgoska in Bydgoszcz, Poland (permit WM.0025.13.2026KB). Given the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified prostate specimens, the ethics committee did not require informed consent. Access to and processing of the data at Karolinska Institutet were approved by the Swedish Ethical Review Authority (permits 2019-05220, 2025-07557-02). The samples were de-identified before transfer to Karolinska Institutet. Study design This study involves retrospective sample collection of prostate core-needle biopsies, laboratory slide preparation, whole-slide scanning at three stages of tissue preparation and pathologist annotation on the stained image, as summarized in Figure 1 . Figure 1: Overview of the sample collection pipeline. Laboratory slide processing and scanning Formalin-fixed tissue specimens were processed and embedded in paraffin. In some cases, Safranin O dye was added during paraffin embedding to improve the tissue visibility and facilitate sectioning. The amount and concentration of the dye were not standardized, which resulted in variation of the dye intensity and the raw tissue appearance (Figure 2 ). The resulting formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks were sectioned at a thickness of 3.5 µm and mounted onto glass slides. After sectioning and initial drying, the slides were scanned prior to deparaffinization, generating WSIs. These images represented raw tissue sections prior to any chemical processing. Figure 2: Variation in the appearance of the raw tissue sections due to Safranin O application during paraffin embedding. a) no dye, b) faint pink tint, c) pink-brownish tint, d) intense dye color. Following the initial scan, the slides were incubated in a drying oven at 60 °C for 30 minutes and subsequently deparaffinized in two consecutive xylene baths for 5 minutes each. Afterwards, the slides were allowed to air-dry for approximately 3 minutes to remove residual xylene and were rescanned to generate deparaffinized WSIs. The slides were subsequently subjected to routine automated H&E staining using a Tissue-Tek Prisma ® Plus automated slide stainer and Tissue-Tek ® H&E Staining Kit (Sakura Finetek). The staining procedure was performed according to the validated Sakura H&E staining protocol and comprised rehydration, hematoxylin staining, differentiation, bluing, eosin counterstaining, dehydration, clearing, and coverslipping. Following H&E staining and coverslipping, the slides were scanned for a third time to obtain the corresponding H&E-stained WSIs. All slide scans at the three processing stages were generated using the VENTANA DP 600 Slide Scanner (Roche Diagnostics; serial no. DP6000236) at 40× magnification with a resolution of 0.25 µm/pixel and stored in a TIFF-based format. An example of scans resulting from one glass slide is presented in Figure 3 . Figure 3: Example of a glass slide scanned at three stages of tissue processing: raw, deparaffinized, H&E-stained. Whole-slide thumbnails and regions at 10x magnification are shown for each stage. The dataset includes rare modalities such as raw unstained tissue brightfield images, for which very little public data is available and thus for which there is less established intuition about image quality. Assessment of the scan quality, such as identification of the out-of-focus regions, is more challenging for these images due to low contrast and more unconventional tissue appearance compared to established H&E-stained images. Moreover, the quality of unstained tissue imaging may be sensitive to the scanner vendor, scanner model, and acquisition settings, as these systems can be optimized for conventionally stained histology slides. Each scan was therefore assigned a quality label through visual inspection. The labels represent a subjective, descriptive assessment and are reported both to facilitate comparison of image quality across datasets and scanners and to raise awareness of potential scanner-specific limitations when imaging unstained tissue. We hope that reporting these observations will contribute to a broader discussion within the stain-free imaging community and help establish more systematic approaches to evaluating image quality for this emerging modality. Further details on the quality and dye labels are provided in the Data records section. Reference standard protocol The digitized slides were reviewed by M.Z., a certified uropathologist from Prof. Franciszek Łukaszczyk Oncology Centre in Bydgoszcz with 5 years of diagnostic experience. Each H&E-stained whole-slide image was assessed using Roche VENTANA uPath Digital Pathology software and assigned a slide-level Gleason score. Slides without evidence of malignancy were labeled as benign (0+0). The Gleason scores were then mapped to the corresponding ISUP Grade Groups. No additional pixel-level annotations were provided.
