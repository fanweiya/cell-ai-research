Evaluation subsets. Compounds differ greatly in their number of training images and in the strength of their phenotypes. In addition to the overall accuracy, the leaderboard therefore reports accuracy on three subsets of the test set: • POSCON : the eight positive-control compounds, present on every plate, usually at fixed well positions. They have many training images and strong, well-characterized phenotypes. • TARGET : 293 compounds from the JUMP-Target collection, a reference library chosen to cover diverse biological targets and mechanisms of action. • REST : all remaining compounds, each represented by few images (about 12 per compound in the public split). The three subsets correspond to increasingly difficult regimes: abundant data with strong phenotypes ( POSCON ), a curated mechanism-of-action reference set ( TARGET ), and a long tail of rare classes ( REST ). Evaluation. The evaluation server passes the private test images, as an array of shape ( N , H , W , 5 ) (N,H,W,5) , together with a metadata list containing the source, batch, and plate identifier of every image, to both \code adapt() and \code predict(). No labels are provided. \code adapt() may update the model using the unlabeled test images and their metadata, which permits test-time adaptation. Methods that do not adapt should implement it as a no-op. \code predict() must return one prediction per image, in the original order. 4 Development datasets To lower the entry barrier and enable fast prototyping before a leaderboard submission, we release two smaller variants of JUMP-CP-2k: • JUMP-CP-MNIST contains only the eight positive-control compounds. Its images are downsampled to 200 × 200 200\times 200 pixels, and the five imaging channels (DNA, RNA, ER, AGP, and Mito) are combined into three channels, so that standard RGB architectures and pretrained models can be applied without modification. • JUMP-CP-2k-200 contains all 2,000 compounds of JUMP-CP-2k at the reduced resolution of 200 × 200 200\times 200 pixels, with all five channels. Both variants use the same source-level split as JUMP-CP-2k. Leaderboard rankings are computed on the full-resolution private test set. 6 Results Table 2 reports the current leaderboard entries. Test-time adaptation reduces the domain gap. Without adaptation, ResNet18 reaches an average overall accuracy of 0.175. Re-estimating the BatchNorm statistics on each test plate (AdaBN) raises this to 0.244. Most of this gain comes from POSCON , where accuracy increases from 0.465 to 0.646. TENT’s additional entropy minimization yields a higher performance in the POSCON group, but no improvement in TARGET or REST . Domain-invariant training does not improve over the non-adapted baseline: DANN reaches 0.170. The domain gap remains for compounds with few images. Performance on POSCON is substantially higher than on TARGET and REST , consistent with the much larger number of training images per compound, lower number of classes and strong phenotypes. These factors are absent for most TARGET and REST compounds, and none of the current entries compensates for them. Identifying compounds with weak phenotypes and few training images on unseen sources is therefore the main open problem posed by the leaderboard.
