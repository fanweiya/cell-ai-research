6 Experimental Protocol 6.1 Datasets and Deployment Scenarios We evaluate reliability-preserving compression on two 2D and three 3D medical segmentation settings. For 2D, we use PROMISE12 [ 26 ] for prostate MRI segmentation and MosMedPlus, derived from MosMedData [ 31 ] , for COVID-19 lung-infection CT segmentation [ 38 ] . For 3D, we use SegResNet on AMOS22 [ 19 ] for abdominal multi-organ segmentation, BraTS [ 30 ] for brain-tumor segmentation, and BTCV [ 24 ] for abdominal CT multi-organ segmentation. These datasets cover 2D MRI/CT and volumetric MRI/CT segmentation, allowing us to test whether compression-induced reliability changes persist across modality, anatomy, and dimensionality. For all experiments, calibration data are disjoint from the held-out test split and are used only for quantization-range estimation, SGAV-MP scoring, and aggregate validation; all final metrics are reported on the held-out test set. For the 2D experiments, we use the original MedSegBench partitions without modifying the split: MosMedPlus has 1910 / 272 / 547 1910/272/547 train/calibration/test images of size 512 × 512 × 3 512\times 512\times 3 , and PROMISE12 has 1031 / 147 / 295 1031/147/295 images of size 512 × 512 512\times 512 . The MedSegBench validation split is used as calibration data for the main 2D results. For 3D, we use one fixed volume-level split for all methods and ablations: AMOS22 168 / 24 / 48 168/24/48 , BraTS 1134 / 162 / 325 1134/162/325 , and BTCV 21 / 3 / 6 21/3/6 train/calibration/test volumes. In the default SGAV-MP setting, score computation uses 10 % 10\% of the calibration data for efficiency; calibration-size sensitivity using 10 % 10\% , 25 % 25\% , 50 % 50\% , and 100 % 100\% is reported in the supplement. We treat compression as the main objective and report deployment-related measurements separately. For representative 2D and 3D deployment settings, we measure backend-supported GPU execution, including latency, peak memory, model size, and compression ratio. For SGAV-MP policies containing mixed INT4 or INT2 actions, we report storage compression and reliability metrics without claiming runtime speedup unless directly supported by the backend. 6.2 Models For the 2D experiments, we evaluate representative CNN-based segmentation models instantiated with five encoder families: ResNet18, ResNet50 [ 12 ] , MobileNetV2 [ 37 ] , EfficientNet-B0 [ 42 ] , and DenseNet121 [ 15 ] . These models cover residual, lightweight mobile, compound-scaled, and densely connected architectures, allowing us to test whether reliability degradation under quantization is architecture-dependent. For the 3D experiments, we use SegResNet, a residual encoder–decoder segmentation model widely used for volumetric medical segmentation and related to the BraTS-winning residual segmentation design [ 32 ] . For the separate 3D TensorRT deployment check, we use UNet3D on AMOS22 because the MedPTQ/TensorRT path is directly compatible with this backbone, while SegResNet deployment is limited in our setting by GroupNorm and residual-path execution issues. The 3D study evaluates whether SGAV-MP remains effective when compression is applied to volumetric models with higher memory and inference cost. 6.3 Baselines and Deployment We compare SGAV-MP with fixed-precision, PTQ, and mixed-precision baselines. FP32 is the reference model; FP16, INT8, and INT4 are uniform fixed-bit baselines. We also include AdaRound [ 33 ] , Hessian Greedy, MCKP, and EfficientQ [ 43 ] to compare against calibration-based rounding, sensitivity-guided selection, constrained mixed-precision assignment, and medical PTQ. EfficientQ2/4 are uniform weight-only EfficientQ baselines at 2/4 bits, run with the released code under the same calibration/test protocol. MedPTQ is discussed as related medical PTQ, but direct reproduction is limited by code/export/backend reproducibility in our setting and by INT4 deployment support. Tables 7 and 8 report backend-supported TensorRT measurements for representative 3D and 2D settings. All TensorRT measurements use an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada Generation GPU (48 GB, compute capability sm 89 ) with TensorRT 10.3, supporting optimized FP32, FP16, and INT8 inference. The 2D SGAV-MP deployment policies are restricted to FP16/INT8 execution, while mixed INT4/INT2 policies are reported as storage-compression results unless directly supported by the backend. For 3D deployment, we report an AMOS22 UNet3D TensorRT check because direct SegResNet reproduction with MedPTQ is limited in our setting by code/export/backend compatibility, GroupNorm and residual-path execution issues, and INT4 deployment support. MedPTQ also does not provide a directly usable INT2 or arbitrary mixed INT2/INT4 variant for our evaluated backbones/backend. 6.4 Evaluation Metrics We evaluate compressed models using segmentation, compression, calibration, uncertainty-preservation, and failure-detection metrics. Segmentation quality is measured by Dice [ 3 , 41 ] . Storage efficiency is reported using model size and compression ratio; for TensorRT-supported deployment variants, we also report latency and peak memory. Calibration and probability quality are measured by ECE and Brier score [ 9 , 8 , 29 ] . Uncertainty preservation is measured by the rank correlation ρ U \rho_{U} between predictive-entropy maps of the compressed and FP32 models [ 39 , 40 ] . Failure-detection behavior is evaluated using uncertainty-error AUROC and AURC [ 5 , 7 ] . SGAV-MP uses Dice, ECE, ρ U \rho_{U} , and AURC as aggregate acceptance constraints, while error-AUROC is reported as an additional diagnostic. Additional calibration-size sweeps, paired-layer analysis, and qualitative examples are provided in the supplement. 8 Discussion SGAV-MP shows that medical segmentation compression should be evaluated beyond Dice and model size. Across 2D and 3D experiments, fixed low-bit baselines can preserve usable Dice while changing calibration, uncertainty maps, or risk–coverage behavior. This motivates aggregate validation: layer scores rank candidate precision changes, but acceptance is decided by evaluating the complete compressed model on a calibration set. The Pareto analysis also shows that reliability tolerance defines a compression–uncertainty frontier rather than a single fixed INT8 or INT4 point. SGAV-MP is compression-oriented and does not imply automatic runtime speedup. We separate storage compression from backend-supported measurements: TensorRT FP32/FP16/INT8 results are reported for representative 2D and 3D settings, while mixed INT4/INT2 policies are reported only by storage compression and reliability metrics unless directly supported by the backend. Limitations include dependence on the FP32 model’s reliability, use of labeled calibration data, sensitivity to calibration representativeness, nonzero search cost, and the need for broader evaluation on foundation-style segmenters, additional modalities, and real mixed INT4/INT2 deployment backends. 9 Conclusion We studied reliability-preserving post-training compression for medical image segmentation. Dice-preserving quantization can still degrade uncertainty, calibration, and failure-detection behavior, motivating SGAV-MP, a score-guided mixed-precision method with aggregate full-model reliability validation. Across 2D MRI/CT and 3D volumetric benchmarks, SGAV-MP preserves FP32-like segmentation and uncertainty behavior while achieving storage compression. Overall, compressed medical segmenters should be evaluated using reliability signals in addition to accuracy and compression ratio.
