2 Method 2.1 Overview of stMIND stMIND models a time-series of spatial transcriptomic sections as unmatched snapshots sampled from an evolving, proliferating, and migrating cell population (Fig. 1a). Each cell i i observed at an initial stage provides an initial gene-expression vector x i t ∈ ℝ d x_{i}^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} , a physical tissue coordinate s i t ∈ ℝ d s s_{i}^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}} , and a positive mass weight m i t ∈ ℝ + m_{i}^{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+} representing its contribution to the population distribution. To describe how its expression state evolves, stMIND uses an interacting stochastic differential equation: d ​ x i t = f i x ​ ( X t , S t , m t ) ​ d ​ t + σ x ​ d ​ W i x , \mathrm{d}x_{i}^{t}=f_{i}^{x}(X^{t},S^{t},m^{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{x}\,\mathrm{d}W_{i}^{x}, (1) Here X t X^{t} , S t S^{t} , and m t m^{t} collect the modeled expression states, positions, and population weights at time t t . The velocity vector, or the drift term of SDE f i x f_{i}^{x} determines the modeled direction of expression change and can depend on both the focal cell and its cellular context. To model cell migration and proliferation, we formulate tissue-position and log-weight dynamics using separate learned fields, as in stVCR [ 13 ] . Tissue positions evolve according to d ​ s i t = f i s ​ ( x i t , s i t , t ) ​ d ​ t , \mathrm{d}s_{i}^{t}=f_{i}^{s}(x_{i}^{t},s_{i}^{t},t)\,\mathrm{d}t, (2) where f i s f_{i}^{s} is the learned spatial velocity field. Population change is represented by positive cell weights, initialized at m i t 0 = 1 m_{i}^{t_{0}}=1 , whose log-growth rates satisfy d ​ log ⁡ m i t = r i ​ ( x i t , s i t , t ) ​ d ​ t , \mathrm{d}\log m_{i}^{t}=r_{i}(x_{i}^{t},s_{i}^{t},t)\,\mathrm{d}t, (3) where r i r_{i} is the learned log-weight growth-rate field. To resolve cellular context across distinct spatial scales, stMIND decomposes the transcriptomic drift f i x f_{i}^{x} of focal cell i i into three additive vector fields: f i x ​ ( X t , S t , m t ) = f intrinsic ​ ( x i t ) + f nonlocal , i ​ ( X t , m t ) + f local , i ​ ( X t , S t , m t ) . f_{i}^{x}(X^{t},S^{t},m^{t})=f_{\text{intrinsic}}(x_{i}^{t})+f_{\text{nonlocal},i}(X^{t},m^{t})+f_{\text{local},i}(X^{t},S^{t},m^{t}). (4) Here, f intrinsic f_{\text{intrinsic}} depends solely on the focal cell’s internal transcriptomic state x i t x_{i}^{t} . The interaction terms f local , i f_{\text{local},i} and f nonlocal , i f_{\text{nonlocal},i} aggregate intercellular cues from two mutually exclusive sets of context cells partitioned by a physical interaction radius R R , which are dynamically updated as cells migrate along a learned spatial velocity field (Fig. 1b). The choice of R R was informed by GITIII [ 24 ] ; the radius-calibration procedure and fitting-range-specific parameter values are provided in Section 2 of the Supplementary Material. The nonlocal operator f nonlocal , i f_{\text{nonlocal},i} captures long-range population influences from context cells residing outside the focal cell’s immediate neighborhood ( ‖ s j t − s i t ‖ 2 ≥ R \|s_{j}^{t}-s_{i}^{t}\|_{2}\geq R ). Extending the mean-field attention mechanism of scIMF [ 14 ] , the focal cell’s expression state generates a query, while eligible distant context cells supply expression-derived keys and values weighted by their evolving masses m j t m_{j}^{t} . At the continuum level, this mass-normalized attention acts as a learned integral operator over the exterior empirical measure. Conversely, the local operator f local , i f_{\text{local},i} models short-range microenvironmental interactions over a dynamic radius graph ( ‖ s j t − s i t ‖ 2 < R \|s_{j}^{t}-s_{i}^{t}\|_{2}<R ). Rather than aggregating raw expression states, the local operator aggregates pairwise expression differences ( x j t − x i t ) (x_{j}^{t}-x_{i}^{t}) between neighboring context cells and the focal cell, with attention keys modulated by a learned spatial encoding of their relative displacement ( s j t − s i t ) (s_{j}^{t}-s_{i}^{t}) . Mathematically, this formulation endows f local , i f_{\text{local},i} with the structure of an anisotropic, state-dependent graph difference operator whose response vanishes when a neighborhood is transcriptionally homogeneous and adapts dynamically as local tissue geometry reorganizes. stMIND is trained end-to-end by minimizing a relaxed dynamic least-action objective that couples trajectory kinetic energy with optimal transport and spatial occupancy matching at observation time points. Because supervising only marginal snapshots leaves an additive decomposition under-constrained, we introduce an endpoint orthogonality penalty that discourages directional collinearity between f intrinsic f_{\text{intrinsic}} and each interaction term, ensuring a parsimonious and identifiable separation of intrinsic and extrinsic dynamics (Fig. 1d,e). Detailed network configurations and implementation settings are provided in Section 2 of the Supplementary Material. 3 Results 3.1 stMIND accurately predicts gene expression and spatial distributions We first evaluated stMIND on two time-resolved spatial transcriptomics datasets, axolotl brain regeneration and zebrafish embryogenesis, against four baseline methods: two spatial-temporal frameworks, stVCR [ 13 ] and STORIES [ 12 ] , and two non-spatial interacting mean-field methods, scIMF [ 14 ] and CytoBridge [ 15 ] . The axolotl dataset from the ARTISTA atlas records molecular and spatial changes in the telencephalon during regeneration after injury [ 22 ] . The zebrafish dataset from the ZESTA atlas captures changes in gene expression and tissue organization during early embryonic development [ 23 ] . Data sources and preprocessing procedures are detailed in Section 3 of the Supplementary Material. We defined short- and long-horizon extrapolation tasks for each dataset. Axolotl models fitted at 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20 days post-injury (DPI) were evaluated at 30 and 60 DPI. Zebrafish models fitted at 3.3, 5.25, 10 and 12 hours post-fertilization (hpf) were evaluated at 18 and 24 hpf. We quantified marginal gene-expression accuracy using W 1 W_{1} and W 2 W_{2} Wasserstein distances in a shared reference embedding, joint transcriptomic–anatomical fidelity using Fused Gromov–Wasserstein (FGW) discrepancy, and spatial coherence of the predicted expression field using Moran’s I I . All metrics are reported as mean ± \pm s.d. across five random seeds. On the short-horizon prediction tasks, stMIND achieved the lowest mean gene-expression errors ( W 1 W_{1} and W 2 W_{2} ) across all five methods in both biological systems (Fig. 2a,b). In axolotl regeneration at 30 DPI, stMIND attained W 1 = 2.287 W_{1}=2.287 and W 2 = 2.386 W_{2}=2.386 (Fig. 2a). Similarly, in zebrafish development at 18 hpf, stMIND achieved the best expression errors with W 1 = 2.179 W_{1}=2.179 and W 2 = 2.265 W_{2}=2.265 (Fig. 2b). At the long-horizon endpoints, stMIND ranked second overall in marginal expression accuracy: at 60 DPI in axolotl, stMIND achieved W 1 = 3.042 W_{1}=3.042 and W 2 = 3.147 W_{2}=3.147 , trailing only its non-spatial predecessor scIMF ( W 1 = 2.501 , W 2 = 2.588 W_{1}=2.501,W_{2}=2.588 ); at 24 hpf in zebrafish, stMIND achieved W 1 = 2.457 W_{1}=2.457 and W 2 = 2.618 W_{2}=2.618 , closely behind scIMF ( W 1 = 2.362 , W 2 = 2.468 W_{1}=2.362,W_{2}=2.468 ). This pattern reflects a natural trade-off at extended extrapolation horizons: whereas scIMF optimizes trajectories unconstrained in expression space alone, stMIND simultaneously enforces physical tissue transport and spatial organization constraints over long unobserved intervals. Crucially, unlike scIMF, CytoBridge, and STORIES—which cannot natively evolve physical coordinates forward in time—stMIND jointly predicts evolving tissue geometry. Compared against stVCR (the only baseline capable of generating spatial coordinates), stMIND achieved substantially lower FGW discrepancy across every completed benchmark, demonstrating superior joint alignment of gene expression and anatomical structure (Fig. 2a,b). Furthermore, stMIND generated stronger positive spatial autocorrelation (Moran’s I I ) than stVCR at both 30 and 60 DPI in axolotl, while stVCR exhibited higher Moran’s I I at 18 hpf in zebrafish. At the 24-hpf zebrafish endpoint, stVCR failed to complete within the computational time limit, leaving stMIND as the only method capable of delivering full joint spatiotemporal predictions at scale. We defined the expression-drift energy of each component as its squared Euclidean norm ( ‖ f ‖ 2 \|f\|^{2} ), providing a quantitative measure of kinetic energy across stages. This analysis uncovered a critical regenerative transition window between 10 and 15 DPI, marked by an acute surge in total expression-drift energy followed by a sharp reorganization between nonlocal and local interaction scales (Fig. 4 a,b). Furthermore, the expression-drift energy stabilizes into an equilibrium state by 30 and 60 DPI (Fig. 4 a). Specifically, expression-drift energy peaked at 10 DPI and subsequently declined, revealing pronounced expression-dynamical activity during the intermediate phase of regeneration (Fig. 4 a, Supplementary Figs. S2 and S3). Following this peak, the nonlocal share of summed component energies reached its maximum at 15 DPI, accounting for approximately 81%. At the same stage, nonlocal drift exhibited its strongest alignment with total drift, whereas local–total alignment was weakest. Spatial analysis further revealed the strongest local–nonlocal opposition at 15 DPI, with negative alignment in approximately 65% of cells within the ROI (Fig. 4 b). This transition window therefore encompassed two successive dynamical features: elevated total drift energy at 10 DPI, followed by nonlocal dominance and local directional counterbalance at 15 DPI. At 30 and 60 DPI, total drift energy had decreased and the relative energy shares of the local and nonlocal components had become more balanced, suggesting that the multiscale dynamics had largely stabilized. To investigate how nonlocal information was organized at this transition, we examined nonlocal attention at 15 DPI (Fig. 4 c). The cell-level attention matrix and its reciprocal difference revealed structured asymmetry in the weights assigned between cell pairs, while cell-type summaries showed heterogeneous patterns of context weighting. Stage-dependent patterns of nonlocal attention and nonreciprocity were also observed across the full regeneration series (Supplementary Figs. S4 and S5). These results characterize nonlocal aggregation as a focal-cell-dependent process in which the two directions of a cell pair can receive different weights. Within the ROI, local–nonlocal directional balance further exhibited cell-type dependence (Fig. 4 d, Supplementary Fig. S6). Opposing directions were particularly pronounced in reactive ependymoglial cells (reaEGCs) and reactive intermediate progenitor cell (rIPC) subtypes, with the strongest opposition in rIPC1. Immature neurons (IMNs) also showed negative mean alignment, whereas the pooled remaining annotations had a mean close to zero. These findings localized the mid-regeneration dynamical reorganization to specific cell populations, linking distinct cellular states to different combinations of multiscale contributions. Decomposition of local contributions further connected this pattern to reaEGC-associated neighborhoods (Fig. 4 e). Across all four focal-cell groups, reaEGC neighbors received a larger attention fraction than their availability among neighboring cells. Along a fixed reaEGC-to-IMN expression-contrast axis, reaEGC neighbors supplied the largest absolute group-mean local contribution. Nonlocal projections were positive, whereas local projections were negative, partially offsetting the nonlocal contribution while leaving a positive total projection. Thus, local neighborhoods contributed to the resulting expression drift through directional counterbalance. Together, stMIND identified a key regenerative window in which elevated total drift energy was followed by nonlocal dominance and cell-type-dependent local counterbalance, and linked this stage-specific dynamical pattern to reaEGC-associated neighborhoods. These findings provide a testable hypothesis for how cellular context at different spatial scales contributes to cell-state reorganization during regeneration. These interactions co-evolve as cells proliferate and migrate, yet spatial transcriptomics provides unmatched snapshots through destructive sampling. Reconstructing the underlying multiscale dynamics therefore remains challenging. Extending our interacting mean-field neural SDE framework (scIMF [ 14 ] ) into physical space, stMIND bridges this gap by jointly evolving gene expression, spatial coordinates, and cellular mass while decomposing transcriptomic drift into intrinsic, local graph-difference, and nonlocal mean-field operators. Separating local and nonlocal context yielded complementary insights. In axolotl ablations, the full model more accurately reconstructed local microenvironment distributions than other spatially informed variants while retaining competitive gene-expression prediction. In the full-series axolotl analysis, a peak in total expression-drift energy preceded nonlocal dominance and cell-type-dependent local counterbalance; the local response also preferentially drew on reaEGC neighbors. Nonreciprocal attention further shows that contextual dependence is directional in the learned model: two cells can assign different importance to one another. Combined with cell-type-dependent local counterbalance, this provides a way to examine how focal-cell state and spatial context jointly shape predicted expression change. In zebrafish, inferred expression drifts captured stage-dependent developmental progression. Together, these findings show how distinguishing interaction ranges supports both tissue prediction and analysis of coordinated molecular and anatomical change. The multiscale decomposition also makes the relationship between drift magnitude and direction explicit. A component with a smaller energy share can still influence total expression change through its orientation relative to the other components. Energy shares quantify relative squared magnitudes, whereas directional alignment and signed projections describe reinforcement and counterbalance. The axolotl analysis illustrates why these readouts are complementary: nonlocal dominance coexists with cell-type-dependent local responses. This combination allows localized, state-dependent responses to be examined even when nonlocal contributions dominate the component energies. The prominence of reaEGC-associated neighborhoods connects these dynamical patterns to the established regenerative role of ependymoglial cells. Targeted EGC depletion has been shown to block axolotl brain regeneration [ 25 ] . In stMIND, reaEGC neighbors received attention exceeding their neighborhood availability and contributed to local counterbalance along the reaEGC-to-IMN expression contrast. These observations motivate the hypothesis that reactive glial neighborhoods help coordinate regenerative cell-state transitions. Spatial profiling combined with targeted perturbations could test whether changing reaEGC-associated context alters the expression responses of nearby progenitor and immature neuronal populations. Several computational and modeling directions merit future investigation. First, full pairwise nonlocal attention incurs 𝒪 ⁡ ( N 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{2}) computational cost per integration step, while repeatedly updating local radius graphs adds further overhead during continuous SDE rollouts. We currently reduce the attention cost through mini-batch sampling of context cells, but this subsampling may omit informative long-range cellular context. TissueFormer addresses quadratic attention costs through a whole-slide attention mechanism with linear complexity in the number of cells [ 26 ] . Adapting a related strategy to stMIND’s evolving spatial masks and mass-weighted aggregation could preserve broader nonlocal context at lower computational cost and reduce reliance on context subsampling. Second, integrating curated ligand–receptor priors [ 27 ] into the query–key projections of local and nonlocal attention, together with gene-resolved features, could help relate learned expression-drift contributions to candidate molecular signaling pathways. We plan to investigate scalable attention and biologically informed interaction terms to extend stMIND to larger tissue atlases and generate more directly testable signaling hypotheses.
