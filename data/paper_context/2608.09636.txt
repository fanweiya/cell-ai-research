Given an initial 3D segmentation mask M i ​ n ​ i ​ t M_{init} and the corresponding image I I , we first apply Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) along the z-axis to obtain projection M x ​ y M^{xy} and I x ​ y I^{xy} . Performing MIP on sparse volumes facilitates their input into VLMs while inducing minimal signal overlap. The projected volume is then partitioned into a set of non-overlapping 2D sub-blocks ℬ = { b i } i = 1 N \mathcal{B}=\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N} , yielding corresponding image and mask patches { I i x ​ y } \left\{I_{i}^{xy}\right\} and { M i x ​ y } \left\{M_{i}^{xy}\right\} . As a crucial metric for neuron segmentation, topological correctness serves as an effective guide for identifying erroneous regions. For each sub-block, the 0th-order Betti number is computed to quantify topological irregularities as β 0 ​ ( b i ) = C ​ C ​ ( M i ) \beta_{0}(b_{i})=CC(M_{i}) , where C ​ C ​ ( ⋅ ) CC(\cdot) denotes the connected component counting function in the corresponding 3D region. An elevated β 0 \beta_{0} value, which indicates a greater number of isolated fragments within a sub-block, suggests a higher likelihood of topological errors. Based on this topological metric and the mask, the inspection policy π ins \pi_{\mathrm{ins}} identifies abnormal regions and categorizes their error types: B fix , τ i = π ins ​ ( M x ​ y , { M i xy } , β 0 ​ ( b i ) ) , \operatorname{B}_{\mathrm{fix}},{\tau_{i}}=\pi_{\mathrm{ins}}(M^{xy},\{M_{i}^{\mathrm{xy}}\},{\beta_{0}(b_{i})}), (2) where B f ​ i ​ x ⊆ ℬ \operatorname{B}_{fix}\subseteq\mathcal{B} denotes the subset of blocks requiring refinement and τ i \tau_{i} labels each block as either false-positive or false-negative. They provide guidance for the Refinement Advisor to generate detailed instructions. 3.2.2 Refinement Advisor generates structured, morphology-aware instructions to guide the correction of identified sub-blocks. To precisely delineate the abnormal region along the z-axis, the agent first applies MIP to the 3D region corresponding to b i b_{i} along the x-axis and then localizes the defect in the yz-plane. Upon obtaining the xy and yz plane projections of the target region, it jointly analyzes the image and mask from orthogonal views to uncover connectivity cues and formulate a refinement instruction, as 𝐭 i = π adv ​ ( I i x ​ y , I i y ​ z , M i x ​ y , M i y ​ z , τ i ) . \mathbf{t}_{i}=\pi_{\mathrm{adv}}\big(I_{i}^{xy},I_{i}^{yz},M_{i}^{xy},M_{i}^{yz},\tau_{i}\big). (3) Each instruction 𝐭 i \mathbf{t}_{i} explicitly encodes both a spatial location (e.g., “upper-left-posterior”) and an editing operation (e.g., “connect” or “remove”). The editing module TopoRefineNet subsequently performs refinement based on the instruction and outputs M i refined M_{i}^{\mathrm{refined}} . This design decouples reasoning from voxel-level operations by using natural language as an intermediary, thereby fully unleashing the agent’s reasoning and expressive capabilities. 3.2.3 Change Validator ensures the reliability of iterative refinement by evaluating the output of TopoRefineNet and preventing erroneous edits from being merged. Specifically, it compares the initial mask M i M_{i} against the refined result M i refined M_{i}^{\mathrm{refined}} , conditioned on the instruction 𝐭 i \mathbf{t}_{i} and the error type τ i \tau_{i} to assess whether the edit is beneficial to morphology. This evaluation is formalized as r i = π val ​ ( M i x ​ y , M i refined , 𝐭 i , τ i ) . r_{i}=\pi_{\mathrm{val}}\big(M_{i}^{xy},M_{i}^{\mathrm{refined}},\mathbf{t}_{i},\tau_{i}\big). (4) The Change Validator only approves an edit when two criteria are satisfied: (1) instruction adherence—the modification correctly implements the spatial and operational intent of 𝐭 i \mathbf{t}_{i} , and (2) topological improvement—the refined mask exhibits reduced fragmentation or noise without introducing new artifacts. If either condition fails, it triggers the refinement advisor to generate a new instruction, thereby closing the loop with corrective feedback. To endow TopoRefineNet with precise instruction-to-voxel editing capability, we devise a two-stage training strategy as shown in Fig. ˜ 3 (b). The initial stage prioritizes cross-modal alignment using synthetic data with morphological defects. Specifically, ground-truth masks M gt M_{\mathrm{gt}} are perturbed via data augmentation to yield corrupted variants ( Sec. ˜ 4.1.3 ), with corresponding refinement instructions generated based on the location and type of perturbation. The second stage subsequently shifts focus toward real-world generalization. We curate segmentation outputs from multiple networks trained on diverse neuronal datasets and specifically select instances that exhibit representative topological errors. For each identified error, a refinement instruction is synthesized using Qwen3-VL [ bai2025qwen3 ] . By progressively increasing defect complexity from synthetic scenarios to complex real-world cases, this scheme stabilizes optimization and enhances robustness against the diverse instructions encountered during inference. 3.3.1 Architecture. To evaluate NeuroRefiner across diverse neuronal morphologies and imaging conditions, we conducted experiments on three representative benchmarks, adhering to the training–testing splits established in prior work [ yan2025glancing , yang2021structure , liu2024brain ] . The BigNeuron [ peng2015bigneuron ] dataset comprises expert-annotated neurons spanning multiple species. Images were acquired across different laboratories and imaging platforms, exhibiting substantial variations in appearance, resolution, and scale. The CWMBS [ liu2024brain ] dataset is derived from whole-brain mouse imaging and contains volumes of size 256 × 256 × 256 voxels (physical resolution: 0.2µm × 0.2µm × 1µm), of which 83 exhibit strong background noise and 162 feature thin filamentary structures with weak signals. The ZBFWB dataset [ du2025central ] consists of confocal microscopy images from 6-day-old zebrafish larvae (1000 × 2000 × 250 voxels; 0.5µm × 0.5µm × 1µm), presenting challenges due to its low contrast, long-range axonal projections, intricate network topologies, and low contrast. 4.1.2 Metrics. To ensure consistency with prior work [ yan2025glancing , liu2024brain , yang2021structure ] , we use reconstructions generated by APP2 [ xiao2013app2 ] to assess segmentation quality from point-level accuracy to topological completeness. We compute the precision, recall, and F1-score to evaluate voxel-level accuracy and further employ the distance-based metric Spatial Distance (SD) to measure the average bidirectional distance between prediction and ground-truth structures. Its variant SSD [ peng2010v3d ] , excludes matched points within 2 voxels to focus on significant topological errors. Finally, MES [ xie2011anisotropic ] evaluates the overall morphological fidelity of topology by quantifying the lengths of missing and extra structures. Lower SD and SSD values, along with higher values for all other metrics, indicate superior reconstruction. 4.1.3 Details. In our experiments, all agents use Qwen3-VL-8B [ bai2025qwen3 ] as the foundation model, and text embeddings are obtained from Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B [ zhang2025qwen3 ] . The Global Inspector performs error detection using a region of 128 × 128 pixels as its fundamental unit. The maximum number of iterations T max T_{\text{max}} is set to 5. The prompts for all agents and the template of instructions for TopoRefineNet are provided in the supplementary material. Both training stages of TopoRefineNet employ cross-entropy loss and dice loss [ milletari2016v ] . In the first stage, to synthesize FN segmentations, we randomly select a positive sample as the center and perform an erosion operation with a random kernel size k ∈ [ 5 , 20 ] k\in[5,20] to generate breaks of varying lengths. For FP samples, we either randomly copy-paste annotations of other neurons or introduce Gaussian noise within local regions. In the second stage, we trained several common architectures—namely, 3D U-Net [ cciccek20163d ] , V-Net [ liu2018improved ] , UNETR [ hatamizadeh2022unetr ] , nnFormer [ zhou2023nnformer ] , and SwinUNETR [ hatamizadeh2021swin ] —on a subset of the training set, followed by inference on the complete training set. Based on connected component and F1 scores, we selected a total of 5,310 volumes of 128 × 128 × 64 voxels exhibiting significant segmentation errors for training. To validate the efficacy of the proposed multi-agent system, we conducted ablation studies on the ZBFWB and CWMBS datasets, summarized in Table 3 . In the absence of agents, TopoRefineNet refines all blocks indiscriminately for T m ​ a ​ x T_{max} rounds. While this improves initial segmentation, the lack of global context and targeted guidance yields suboptimal results. Employing solely the Global Inspector for both error localization and instruction generation provides negligible gains, as a single agent struggles to manage these distinct tasks and lacks quality control mechanisms. The Refinement Advisor decouples instruction generation from inspection. This functional specialization yields F1 gains of 6.18% and 6.80%, respectively. Further incorporating the Change Validator to filter unreliable refinements yields additional gains of 2.66% and 1.68%. Ultimately, the full system achieves optimal performance, confirming that synergistic collaboration is essential for robust, high-fidelity neuron segmentation refinement. Fig. ˜ 5 illustrates the impact of the number of iterations. The results indicate that across all three datasets, the initial iteration yields substantial gains, whereas improvements diminish significantly after the fifth iteration. Consequently, we set the maximum iteration count T m ​ a ​ x = 5 T_{max}=5 . Notably, the more challenging ZBFWB and CWMBS datasets require more steps to converge and demonstrate significant improvement over the baseline segmentation. 4.3.2 Ablation Study on Foundation models. To evaluate NeuroRefiner across diverse neuronal morphologies and imaging conditions, we conducted experiments on three representative benchmarks, adhering to the training–testing splits established in prior work [ yan2025glancing , yang2021structure , liu2024brain ] . The BigNeuron [ peng2015bigneuron ] dataset comprises expert-annotated neurons spanning multiple species. Images were acquired across different laboratories and imaging platforms, exhibiting substantial variations in appearance, resolution, and scale. The CWMBS [ liu2024brain ] dataset is derived from whole-brain mouse imaging and contains volumes of size 256 × 256 × 256 voxels (physical resolution: 0.2µm × 0.2µm × 1µm), of which 83 exhibit strong background noise and 162 feature thin filamentary structures with weak signals. The ZBFWB dataset [ du2025central ] consists of confocal microscopy images from 6-day-old zebrafish larvae (1000 × 2000 × 250 voxels; 0.5µm × 0.5µm × 1µm), presenting challenges due to its low contrast, long-range axonal projections, intricate network topologies, and low contrast. 4.1.2 Metrics. To ensure consistency with prior work [ yan2025glancing , liu2024brain , yang2021structure ] , we use reconstructions generated by APP2 [ xiao2013app2 ] to assess segmentation quality from point-level accuracy to topological completeness. We compute the precision, recall, and F1-score to evaluate voxel-level accuracy and further employ the distance-based metric Spatial Distance (SD) to measure the average bidirectional distance between prediction and ground-truth structures. Its variant SSD [ peng2010v3d ] , excludes matched points within 2 voxels to focus on significant topological errors. Finally, MES [ xie2011anisotropic ] evaluates the overall morphological fidelity of topology by quantifying the lengths of missing and extra structures. Lower SD and SSD values, along with higher values for all other metrics, indicate superior reconstruction. 4.1.3 Details. In our experiments, all agents use Qwen3-VL-8B [ bai2025qwen3 ] as the foundation model, and text embeddings are obtained from Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B [ zhang2025qwen3 ] . The Global Inspector performs error detection using a region of 128 × 128 pixels as its fundamental unit. The maximum number of iterations T max T_{\text{max}} is set to 5. The prompts for all agents and the template of instructions for TopoRefineNet are provided in the supplementary material. Both training stages of TopoRefineNet employ cross-entropy loss and dice loss [ milletari2016v ] . In the first stage, to synthesize FN segmentations, we randomly select a positive sample as the center and perform an erosion operation with a random kernel size k ∈ [ 5 , 20 ] k\in[5,20] to generate breaks of varying lengths. For FP samples, we either randomly copy-paste annotations of other neurons or introduce Gaussian noise within local regions. In the second stage, we trained several common architectures—namely, 3D U-Net [ cciccek20163d ] , V-Net [ liu2018improved ] , UNETR [ hatamizadeh2022unetr ] , nnFormer [ zhou2023nnformer ] , and SwinUNETR [ hatamizadeh2021swin ] —on a subset of the training set, followed by inference on the complete training set. Based on connected component and F1 scores, we selected a total of 5,310 volumes of 128 × 128 × 64 voxels exhibiting significant segmentation errors for training. To validate the efficacy of the proposed multi-agent system, we conducted ablation studies on the ZBFWB and CWMBS datasets, summarized in Table 3 . In the absence of agents, TopoRefineNet refines all blocks indiscriminately for T m ​ a ​ x T_{max} rounds. While this improves initial segmentation, the lack of global context and targeted guidance yields suboptimal results. Employing solely the Global Inspector for both error localization and instruction generation provides negligible gains, as a single agent struggles to manage these distinct tasks and lacks quality control mechanisms. The Refinement Advisor decouples instruction generation from inspection. This functional specialization yields F1 gains of 6.18% and 6.80%, respectively. Further incorporating the Change Validator to filter unreliable refinements yields additional gains of 2.66% and 1.68%. Ultimately, the full system achieves optimal performance, confirming that synergistic collaboration is essential for robust, high-fidelity neuron segmentation refinement. The results indicate that across all three datasets, the initial iteration yields substantial gains, whereas improvements diminish significantly after the fifth iteration. Consequently, we set the maximum iteration count T m ​ a ​ x = 5 T_{max}=5 . Notably, the more challenging ZBFWB and CWMBS datasets require more steps to converge and demonstrate significant improvement over the baseline segmentation. 4.3.2 Ablation Study on Foundation models. By coordinating morphology-aware global inspection, language-guided editing, and validation, our approach achieves progressive refinement without agent training. Consequently, it overcomes the limited topological fidelity and poor interpretability inherent in end-to-end segmentation models. The proposed TopoRefineNet bridges the gap between instructions and segmentation models, offering novel insights into agent–tool interaction design. Notably, our framework is agnostic to the foundation model, ensuring it evolves in tandem with advancements in VLMs. 5.0.1 Limitation While NeuroRefiner’s 2D MIP detects topological anomalies in sparse neurons, it loses depth information in dense regions, hindering precise 3D error localization. Future work can introduce depth-aware encoding, which maps Z-axis depth to pseudo-color channels or generates depth-weighted projections, allowing the Global Inspector to resolve occlusions and accurately infer the Z-axis depth of topological defects. 5.0.2 Acknowledgement This work was supported by the Brain Science and Brain-like Intelligence Technology - National Science and Technology Major Project (2021ZD0204500, 2021ZD0204503 to L.L.), National Key Research and Development Program of China (2025YFA1614600 to J.L.). 5.0.1 Limitation While NeuroRefiner’s 2D MIP detects topological anomalies in sparse neurons, it loses depth information in dense regions, hindering precise 3D error localization.
