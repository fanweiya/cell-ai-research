3 Method FlowMorph combines a low-dimensional contour representation, a differentiable “capsule-in-flow” dynamics model, and a lightweight temporal encoder to infer a per-track scalar mechanics proxy k k from short brightfield clips. Given a tracked sequence ( I 1 : T ) (I_{1:T}) and coarse silhouettes ( M 1 : T ) (M_{1:T}) extracted automatically, the model predicts shape parameters s 1 : T s_{1:T} and a scalar k k , advances boundary points under laminar flow plus curvature-regularized elastic relaxation, rasterizes the predicted contour into a soft mask, and optimizes self-supervised losses that enforce silhouette, flow, and physics consistency. 3.1 Overview and Notation We consider short brightfield sequences in which individual red blood cells (RBCs) flow through straight or weakly constricted microchannels under low-Reynolds-number conditions, so that the surrounding plasma can be modeled as an incompressible Newtonian fluid in the Stokes regime. [ 12 , 4 , 1 ] For each automatically tracked RBC we extract a cropped sequence I 1 : T = ( I 1 , … , I T ) I_{1:T}=(I_{1},\dots,I_{T}) and derive coarse binary silhouettes M t M_{t} by thresholding and morphological cleanup; these masks are treated as noisy observations for self-supervision rather than ground truth. [ 14 , 6 ] When available (RBCdataset), we also have a binary channel mask Ω wall ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega_{\text{wall}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} and a per-pixel velocity field u ​ ( 𝐱 ) ∈ ℝ 2 u(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2} obtained from Stokes simulations calibrated to the microchannel geometry. [ 6 , 4 ] In datasets where only centered crops are provided (RBC dynamics), we approximate the local flow by a simple extensional velocity gradient with learnable strain rate (Section 3.3 ). [ 7 ] At each time t t , the RBC outline is represented by a compact parameter vector s t ∈ ℝ d s_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and a set of boundary points { 𝐩 t n } n = 1 N ⊂ ℝ 2 \{\mathbf{p}_{t}^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} sampled at fixed angles. Each track is associated with a single scalar k ∈ ℝ + k\in\mathbb{R}_{+} , interpreted as an effective stiffness proxy and assumed to be constant over the T T frames of that track, reflecting the fact that RBC mechanical properties do not vary appreciably on these short time scales. [ 1 , 7 ] Our formulation relies on standard assumptions for RBCs in microfluidic channels: (i) Reynolds numbers Re ≪ 1 \mathrm{Re}\ll 1 so inertia is negligible; (ii) flow profiles are near-Poiseuille in straight channels and well approximated locally by extensional shear in constrictions; (iii) RBCs are effectively incompressible, so projected area changes are small absent strong out-of-plane motion; and (iv) no-slip and non-penetration conditions hold at channel walls when present. [ 12 , 4 , 1 ] These assumptions are satisfied by the devices and flow conditions in the datasets we study. Optimizing this physics-consistent loss yields temporally coherent contours and a per-track scalar k k that capture the strength of curvature-regularized relaxation consistent with observed motion, without any manual masks, motion labels, or mechanical measurements during training. 4 Datasets and Evaluation Protocol We evaluate FlowMorph on four public resources that are widely used in microfluidics and RBC mechanics: (i) microchannel videos with companion velocity fields, (ii) a large corpus of centered RBC dynamics sequences with motion labels, (iii) real-time deformability cytometry (RT-DC) measurements with apparent Young’s modulus values from a hyperelastic lookup table, and (iv) a motility-in-confinement dataset. Unless otherwise noted, all numbers are averaged over five independent training runs with different random seeds, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are estimated by nonparametric bootstrap. [ 10 , 8 ] On RBCdataset, we report mean soft IoU between M ^ t \hat{M}_{t} and M t M_{t} , the fraction of steps with relative projected area change below 2%, the wall-violation rate (percentage of time steps with any boundary point inside the wall region), and the mean endpoint error (EPE) between observed and predicted intra-cellular flow inside the predicted mask. [ 6 , 4 , 11 ] On the RBC dynamics test experiments, we treat k k as a continuous score for dynamic behavior and measure AUC for tank-treading vs. flipping, Spearman rank correlation between k k and the binary label, and expected calibration error (ECE) computed over deciles of predicted scores. [ 7 , 1 , 10 ] On the RT-DC subset, we evaluate mean absolute error (MAE) in apparent Young’s modulus, negative log-likelihood (NLL) under a Gaussian noise model with variance estimated from calibration residuals, ECE over bins of predicted E E , and the percentage of monotonicity violations, i.e., pairs ( i , j ) (i,j) with k i < k j k_{i}<k_{j} but E ^ i > E ^ j \hat{E}_{i}>\hat{E}_{j} . [ 12 , 10 , 2 ] To probe domain shift, we consider scenarios where we train on one microchannel geometry and test on another within RBCdataset, train on one optical setup and test on another within the RBC dynamics experiments, and evaluate physics-validity metrics and qualitative behavior of k k on the confinement dataset. [ 6 , 7 , 3 ] We report changes in AUC, MAE ( E ) (E) , area-conservation rate, and wall-violation rate relative to in-domain performance (see Supplementary Table S1). Robustness to training stochasticity is quantified by the coefficient of variation of the mean k k across runs. Baselines include a classical deformation-index (DI) pipeline, a mask-heavy deep model that combines Cellpose or Mask R-CNN masks with a supervised regressor, and a no-physics network that shares FlowMorph’s encoder and temporal backbone but predicts next-frame masks via a learned warp without explicit laminar-flow or elastic-relaxation terms; ablations remove individual loss terms or alter the contour and temporal models, and are evaluated with the same metrics. Loss ablations. Removing any single physics term degrades both mechanics and physics-validity metrics. Omitting the flow-consistency term ℒ flow \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} or the area-conservation term ℒ area \mathcal{L}_{\text{area}} leads to the largest drops: TT vs. FL AUC decreases by roughly 3–5 points, MAE ( E ) (E) increases, and both area-conservation and intra-cellular flow agreement worsen, confirming that explicitly matching intra-cellular motion and enforcing near-incompressibility are key for learning a meaningful mechanics proxy. The curvature-based elastic energy in Eq. ( 5 ) is an effective surrogate for the full viscoelastic membrane and cytoskeleton of RBCs and does not explicitly model shear elasticity, viscosity, or cytosolic flow, so the scalar k k should be interpreted as an effective stiffness within our contour model rather than a direct membrane modulus. [ 1 , 12 ] The method also assumes short tracks with track-wise constant k k and laminar Stokes flow that is well approximated by Poiseuille or local extensional profiles; performance may degrade in longer recordings, inertial regimes, or strongly three-dimensional flows. [ 4 ] Finally, our 2D contour and signed-distance rasterizer are sensitive to severe overlaps, occlusions, and out-of-plane motion, which must be filtered or down-weighted during preprocessing. [ 3 , 14 ] From an ethics and data-governance standpoint, FlowMorph is trained and evaluated exclusively on public datasets derived from in vitro microfluidic experiments with benign cell samples and no personally identifying information. [ 6 , 7 , 3 , 10 ] The original studies obtained all necessary institutional approvals, and we restrict our work to secondary analysis under their licenses, so no new institutional review board (IRB) review is required. [ 12 , 18 ] In terms of broader impact, FlowMorph enables label-free, resource-efficient mechanical phenotyping pipelines that can be applied across devices and labs without task-specific supervision, potentially helping to screen cohorts or experimental conditions for mechanical alterations indicative of disease, drug response, or storage damage. [ 12 , 1 , 4 ] However, the apparent Young’s modulus estimates obtained after tiny-set calibration are assay-specific surrogates that should complement, not replace, established clinical workflows, and responsible use requires careful calibration and awareness of domain shift when interpreting stiffness estimates in biomedical research. [ 10 ] 8 Conclusion We introduced FlowMorph, a physics-consistent self-supervised framework that learns a scalar mechanics proxy k k from brightfield microfluidic videos via a low-dimensional contour representation and a differentiable capsule-in-flow model. [ 12 , 1 ] Across microfluidic datasets, FlowMorph improves segmentation and physics-validity metrics, yields a k k that correlates with tank-treading versus flipping dynamics, and, after tiny-set calibration, predicts apparent Young’s modulus in line with RT-DC measurements, indicating that modest, mechanically informed forward models can enhance the robustness and interpretability of self-supervised vision pipelines and motivating future work on richer elastic and hydrodynamic models and uncertainty-aware calibration.
