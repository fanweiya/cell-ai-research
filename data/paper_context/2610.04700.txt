III Methodology We first formulate the problem and then describe FSR, DCPA, and S2PA in Secs. III-A – III-C , followed by a discussion ( Sec. III-D ) and a convergence analysis ( Sec. III-E ). Problem Formulation and Notation. We consider a standard federated learning setting [ 2 ] with M M clients indexed by m m . Each client holds a private dataset 𝒟 m = { ( x i , y i ) } i = 1 N m \mathcal{D}_{m}=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N_{m}} of size N m N_{m} . All clients share a segmentation architecture comprising an encoder F enc : 𝒳 → 𝒵 F_{\text{enc}}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z} , which maps an input image x x to feature maps, and a decoder F dec : 𝒵 → 𝒪 F_{\text{dec}}:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O} , which produces the segmentation output 𝐨 = F dec ​ ( F enc ​ ( x ) ) ∈ ℝ | 𝒞 | \mathbf{o}=F_{\text{dec}}(F_{\text{enc}}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|} at each spatial location, where 𝒞 = { 0 , 1 , … , | 𝒞 | − 1 } \mathcal{C}=\{0,1,\dots,|\mathcal{C}|-1\} is the set of classes and class 0 0 denotes the background. The encoder extracts hierarchical features { 𝐳 enc k } k = 1 S \{\mathbf{z}_{\text{enc}}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{S} at S S resolution levels, and the decoder produces corresponding features { 𝐳 dec k } k = 1 S \{\mathbf{z}_{\text{dec}}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{S} . We construct prototypes from a subset of K ≤ S K\leq S levels. We focus on feature heterogeneity , characterized by P m ​ ( x | y ) ≠ P m ′ ​ ( x | y ) P_{m}(x|y)\neq P_{m^{\prime}}(x|y) : all clients share the same label space, while the conditional feature distribution P ⁡ ( x | y ) P(x|y) varies with scanning devices and acquisition protocols. Target sizes, and hence foreground proportions, may also differ across clients (Fig. 3 ). The objective is to collaboratively learn a global model that performs well across all client domains. III-A Frequency-domain Style Recalibration FedBCS+ retains the FSR module introduced in FedBCS [ 24 ] , which recalibrates intermediate feature styles before prototype construction. Motivation for Style-purified Prototype Construction. Existing prototype-based methods assume that same-class features follow similar distributions across clients and therefore construct prototypes by averaging these features. In medical imaging, however, differences in scanning protocols introduce substantial style variations that propagate through network layers and are reflected in the amplitude spectra of intermediate features. Direct averaging retains these discrepancies, so representations of the same anatomical structure can differ markedly across institutions, as shown in Sec. IV-D2 . Reliable semantic anchors for cross-client alignment therefore require style-purified features. We build on the observation that style and content are largely separable in the frequency domain [ 45 , 46 ] , which enables targeted style modulation before prototype construction. Frequency-domain Recalibration. Comparison with Related Methods. HarmoFL [ 5 ] normalizes input amplitudes using shared amplitude statistics and applies weight perturbation to reduce model drift. Instance normalization [ 47 ] normalizes feature statistics without adapting the degree of style removal. FSR instead learns frequency-domain mixing weights, so the balance between content preservation and style removal is adapted during training rather than fixed. Existing prototype-based methods [ 10 , 11 ] typically construct and align prototypes using final-layer features. This approach overlooks the hierarchical information distributed across network layers, which captures both fine-grained tissue details and global anatomical context. Our conference version [ 24 ] introduced CDPA to fuse multi-level encoder and decoder features into a coupled representation. Although this fusion captures richer context, combining deep semantic cues and shallow structural details in one prototype introduces representational interference: semantic consensus and structural preservation compete within the shared representation, and averaging coupled prototypes tends to smooth anatomical contours. We examine this effect in Sec. IV-D3 . To address it, DCPA retains this hierarchical fusion and extends CDPA by constructing separate semantic and structural prototypes and aligning them independently. Multi-level Feature Integration. We extract features from K K selected levels of the encoder and decoder and use hierarchical fusion to guide shallower features with deeper ones. FedBCS [ 24 ] uses FedAvg [ 2 ] for server-side parameter aggregation, with weights proportional to each client’s sample count. S2PA instead derives the aggregation weights from representation differences across clients. Style-purified semantic and structural prototypes provide complementary measures of each client’s divergence from the global consensus, and this information can therefore guide aggregation. Prototype Divergence Computation. At the end of local training in each communication round, client m m uploads its style-purified semantic and structural prototypes along with its model parameters. The server applies FINCH clustering [ 48 ] separately to prototypes from the same stream, type, and class, and then averages the resulting centroids to obtain the corresponding global consensus prototype. Both are broadcast: the centroids serve as positive and negative anchors in Eqs. 11 and 12 , and the consensus prototypes enter the MSE term of Eq. 11 and the divergence in Eq. 14 . FedBCS+ achieves the highest mean Dice scores of 75.47%, 82.45%, 79.22%, and 80.78% on Histology, Prostate, BUS, and Polyp, respectively. It outperforms the conference version, FedBCS, on all five benchmarks. Segmentation Accuracy. As shown in Table III , FedBCS+ improves over FedAvg by 4.33, 3.31, 2.16, and 3.26 Dice points on the four main benchmarks, respectively. It also improves Dice by 1.03 points on 3D FeTS 2022 (Table IV ). General FL methods, including FedAvg, MOON, and HarmoFL, lack explicit cross-client prototype alignment. Prototype-based methods, including FedProto, FPL, and FedPLVM, align client representations but rely on single-layer features and conventional aggregation. FedBCS+ targets both limitations by combining the multi-level contextual alignment of DCPA with the prototype-guided weighting of S2PA. The qualitative examples in Fig. 4 are consistent with the quantitative results, with fewer missed regions and false positives for FedBCS+. Cross-client Consistency and Generalization. FedBCS+ also improves performance consistency across clients. Relative to FedAvg, σ Dice \sigma_{\text{Dice}} decreases on all four 2D benchmarks: from 4.20 to 3.51 on Histology, 4.62 to 3.52 on Prostate, 8.39 to 6.56 on BUS, and 10.47 to 7.71 on Polyp. FedBCS+ attains the lowest σ Dice \sigma_{\text{Dice}} among all methods on BUS and Polyp and the third lowest on Histology and Prostate. This agrees with the design of S2PA, which gives more weight to clients that are under-represented in the global consensus. Table V reports the LOSO results on Histology. FedBCS+ achieves the highest average Dice score of 69.97%, compared with 67.92% for FedBCS and at most 67.14% for the other methods in Table V . These results indicate better transfer to unseen imaging domains. Comparison with the Conference Version. FedBCS+ extends FedBCS in two respects. (1) Decoupled prototype alignment. DCPA replaces coupled prototype alignment with separate semantic and structural branches, so that regional and contour cues are aligned separately. In the standalone alignment comparison on Histology (Sec. IV-C2 ), this change increases Dice from 71.50% to 72.23%. (2) Adaptive parameter aggregation. Weighting clients by their semantic–structural prototype divergence provides the largest standalone gain of 2.67 Dice points in Table VI . Together, these extensions yield gains over FedBCS ranging from 0.70 Dice points on Prostate to 1.83 points on Histology, with 1.10 points on FeTS 2022. Feature Separability. Fig. 5 visualizes pixel-level foreground and background encoder features using t-SNE [ 71 ] to examine the representations learned by FedBCS+. Compared with FedAvg, FedBCS+ produces more separable features on every benchmark. The silhouette score rises most on Histology (from 0.20 to 0.61) and Polyp (from 0.16 to 0.51), and least on FeTS 2022 (from 0.78 to 0.92), where the FedAvg features are already well separated.
