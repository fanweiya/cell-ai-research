Figure 7: Localization produced by the PixelCAM WSOL model after SFDA-SPF and the state-of-the-art SFDA methods ( GlaS → \rightarrow CAMELYON17-3 ). 4 Conclusion This paper addresses dominant-class prediction bias, a critical challenge in source-free adaptation for WSOL models in histopathology. We introduced SFDA-SPF , a new SFDA method that corrects this bias through complementary forget and retain objectives. The forget set contains uncertain samples currently assigned to dominant classes and likely to lie near the target decision boundary. The proposed forgetting objective reduces the probability assigned to their current dominant class, allowing under-predicted classes to emerge and progressively reducing the skew in the target prediction distribution. In parallel, the retain objective preserves the predictions of remaining target samples through cross-entropy supervision, thereby maintaining useful target structure and limiting excessive model drift. Experiments show that SFDA-SPF achieves the best average localization performance across all evaluated WSOL architectures while improving average classification accuracy. Appendix A Dataset Construction and Experimental Protocol A.1 CAMELYON17 Dataset Construction In this work, we do not use the standard CAMELYON17 [ Litjens et al., 2018 ] classification benchmark. Instead, we construct a patch-level version of CAMELYON17 specifically designed for Weakly Supervised Object Localization (WSOL). Each extracted patch is associated with an image-level normal/tumor label used for classification and WSOL training, while the corresponding pixel-level tumor annotation is retained exclusively for localization evaluation. Our construction protocol closely follows the patch-sampling strategy introduced in [ Rony et al., 2023 ] for CAMELYON16 [ Rony et al., 2023 , Guichemerre et al., 2025 ] . Importantly, patches originating from the five acquisition centers of CAMELYON17 are kept as separate domains, resulting in five domain-specific datasets, denoted C17-0 to C17-4 . This construction enables controlled source-to-target experiments across acquisition centers while preserving the same tissue type and prediction task. A.1.1 Patch Extraction Procedure Sampling of Metastatic Patches Metastatic patches are sampled exclusively from metastatic whole-slide images (WSIs) and only in regions surrounding cancerous tissue. Each sampled patch is associated with both an image-level label and a pixel-level annotation mask derived from the WSI annotations. A.3 EBHI Corruption Benchmark To extend our evaluation to a challenging multi-class setting, we construct a new benchmark for SFDA and WSOL in colorectal histopathology based on EBHI Shi et al. Corruption Types : To construct controlled target-domain shifts, we follow the Histopath-C benchmark [ Noori et al., 2026 ] , which introduces histopathology-specific synthetic corruptions designed to mimic distribution shifts encountered in digital pathology. Histopath-C groups ten corruption types into five categories: staining , contamination , blurring , noise , and illumination . We apply each corruption independently to the held-out EBHI-T subset, resulting in 11 distinct target domains. The source domain remains composed essentially of clean images, ensuring that the observed distribution shift arises only from the applied corruption. A.1 CAMELYON17 Dataset Construction In this work, we do not use the standard CAMELYON17 [ Litjens et al., 2018 ] classification benchmark. Instead, we construct a patch-level version of CAMELYON17 specifically designed for Weakly Supervised Object Localization (WSOL). Each extracted patch is associated with an image-level normal/tumor label used for classification and WSOL training, while the corresponding pixel-level tumor annotation is retained exclusively for localization evaluation. Our construction protocol closely follows the patch-sampling strategy introduced in [ Rony et al., 2023 ] for CAMELYON16 [ Rony et al., 2023 , Guichemerre et al., 2025 ] . Importantly, patches originating from the five acquisition centers of CAMELYON17 are kept as separate domains, resulting in five domain-specific datasets, denoted C17-0 to C17-4 . This construction enables controlled source-to-target experiments across acquisition centers while preserving the same tissue type and prediction task. B.2 Experimental Settings B.2.1 SFDA Methods We compare our proposed method against five representative SFDA baselines covering different adaptation strategies. • SHOT [ Liang et al., 2020 ] exploits the structure of the target domain through pseudo-label refinement, with a diversity objective (information maximization loss) that discourages predictions from collapsing toward a subset of classes. • SFDA-DE [ Ding et al., 2022a ] estimates source feature distributions from the source model classifier and reliable target features, and uses the surrogate source representations to reduce the source–target distribution discrepancy. • CDCL [ Wang et al., 2022 ] relies essentially on confident pseudo-labels and performs cross-domain contrastive learning using class prototypes (from the source classifier), encouraging features from the same pseudo-labels to remain close while separating features associated with different classes. • ERL [ Yi et al., 2023 ] exploits prediction uncertainty through entropy-based regularization to progressively refine the target model without accessing source samples. • RGV [ Zhu et al., 2025 ] leverages a memory-based mechanism to exploit relationships among target samples during adaptation. B.2.2 Hyperparameter Selection and Validation Protocol Hyperparameter selection constitutes a key challenge in SFDA. While traditionally SFDA is performed without any source or target annotations, benchmarking additionally requires a model-selection protocol for choosing hyperparameters such as the learning rate, number of epochs, and method-specific parameters. Previous work has highlighted that model selection remains an unresolved issue in domain adaptation. Different protocols are used across the literature [ Ericsson et al., 2023 ] . Historically, oracle selection based on target performance has been used in several adaptation methods, while other works rely on source validation data or dedicated validation sets [ Guichemerre et al., 2024 , Sharafi et al., 2025 , Xu et al., 2025 ] . In this work, we adopt an explicit validation-assisted protocol. The target training set used by all the SFDA methods remains entirely unlabeled, and no target annotation is involved in the adaptation objective. Hyperparameters and checkpoints are instead selected using a separate labeled validation split, while the test set remains strictly unseen and is accessed only for the final evaluation. This protocol is consistent with validation strategies considered in recent SFDA methods. For example, [ Boudiaf et al., 2023 ] explicitly use a validation set for hyperparameter and model selection when evaluating SFDA approaches. More recently, [ Xu et al., 2025 ] investigate a similar validation-assisted setting by manually annotating 300 samples from the target domain to form a small validation set for hyperparameter selection. Our protocol follows such previous work and our adaptation remains source-free and is performed only on unlabeled target training data, whereas a separate validation set is used only to determine the experimental configuration. Figure 13 provides an explanation for the more modest gains observed with transformer-based WSOL models compared with CNN-based models. Unlike PixelCAM , for which uncertain dominant-class predictions are enriched in misclassification errors, SAT produces a highly concentrated low-entropy distribution. Figure 13: Entropy distributions of correctly and incorrectly classified samples predicted as cancer by SAT. Consequently, many erroneous predictions remain highly confident and are assigned to the retain set, making entropy less effective at separating reliable from incorrect predictions. Despite this less favorable confidence structure, SFDA-SPF improves SAT from 21.3 to 30.3 PxAP and from 52.1 to 69.2 CL on average. Its benefit is particularly pronounced for localization, exceeding the strongest competing SFDA method by 7.9 PxAP points.
