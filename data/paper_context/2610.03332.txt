II Materials and Methods II-A Datasets and Annotations Two rat datasets from the CA1 region of the hippocampus were used. Dataset A was acquired from a healthy rat and served as the control, and Dataset B was acquired from a rat after pilocarpine-induced status epilepticus. Both were imaged at an in-plane resolution of 15 × 15 15\times 15 nm with a 40 nm sectioning interval, yielding anisotropic stacks of 3100 × 3100 3100\times 3100 pixels per slice. After removing unusable border regions, the retained volumes spanned approximately 46.5 × 46.5 × 35.7 ​ μ ​ m 3 46.5\times 46.5\times 35.7\ \mu\mathrm{m}^{3} (Dataset A, 8.6 GB uncompressed) and 46.5 × 46.5 × 22.8 ​ μ ​ m 3 46.5\times 46.5\times 22.8\ \mu\mathrm{m}^{3} (Dataset B, 5.5 GB uncompressed). Animal procedures were approved by the Committee of the Provincial Government of Southern Finland and followed European Community Council Directives 86/609/EEC. The main branch produces per-slice dendrite masks with a SAM variant fine-tuned for slice-wise dendrite delineation [ 15 ] . Z.Z. created 2D sparse object-mask annotations of Dataset A (100 annotated slices, 2168 2D dendrite instances), and A.S., an expert in neuroanatomy, validated them. These annotations were used to train the automatic YOLOv6 detector, taking each bounding-box label as the enclosing rectangle of the corresponding mask. The RF 3D linking classifier and the nnU-Net tissue classifier were trained on 30 slices of sparse 3D instance annotation from Dataset A (27 3D instances). Specifically, we used the inter-slice instance links derived from these labels for the RF classifier, and for the nnU-Net we binarized the same 30 slices into background and dendrite classes for 2D training. For evaluation, Z.Z. prepared sparse 3D instance-level annotations on both Dataset A and Dataset B (slices 452–461 of Dataset A and 285–294 of Dataset B). The Dataset A evaluation slices did not overlap with any slice used to train YOLOv6, the fine-tuned SAM, the RF linker, or nnU-Net, and no Dataset B slice was used at any training stage. II-B Preprocessing Before segmentation, the stacks were aligned to reduce inter-slice displacement using the StackReg plugin in ImageJ [ 20 ] on downsampled 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 stacks. The slice-wise transformations were then scaled and applied to the original-resolution slices, giving consistent stacks at both resolutions. Two aligned representations were used. For YOLO-guided SAM segmentation the stack was downsampled to 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 pixels, while the original-resolution stack was retained for high-resolution refinement. II-C YOLO-Guided SAM Segmentation The pipeline operates in two coordinated branches, as shown in Fig. 1 . We select YOLOv6-L [ 21 ] to balance model capacity and inference efficiency and train it for 400 epochs using stochastic gradient descent with a cosine learning-rate schedule, combining GIoU box-regression loss, distribution focal loss, and a classification loss. The detector is applied to the downsampled and aligned EM slices to detect candidate dendrites in each slice. Each predicted bounding box is used as a separate prompt to SAM, producing one binary mask per box. The resulting per-box masks are then assembled into a single 2D instance map for the slice. II-D Iterative 2D Mask Refinement Since SAM processes each bounding-box prompt independently, initial masks contain artifacts such as spurious components, holes, missed detections, and split labels. We reduce these through an iterative procedure alternating between mask post-processing and supplementary SAM re-prompting. In each iteration, masks are post-processed slice by slice, where each non-background label is decomposed into 4-connected components, components with area smaller than 500 pixels are removed, and all enclosed holes are filled. A harmonization step then evaluates nearby component pairs from different labels using four criteria, namely spatial proximity, normalized contact strength, overlap after dilation, and background gap thickness, merging pairs that satisfy the criteria. Additionally, foreground regions present in slices z + 1 z+1 and z + 2 z+2 but absent in slice z z are converted into supplementary bounding-box prompts for the next SAM iteration. This cycle is iterated three times. II-E 3D Instance Linking Given the refined 2D instance mask stack, an RF classifier [ 22 ] assigns a linking probability to each candidate inter-slice component pair, converting independent slice-wise masks into coherent volumetric dendrite instances. Training data are derived from the sparse 3D instance annotations, in which each dendrite carries a consistent label across slices. Within each slice, every annotated instance is split into its 2D connected components, and candidate links are formed between components on adjacent slices whose pixel overlap exceeds a minimum threshold. A link is labeled positive when both components belong to the same instance. The remaining features are the intersection over union (IoU), IoU i ​ j = o i ​ j / ( A i + A j − o i ​ j ) \mathrm{IoU}_{ij}=o_{ij}/(A_{i}+A_{j}-o_{ij}) , the area ratio r i ​ j = min ⁡ ( A i , A j ) / max ⁡ ( A i , A j ) r_{ij}=\min(A_{i},A_{j})/\max(A_{i},A_{j}) , the centroid displacement ( Δ ​ y , Δ ​ x ) (\Delta y,\Delta x) , and the Euclidean centroid distance d i ​ j = ‖ μ i − μ j ‖ 2 d_{ij}=\|\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\|_{2} , with μ i \mu_{i} and μ j \mu_{j} the component centroids. At inference, the trained classifier assigns a linking probability to each candidate edge between adjacent slices, and edges with probability above a threshold p th p_{\mathrm{th}} are retained. Retained edges are resolved into 3D instances with a union-find pass over the component graph, so a dendrite that branches within the volume is kept as a single connected instance instead of being split at the branch point. II-F High-Resolution Refinement Downsampling reduces the effective in-plane resolution from approximately 15 × 15 15\times 15 nm 2 to approximately 45 × 45 45\times 45 nm 2 per pixel, weakening thin spine necks and fine protrusions. To recover these structures, nnU-Net [ 8 ] is applied at the original resolution after RF-based 3D linking. The downsampled instance segmentation is upscaled to the original resolution by nearest-neighbor interpolation, which preserves discrete instance identities. Independently, nnU-Net produces a high-resolution dendrite probability volume for the whole stack. The recovery step combines the two, using the probability volume as tissue evidence and the upscaled instances to assign recovered voxels to the correct dendrite. We train a binary 2D nnU-Net on the 30 annotated original-resolution slices. nnU-Net self-configures the training setup from the dataset properties, selecting Z-score normalization, a patch size of 1280 × 1024 1280\times 1024 , and a batch size of 2. Recovery is performed on all linked instances. Each instance is cropped to its 3D bounding box, and within each slice the probability volume is thresholded at 0.98 and split into 4-connected components. Any component overlapping the instance on that slice is taken as evidence for the same dendrite and added to its mask. Because recovery is additive, unioning components without removing voxels, the instance identity is retained while thin, high-confidence protrusions are reattached at full resolution. After recovery, a set of post-processing steps is applied to consolidate the final 3D instances. Full implementation details are released as open-source code. III Experiments and Results III-A Evaluation Protocol We evaluated on Dataset A (control) and Dataset B (epileptic), each on 10 sparsely annotated slices. Dense 3D instance ground truth (GT) is infeasible to produce manually at scale, so each reference was annotated sparsely. To reduce annotation time, the pipeline output was used as an initialization. Each instance was then manually refined pixel by pixel on every annotated slice, and dendrites missed by the pipeline were annotated from scratch. This limits the dependence of the reference on the method, although some bias toward the pipeline output, mainly at ambiguous boundaries, cannot be fully excluded and was accepted as a trade-off against annotation time. The low instance-level scores on Dataset B further indicate that the reference did not simply inherit the pipeline’s instance decisions. Evaluation followed a sparse GT strategy consistent with the general recommendations of [ 23 ] , in which the union of annotated reference voxels defined the evaluated region, and predicted instances lying entirely outside it were ignored rather than counted as false positives, since they might be valid but unannotated structures. This strategy underlies all metrics defined below. All metrics were computed on 3D label volumes following established recommendations for biomedical image segmentation evaluation [ 23 , 24 ] . We compared the RF linker against a purely geometric baseline that removes the classifier and establishes inter-slice correspondence from raw overlap alone, operating on the same refined 2D instance masks. Table II reports the comparison. The RF linker outperformed the geometric baseline on both datasets, with the largest margin on Dataset A (healthy tissue, S inst S_{\mathrm{inst}} 0.63 against 0.33). The gap narrowed on Dataset B (epileptic tissue, S inst S_{\mathrm{inst}} 0.38 against 0.25), where both linkers were constrained by the upstream detection failures described above rather than by the scoring of individual edges. On control tissue the baseline scores were identical at S inst ​ @ ​ 0.5 S_{\mathrm{inst}}@0.5 and S inst ​ @ ​ 0.75 S_{\mathrm{inst}}@0.75 (0.29 in both cases), so the instances it did assemble were delineated accurately and its loss came from tracks it failed to assemble at all, not from imprecise ones. As a result, learned scoring contributed most where recognition was otherwise reliable, by sustaining tracks through the slice-to-slice variation that a fixed overlap criterion rejects. Although RF linkage outperformed the geometric baseline, it comes with a practical limitation. Six of its eight features are expressed in absolute pixel units, so a classifier trained at one working resolution cannot be applied at another without retraining, whereas an overlap ratio is scale-free. Two properties of the baseline IoU method also bound the comparison. It matches components one to one, so a dendrite that branches within the volume is split at the branch point, and it uses a single overlap threshold that we did not tune. The result consequently establishes that the learned scorer helps at the operating point tested, not that no geometric rule could match it. IV Discussion and Conclusion We presented a fully automatic pipeline for 3D dendrite instance segmentation in SBF-SEM, integrating automatic detection, promptable 2D segmentation, iterative refinement, RF 3D linking, and instance-aware high-resolution refinement into a single system that operates without manual prompting at inference. On control tissue it reconstructs coherent, well-separated dendrites with high semantic accuracy (Dice 0.93) and strong instance-level performance. On the epileptic tissue, sparse semantic recovery remains high and segmentation quality moderate while detection and instance-level scores fall, which locates the dominant errors in recognizing and separating dendrites rather than in delineating them. An ablation of the linking stage shows that the learned edge scorer is a substantive component instead of an incidental one, since replacing it with fixed-threshold overlap matching roughly halves the instance score on control tissue. Volume EM resolves fine neurites at nanometer scale but is restricted to small volumes by acquisition time and data size [ 6 ] . Reducing the manual effort per EM volume, as pursued here, extends nanometer-scale reconstruction to more tissue in the meantime. These results come from one animal per condition, and the RF linker and nnU-Net were trained on only 30 annotated slices of Dataset A, so generalization to other animals and acquisition protocols remains to be established. Transfer is nonetheless plausible, because no stage was trained on epileptic tissue, yet segmentation quality declined far less than detection on Dataset B, and the RF linker relies on geometric rather than appearance features. The modularity of the pipeline points to several concrete next steps. We attribute the performance drop to detection and not delineation, which leads to the adaptation of the detector, either by fine-tuning YOLOv6 on epileptic tissue or by training a separate detector per condition. Validation on more animals and on densely annotated subvolumes, which would also allow precision to be measured, is needed to confirm these results. Another direction extends the method toward its intended downstream use. Because the high-resolution stage reattaches thin protrusions at native resolution, these protrusions provide the substrate on which individual dendritic spines can be identified and separated, enabling the spine-level morphological analysis this reconstruction is meant to support.
