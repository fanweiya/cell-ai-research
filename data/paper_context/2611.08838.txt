Despite differences in embedding dimension, training regimen and patch size, APL showed similar performances across encoders. UNI consistently showed the strongest overall results. Prov-GigaPath achieved lower AUROC (0.83–0.88) and AUPRC (0.58–0.65), while CONCH reached AUROC of 0.84–0.89 and AUPRC values of 0.57–0.61. We also observe embedding space alignment in held-out patches between UNI and Prov-GigaPath, both trained using DINOv2 (256 px patches), with a calibrated linear CKA of 0.78 capturing global alignment and mKNN of 0.52 (calibrated 0.51; within-slide null median 0.03; p ≤ 0.005 p\leq 0.005 ) capturing similarity in local neighbourhood structure. Alignment was similar for artefact and clean patches (calibrated CKA 0.78 vs 0.78; mKNN 0.46 vs 0.51). Lastly, we tested our initial assumptions in held-out WSIs. Angular distance consistently distinguished artefacts from clean tissue (AUROC 0.83–0.84), predominantly identifying blurry patches, whereas Mahalanobis distance (AUROC 0.47–0.86) and kNN density (AUROC 0.54–0.81) were less consistent. Artefact detection is more challenging in a multi-centre cohort: To evaluate under more heterogeneous staining and preparation conditions, we tested APL on a multi-institutional cohort. Fitted on TCGA-OV, the UNI-based APL classifier reached an AUROC of 0.84 and an AUPRC of 0.54, below HistoART (AUROC 0.92, AUPRC 0.63), but above GrandQC (AUROC 0.73, 0.37) (Table 3 ). Transferring APL fitted on the single-centre cohort to TCGA-OV, reduced performance to 0.75 AUROC and 0.40 AUPRC. The underlying assumptions were also less consistent: angular distance retained some discriminative ability (AUROC 0.75), whereas Mahalanobis distance and local density performed poorly (0.39 and 0.54, respectively). Composition of artefact types also differed in this cohort: background (73%) and blood (17%) accounted for most artefacts. We show that we can remove artefacts with high AUPRC in a single-centre cohort and find that certain artefacts are more easily detected than others. Different encoders appear to converge to similar representations of artefacts. Lastly, we find that our approach is less consistent in multi-centre cohorts, which we assume may be due to acquisition protocol differences. Because APL relies on embedding space topology rather than explicit definitions of artefacts, atypical tissue patterns may also be flagged, which we observe in the case of necrosis and blood, the latter is an artefact under [ 12 ] . These may deviate from the dominant tissue distribution but are not necessarily technical artefacts. This leads to a broader discussion on how we define artefacts and the impact of removal of certain types of patches on performance in downstream tasks. Ultimately, APL provides a lightweight, cohort-centric approach to artefact detection. Flagged patches can then be excluded from slide-level representations to prevent artefacts from influencing downstream analyses.
