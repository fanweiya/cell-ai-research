Diffusion models for histopathology generation. Diffusion-based generative modeling for histopathology spans large-image and multi-scale synthesis [ 33 , 21 , 75 ] . Other works incorporate text or mask conditioning for guided synthesis like pathology reports [ 74 ] , cell topology [ 71 ] , and joint nuclei image–label co-synthesis [ 47 ] . PathDiff [ 6 ] conditions on unpaired text and mask; like ZoomLDM [ 76 ] and PixCell [ 75 ] , it is a conditional VAE-latent method. At inference it accepts text, a mask, or both, and provides no unconditional branch, so it cannot sample without a conditioning signal. While digital histopathology routinely leverages pretrained VFMs for downstream tasks (classification, segmentation, retrieval), no current state-of-the-art histopathology generation model exploits these representations as the generative latent space. Therefore, STREAM intends to focus on purely unconditional histopathology generation: a diffusion model that faithfully learns the histopathology data distribution from a VFM-encoded latent space without external conditioning. Latent space modification in diffusion model training. VFMs have emerged as feature extractors for generative models via two paradigms. Paradigm 1 uses VFM features as training losses: REPA [ 77 ] aligns DiT features with DINOv2 [ 49 ] , REPA-E [ 37 ] extends to end-to-end VAE tuning, and others similarly leverage VFM representations [ 55 , 73 , 65 ] . Paradigm 2 uses VFM features as the latent space: RAE [ 79 ] trains DiTs directly on representation-encoder features with noise-augmented decoders, SVG [ 54 ] similarly uses frozen DINOv3 [ 56 ] features, concatenated with a trained residual branch, and related works explore fine-tuning representation encoders under a semantic-preservation loss [ 58 , 12 ] . Decoder regularization via masking or denoising losses has been shown beneficial for jointly-trained tokenizers [ 72 , 52 , 31 ] , but Yang et al. [72] explicitly note that gains depend on joint encoder–decoder training. We adopt Paradigm 2 because histopathology-specific VFMs [ 14 , 80 , 70 ] produce ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -normalized features naturally suited to Riemannian geometry. STREAM differs from approaches that jointly adapt the tokenizer encoder–decoder to improve reconstruction fidelity [ 12 , 37 ] by keeping the histopathology VFM frozen and instead recovering diffusion-friendliness via geometry: a stochastic bridge on 𝒮 d − 1 \mathcal{S}^{d-1} paired with anisotropic decoder regularization ( Section 4.3 ). 4 Method: STREAM Figure 1: Overview of STREAM. We train sequentially in two stages on 8 × \times NVIDIA H200 GPUs. In Stage 1, we train the LightningDiT-XL [ 73 ] ( ∼ 676 {\sim}676 M parameters) with the extracted UNI patch embeddings for 95k steps with batch size 1024 using the stochastic bridge FM loss ( Algorithm 1 ). In Stage 2, we extract the cached SVD of the velocity-field Jacobian from the trained DiT first, and use it to train a ViT-XL decoder [ 79 , 17 ] from scratch with anisotropic noise for 90k steps with a batch size of 512. We train all baselines on BRCA and COADREAD with the default hyperparameters and settings of their respective papers. PixCell’s design prevents unconditional generation: it always requires an input image (or a VFM embedding extracted from one) at inference. Forced to generate from its learned latent space alone, PixCell reports extremely high gFID values, consistent with the measured conditioning-dominance ratio ρ cond = 62 % \rho_{\text{cond}}{=}62\% . ZoomLDM partially sidesteps this requirement by training a conditional diffusion model directly on the VFM embedding, allowing unconditional sampling without an external image; yet its gFID is higher than STREAM’s. Both results indicate that the VAE-induced latent space is too semantically poor to support true generative modeling, even where reconstruction remains strong. STREAM instead treats the VFM embedding as the latent space itself, without external conditioning, outperforming its histopathology-domain counterparts. However, STREAM’s performance is not solely due to UNI being utilized as the latent space: pure RFM without STREAM’s bridge × \times anisotropic-decoder design actually performs worse than ZoomLDM in both reconstruction and generation ( Table 5 , 45k decoder steps). STREAM also outperforms the VFM-latent baselines RAE and SVG, and because the UNI encoder is held fixed across the table, that margin belongs to the stochastic-bridge DiT and the anisotropic decoder rather than to the latent space. Furthermore, STREAM at half NFE ( NFE = 26 \mathrm{NFE}{=}26 ) still beats RAE and SVG at the full NFE ( NFE = 50 \mathrm{NFE}{=}50 ) ( Section D.2 ). STREAM is not state of the art on reconstruction. SVG attains the best rFID on both datasets, and on COADREAD both PixCell and ZoomLDM also reconstruct better than STREAM does. However, that advantage does not carry over to generation, where these models have higher gFID than STREAM. STREAM therefore combines state-of-the-art generation with reasonable reconstruction, as generative models should strive to achieve high generation fidelity first, then aim for a minimal reconstruction trade-off. The histopathology-aware FvD, KvD and vMMD do not always rank methods the same way as the ImageNet-pretrained FID, KID and CMMD, so we report both families. Regardless, STREAM ranks first on nearly all of the generative metrics. The same holds on the independent non-TCGA SPIDER-skin dataset, where all six methods are retrained from scratch on UNI ( Section E.1 ). We further evaluate STREAM-generated images for biological fidelity in two additional ways. First, we measure nuclear morphometry directly on the generated images, and STREAM reproduces the quantitative nuclear morphometry metrics of real tissue. Second, we test the prospective downstream application of STREAM, which is data augmentation for training self-supervised foundation models. A proof-of-concept encoder pretrained only on STREAM-generated samples of BRCA and COADREAD is on par with one pretrained on real tiles on clinically relevant downstream classification and gene expression prediction tasks ( Appendix F ). The two components combine superadditively, and under a single shared UNI encoder STREAM attains state-of-the-art generation quality on three different histopathology datasets.
