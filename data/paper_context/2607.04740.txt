2.2 Pathology Foundation Models Large-scale self-supervised pretraining has produced powerful visual encoders for computational pathology. CTransPath (Wang et al. , 2022 ) pioneered pathology-specific pretraining with DINO on TCGA data. UNI (Chen et al. , 2024 ) and its successor UNI2 (Chen et al. , 2025 ) scaled DINOv2-based training to over 100 million patches, achieving strong cross-task generalization. Complementarily, CONCH (Lu et al. , 2024 ) adopted vision-language contrastive learning on pathology image-text pairs, capturing semantic-level information beyond pure morphological features. Pure vision models and vision-language models encode orthogonal information, and their concatenation has been shown to benefit downstream prediction tasks. 2.3 Drifting-Based Generative Models Deng et al. ( 2026 ) proposed the Drifting Model, a generative paradigm that enables single-step generation by shifting distributional evolution into training. Several concurrent works have since analyzed its theoretical foundations: Lai et al. ( 2026 ) , Turan and Ovsjanikov ( 2026 ) , and Li and Zhu ( 2026 ) connected the drifting field to score matching and flow matching, while He et al. ( 2026 ) proposed Sinkhorn-Drifting to improve training stability via entropic optimal transport. However, all existing drifting models operate in unconditional or class-conditional settings and none addresses paired prediction tasks such as spatial gene expression prediction. 3 Methodology 3.1 Preliminaries Notations. Let 𝐗 cnt ∈ ℕ N × G \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{cnt}}\in\mathbb{N}^{N\times G} be the raw ST count matrix for N N spatial locations (multi-cell spots or single cells) across G G genes, and 𝐒 ∈ ℝ N × 2 \mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 2} their spatial coordinates. We model the log1p -normalized expression 𝐗 = log ⁡ ( 1 + 𝐗 cnt ) ∈ ℝ N × G \mathbf{X}=\log(1+\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{cnt}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times G} , while the raw counts 𝐗 cnt \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{cnt}} are retained for the ZINB likelihood (Sec. 3.2). The H&E histology image is 𝐈 ∈ ℝ H × W × 3 \mathbf{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3} , from which we crop a localized patch 𝐈 s ∈ ℝ P × P × 3 \mathbf{I}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times P\times 3} at location s s . Our task is to infer the expression profile 𝐱 ∈ ℝ G \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{G} (in log1p space) from 𝐈 s \mathbf{I}_{s} , with 𝐱 cnt ∈ ℕ G \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{cnt}}\in\mathbb{N}^{G} its corresponding raw counts. Overview. DriftST is a unified generative framework that directly learns the distributional drift from the H&E morphology distribution p H&E p_{\text{H\&E}} to the ST expression distribution p ST p_{\text{ST}} , combining the fidelity of generative modeling with the efficiency of a single forward pass. At its core, a Cellular Drifting model (Figure 1 ) transports the H&E-conditioned source distribution to the target p ST ​ ( 𝐱 ) p_{\text{ST}}(\mathbf{x}) . By minimizing this drift field during training, the pushforward distribution q θ = [ f θ ​ ( 𝐈 s ) ] # ​ p H&E q_{\theta}=[f_{\theta}(\mathbf{I}_{s})]_{\#}p_{\text{H\&E}} is driven to match p ST p_{\text{ST}} , which removes the need for iterative sampling at inference Deng et al. ( 2026 ) . The generator f θ f_{\theta} is instantiated as the STransformer (Figure 2 ), which extracts morphological features from 𝐈 s \mathbf{I}_{s} while explicitly modeling the inter-gene dependencies and differential gene importance of the output space ℝ G \mathbb{R}^{G} . Cellular Drifting Field. To transport the pushforward distribution q θ q_{\theta} toward p ST p_{\mathrm{ST}} , we construct a cellular drifting field that decouples the interaction into a positive attraction and a negative repulsion, which prevents the single paired target from being overwhelmed by the abundant negatives. For a patch I s I_{s} , the generator produces a prediction 𝐱 = f θ ​ ( I s ) \mathbf{x}=f_{\theta}(I_{s}) . We define the positive set 𝒫 = { 𝐱 + } \mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{x}^{+}\} as the paired ground-truth profile of I s I_{s} (hence | 𝒫 | = 1 |\mathcal{P}|=1 ), and the negative set 𝒩 \mathcal{N} as the union of the K − 1 K{-}1 other stochastic dropout predictions of the same patch and a pool of historical predictions sampled from a prediction bank; the prediction’s own copy is excluded from 𝒩 \mathcal{N} , so it is never repelled from itself. To stabilize the vector field in the high-dimensional space ℝ D \mathbb{R}^{D} , we apply a global distance scaling factor σ d = max ⁡ ( 1 D ​ 𝔼 ​ [ d ] , ϵ ) \sigma_{d}=\max(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\,\mathbb{E}[d],\epsilon) , where 𝔼 ​ [ d ] \mathbb{E}[d] is the mean Euclidean distance from 𝐱 \mathbf{x} to all samples in 𝒫 ∪ 𝒩 \mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{N} , and denote the scaled features as 𝐱 ~ = 𝐱 / σ d \tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}/\sigma_{d} . We compute the field with a multi-scale formulation over a set of bandwidth ratios ℛ \mathcal{R} (e.g., ℛ = { 0.02 , 0.05 , 0.2 } \mathcal{R}=\{0.02,0.05,0.2\} ), so that both local and global structure are captured. For each r ∈ ℛ r\in\mathcal{R} , the effective temperature is τ r = r ​ D \tau_{r}=r\sqrt{D} , and the attraction and repulsion weights are computed independently via softmax: w pos ( r ) ​ ( 𝐱 + ) \displaystyle w^{(r)}_{\mathrm{pos}}(\mathbf{x}^{+}) = softmax ​ ( − ‖ 𝐱 ~ − 𝐱 ~ + ‖ 2 τ r ) , ∀ 𝐱 + ∈ 𝒫 , \displaystyle=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(-\frac{\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{+}\|^{2}}{\tau_{r}}\right),\quad\forall\,\mathbf{x}^{+}\in\mathcal{P}, (1) w neg ( r ) ​ ( 𝐱 − ) \displaystyle w^{(r)}_{\mathrm{neg}}(\mathbf{x}^{-}) = softmax ​ ( − ‖ 𝐱 ~ − 𝐱 ~ − ‖ 2 τ r ) , ∀ 𝐱 − ∈ 𝒩 . \displaystyle=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(-\frac{\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{-}\|^{2}}{\tau_{r}}\right),\quad\forall\,\mathbf{x}^{-}\in\mathcal{N}. (3) Drifting Loss Optimization. With the multi-scale, split-form velocity field V ​ ( 𝐱 ) V(\mathbf{x}) established, we cast training as a self-consistent iterative optimization: rather than optimizing over a static error surface, the network updates its predictions toward an evolving target guided by the biological drifting field. For a current prediction 𝐱 \mathbf{x} and its scaled counterpart 𝐱 ~ \tilde{\mathbf{x}} , we define the local goal state as an incremental drift step with step-size hyperparameter η \eta : 𝐱 ~ goal = stopgrad ​ ( 𝐱 ~ + η ​ V ​ ( 𝐱 ) ) \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{goal}}=\text{stopgrad}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}+\eta V(\mathbf{x})) The stopgrad operator is crucial here: by freezing the target coordinate, it prevents the network from minimizing the objective through representation collapse or trivial identity shortcuts. The generator f θ f_{\theta} is then optimized to contract the distance between its prediction and this stabilized goal, using an MSE drift loss in the scaled feature space: ℒ drift = 𝔼 𝐈 s ∼ p H&E ​ [ ‖ 𝐱 ~ − 𝐱 ~ goal ‖ 2 2 ] \mathcal{L}_{\text{drift}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{I}_{s}\sim p_{\text{H\&E}}}\left[||\tilde{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{goal}}||_{2}^{2}\right] Minimizing ℒ drift \mathcal{L}_{\text{drift}} implicitly establishes a contraction mapping that progressively drives the velocity field toward an equilibrium where V ​ ( 𝐱 ) → 0 V(\mathbf{x})\to 0 . As shown in Figure 1 c, this objective reshapes the pushforward distribution over successive epochs until it mirrors the true transcriptomic landscape. Consequently, iterative sampling and stochastic path integration are entirely bypassed at inference: DriftST needs only a single deterministic forward pass to generate high-fidelity spatial expression profiles. Unlike standard architectures that treat output dimensions as independent, it is explicitly designed to capture the inter-dependencies and heterogeneous informativeness inherent to spatial transcriptomics. The network first encodes a patch and its microenvironment into a unified spot representation, then unfolds it into gene-specific tokens that are progressively refined by co-expression attention and gene-wise gating before being decoded into the final expression profile. Gene Residual Gate. Since the transcriptomic landscape is highly uneven, with stable housekeeping genes requiring minimal processing compared to complex biomarker genes, we introduce a progressive Gene Residual Gate (Figure 2 d). The gated residual update then modulates the per-gene contribution of each block: 𝐗 ( l + 1 ) = 𝐗 ( l ) + g l ⊙ ℱ l ​ ( 𝐗 ( l ) ) , \mathbf{X}^{(l+1)}=\mathbf{X}^{(l)}+g_{l}\odot\mathcal{F}_{l}(\mathbf{X}^{(l)}), (10) so that informative, tightly-coupled genes receive deeper refinement while stable genes pass through with near-identity updates, which stabilizes optimization across the heterogeneous output space. Additional ablation of parameters is provided in Appendix B 5 Conclusion We presented DriftST, a unified framework for inferring spatially resolved gene expression from H&E histology at both spot- and cell-level resolution. It reformulates the task as Cellular Drifting, a generative model that predicts in a single forward pass via an STransformer with co-expression attention and a gene residual gate, achieving SOTA results across four datasets while preserving the heterogeneity that regression baselines over-smooth. This faithful recovery of spatial variance ensures the reliability of downstream biological discoveries. DriftST also has limitations that point to future work. Its predictions are restricted to the genes in the training panel rather than the full transcriptome, and cross-patient generalization under heterogeneous tissue conditions and strong batch effects, as observed on Kidney Visium, remains challenging. Extending the framework to transcriptome-wide prediction, jointly modeling multiple slides and patients to learn domain-invariant features, and integrating complementary modalities such as spatial multi-omics are promising directions for broadening its applicability, moving a step closer to practical computational pathology.
