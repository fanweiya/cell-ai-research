3 Method Our model is shown in Figure 1 . It takes two grayscale images: the reference (fixed) image S a S_{a} and the moving image S b S_{b} to be registered onto it. In our evaluation domain these are a fingerprint master template and an impression of the same finger, pairs that synthetic generators produce natively ( Section 4 ). Weight-shared feature extractors produce feature maps v a v_{a} and v b v_{b} , which a Siamese matching module converts into two correlation maps: at spatial coordinate ( i , j ) (i,j) , c a ​ b c_{ab} holds the similarity between v a v_{a} at ( i , j ) (i,j) and all features of v b v_{b} , with c b ​ a c_{ba} defined symmetrically. Both maps are processed by identical network branches, flattened, and concatenated to estimate the transformation parameters. The spatial transformation is realized by a dense deformation field 𝚽 ∈ ℝ H × W × 2 \mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 2} ; however, to ensure smoothness and reduce complexity, we do not regress this field directly. Instead it is parameterized by a sparse control-point grid 𝐏 ∈ ℝ 2 × G × G \mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times G\times G} : for each control point P i P_{i} , i ∈ { 1 , … , G 2 } i\in\{1,...,G^{2}\} , the network regresses a localized translation Δ ​ 𝐩 i = ( Δ ​ x i , Δ ​ y i ) \Delta\mathbf{p}_{i}=(\Delta x_{i},\Delta y_{i}) , and the resulting 2 ​ G 2 2G^{2} parameters are upsampled via bicubic interpolation to the full-resolution field 𝚽 \mathbf{\Phi} . The objective is to find the field that maximizes structural overlap between S a S_{a} and the warped S b ^ S_{\hat{b}} , completely unsupervised: a soft Dice alignment loss [ 37 ] plus a smoothness regularizer ( Section 3.4 ) is back-propagated through the differentiable warp. 3.1 Feature extraction module Popular feature-extraction backbones such as DenseNet [ 38 ] , InceptionNet [ 39 ] , and ResNet [ 40 ] rely on max or average pooling, whereas spectral subsampling preserves considerably more spatial information per parameter while allowing flexible output dimensionality [ 9 ] . Two-dimensional spectral pooling can be based on the DFT [ 9 ] , DCT [ 11 ] , or Hartley transform [ 10 ] ; among these, the DCT has recurred in fingerprint pipelines [ 23 , 24 ] and, relying only on real-valued cosine functions rather than complex exponentials, produces output in the real domain ℝ \mathbb{R} . We implement our own 2D spectral pooling layer based on the 2D Type-II DCT. Our implementation achieves a significant computational speed-up by decomposing the 2D transform into sequential 1D transforms and pre-computing the spectral transformation bases via an identity-matrix mapping. This reframes the transform as a pure General Matrix Multiply (GEMM) operation, achieving a 50 × 50\times speedup on CUDA devices (details in Appendix C ). Rather than evaluating the standard separable Type-II DCT summation [ 41 ] (restated in Appendix C ) directly, our implementation computes the full 2D transform as a single bilinear matrix multiplication: Y = W H ​ X ​ W W ⊤ Y=W_{H}\,X\,W_{W}^{\top} (2) Here W H = 𝒟 1 ​ D ​ ( I H ) W_{H}=\mathcal{D}_{1D}(I_{H}) and W W = 𝒟 1 ​ D ​ ( I W ) W_{W}=\mathcal{D}_{1D}(I_{W}) , where 𝒟 1 ​ D \mathcal{D}_{1D} denotes the one-dimensional Type-II DCT operator, while I H ∈ ℝ H × H I_{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H} and I W ∈ ℝ W × W I_{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{W\times W} denote identity matrices. The inverse of the 2D Type-II DCT is well defined as the 2D Type-III DCT [ 41 ] ; thus the pooled and filtered representation in the frequency domain is transformed back to the spatial domain through the 2D Type-III DCT as the output of the layer. Figure 2: Reconstructions comparing max, average, and DCT spectral pooling on a fingerprint impression. DCT spectral pooling superiorly preserves fine spatial structure and supports arbitrary output dimensions. The layer thus shifts dimensionality reduction into the frequency domain: the Type-II DCT concentrates the structurally significant low-frequency content into the top-left of the frequency matrix; downsampling is a simple crop to the top-left h f ​ r ​ e ​ q × w f ​ r ​ e ​ q h_{freq}\times w_{freq} block; and the Type-III DCT reconstructs the spatial feature map at a chosen h o ​ u ​ t × w o ​ u ​ t h_{out}\times w_{out} . We maintain h f ​ r ​ e ​ q ≤ h o ​ u ​ t h_{freq}\leq h_{out} and w f ​ r ​ e ​ q ≤ w o ​ u ​ t w_{freq}\leq w_{out} , since the converse would discard retained frequencies during the inverse transform and render the crop wasteful. In each convolutional block we use a cropping ratio h f ​ r ​ e ​ q / h o ​ u ​ t ≈ w f ​ r ​ e ​ q / w o ​ u ​ t ≈ 0.85 h_{freq}/h_{out}\approx w_{freq}/w_{out}\approx 0.85 , avoiding the aggressive reduction of 2 × 2 2{\times}2 max or average pooling, which discards 75 % 75\% of the spacial samples per stage. Given an input pair S a , S b ∈ ℝ 1 × 192 × 192 S_{a},S_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 192\times 192} , the feature extraction module derives feature maps v a , v b ∈ ℝ 512 × 14 × 14 v_{a},v_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times 14\times 14} . 3.2 Siamese matching module While matching is often treated as an independent post-processing step [ 42 , 43 , 44 , 45 , 3 ] , the 2D correlation layer of [ 2 ] integrates matching information directly: acting as a normalized cross-correlation G : ℝ C × H × W × ℝ C × H × W → ℝ ( H ​ W ) × H × W G:\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}\times\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(HW)\times H\times W} , it flattens one feature map to ℝ C × N \mathbb{R}^{C\times N} ( N = H ​ W N=HW ) and computes, for each spatial feature of the other map, a dot product against all N N flattened features. While effective, this breaks all spatial axes of one input while preserving the other. For precise alignment of fine, repeating structure, preserving the spatial hierarchy of both images is critical, since the reference and moving images differ fundamentally in their photometric properties. Inspired by the 3D matching approach in Gum-Net [ 4 ] , we therefore propose a symmetric 2D Siamese correlation layer that computes two maps, C a ​ b C_{ab} and C b ​ a C_{ba} , processed in parallel. Given the two outputs of the feature extraction module ( Section 3.1 ), v a ∈ ℝ 512 × 14 × 14 v_{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times 14\times 14} (reference) and v b ∈ ℝ 512 × 14 × 14 v_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times 14\times 14} (moving), we obtain C a ​ b , C b ​ a ∈ ℝ 196 × 14 × 14 C_{ab},C_{ba}\in\mathbb{R}^{196\times 14\times 14} . The first correlation map, C a ​ b C_{ab} , preserves the spatial coordinates of v a v_{a} . Each element at channel k k (where k ∈ { 1 , … , N } k\in\{1,...,N\} indexes a flattened spatial location of v b v_{b} ) and spatial location ( h , w ) (h,w) of v a v_{a} is the normalized dot product, with C b ​ a C_{ba} defined symmetrically: ( C a ​ b ) k , h , w = ⟨ v a : , h , w , v b : k ⟩ ∑ ⟨ v a , v b ⟩ , ( C b ​ a ) k , h , w = ⟨ v b : , h , w , v a : k ⟩ ∑ ⟨ v b , v a ⟩ (C_{ab})_{k,h,w}=\frac{\left\langle v_{a_{:,h,w}},\,v_{b_{:k}}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\sum\left\langle v_{a},\,v_{b}\right\rangle}},\qquad(C_{ba})_{k,h,w}=\frac{\left\langle v_{b_{:,h,w}},\,v_{a_{:k}}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\sum\left\langle v_{b},\,v_{a}\right\rangle}} (3) The correlation maps C a ​ b , C b ​ a C_{ab},C_{ba} are fed into a pseudo-Siamese network of contiguous convolutional layers. Each map is convolved separately, then the branch outputs are concatenated and flattened to predict the dense deformation field. 3.3 Unsupervised non-linear alignment module Unlike the original Gum-Net [ 4 ] , which regresses a rigid 3D transformation suitable for macromolecular structures, our architecture predicts a dense deformation field 𝚽 \mathbf{\Phi} . The non-linear alignment module translates the correlation representations from Section 3.2 into a pixel-wise displacement field in two stages: control-point regression and dense flow-field generation. Control point regression: Each correlation branch is reduced by six successive 1024 1024 -channel valid convolutions to spatial size 2 × 2 2\times 2 and flattened to ℝ 4096 \mathbb{R}^{4096} ; the two branches are concatenated into a joint representation v c ​ o ​ r ​ r ∈ ℝ 8192 v_{corr}\in\mathbb{R}^{8192} that captures the geometric consensus (full architecture in Appendix A ). v c ​ o ​ r ​ r v_{corr} is fed into a multi-layer perceptron with two hidden layers of 2000 ReLU units. The final regressor head projects an output vector d ∈ ℝ 2 ​ G 2 d\in\mathbb{R}^{2G^{2}} , where G G is the resolution of the sparse control-point grid. We reshape d d with the operator ℛ : ℝ 2 ​ G 2 → ℝ 2 × G × G \mathcal{R}:\mathbb{R}^{2G^{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times G\times G} , mapping the linear activations onto their spatial coordinates on the control-point grid to obtain Δ ​ 𝐏 = ( Δ ​ x , Δ ​ y ) \Delta{\mathbf{P}}=(\Delta{x},\Delta{y}) . Dense flow field generation: To generate a smooth, topology-preserving dense deformation field, we upsample the coarse control points to the input resolution ( H , W ) (H,W) using bicubic interpolation: 𝚽 = Upsample b ​ i ​ c ​ u ​ b ​ i ​ c ​ ( Δ ​ 𝐏 ) \mathbf{\Phi}=\text{Upsample}_{bicubic}(\Delta\mathbf{P}) (4) where 𝚽 ∈ ℝ H × W × 2 \mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 2} . The final aligned image S b ^ S_{\hat{b}} is generated by spatially resampling the moving image S b S_{b} according to 𝚽 \mathbf{\Phi} : S b ^ = S b ∘ 𝚽 S_{\hat{b}}=S_{b}\circ\mathbf{\Phi} (5) This parameterization is, structurally, a free-form deformation model: any field in the network’s range is a bicubic lift of a coarse lattice, so smoothness is enforced architecturally , as high-frequency or folding fields are inexpressible, and is only refined, not created, by the regularizer below. It is the learned analogue of classical spline-based elastic registration [ 12 , 21 ] , with correspondence extraction and per-pair optimization amortized into one forward pass. 3.4 Training objective To quantify pixel-wise alignment between the reference S a S_{a} and the warped image S b ^ S_{\hat{b}} , we use the soft Dice loss [ 37 ] . Let N N be the number of pixels in the flattened images and k k index a batch of size B B : ℒ dice = 1 B ​ ∑ k = 1 B ( 1 − 2 ​ ∑ i = 1 N S b ^ , k ( i ) ​ S a , k ( i ) + ϵ ∑ i = 1 N S b ^ , k ( i ) + ∑ i = 1 N S a , k ( i ) + ϵ ) \mathcal{L}_{\text{dice}}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{k=1}^{B}\left(1-\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{\hat{b},k}^{(i)}S_{a,k}^{(i)}+\epsilon}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{\hat{b},k}^{(i)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{a,k}^{(i)}+\epsilon}\right) (6) On near-binary ridge imagery, this is directly interpretable as the fraction of structure brought into coincidence. Let V V denote the predicted control-point grid; we apply a first-order smoothness penalty ℒ smooth = 𝔼 ⁡ [ ‖ ∇ x V ‖ 2 + ‖ ∇ y V ‖ 2 ] \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla_{x}V\right\|^{2}+\left\|\nabla_{y}V\right\|^{2}\right] (7) and train with the combined objective ℒ total = ℒ dice + λ reg ​ ℒ smooth . \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{dice}}+\lambda_{\text{reg}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}. (8) No deformation ground truth, correspondence annotation, or identity label is used at any stage. 4 Experiments We train Gum-Net entirely on synthetic data and evaluate on synthetic as well as real-world data. Unlike [ 4 ] , we perform no fine-tuning or adaptation on real-world datasets: the model is evaluated zero-shot on real benchmarks, so all real-data results directly measure synthetic-to-real transfer of the learned registration function. 4.1 Datasets Real datasets. As real-world benchmarks providing organically generated elastic deformation, we evaluate on the FVC2004 benchmark [ 46 ] (110 fingers, 8 impressions each across 4 databases) and the Tsinghua Distorted Fingerprint Dataset (320 pairs of undistorted and distorted impressions) [ 47 ] . We use FVC2004 to quantify pre-matching structural alignment improvements via the Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC). Conversely, the Tsinghua dataset features severe real-world torque and complex skin distortion, and we use it exclusively to stress-test downstream minutiae matching behavior. Simulated datasets. We use Anguli [ 48 ] , a free and open-source synthetic fingerprint generator developed at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru, to generate 73,500 fingerprints: 2,100 distinct impressions per noise level per class, across five signal-to-noise ratios and all fingerprint classes of the Henry classification [ 49 ] . The generator first produces a master template image, then casts impressions from that template ( Figures 7 and 8 in the appendix show the templates and corpus diversity). Anguli is a procedural generator: it synthesizes orientation fields from mathematical models of singular points and grows ridge patterns by iterative filtering [ 48 , 50 ] . It is not a learned generative model fitted to real fingerprints, so synthetic training involves no real biometric data at any stage; real datasets are used strictly for evaluation. Beyond scale, this yields unlimited reference–moving training pairs with correspondence guaranteed by construction. 4.2 Training Figure 3: Inference plot obtained from test data. Topological validation via an independent minutiae matcher. A registration method can inflate similarity metrics by hallucinating structure; the real question is whether the predicted field restores true topology. Our testbed supplies an external oracle for exactly this question: minutiae are precise point landmarks consumed by matchers entirely independent of our training objective, so a topology-restoring warp raises genuine match scores, a hallucinating warp is penalized, and, since no warp should manufacture similarity between different fingers, imposter scores should remain unchanged. Concretely, we evaluate with the NIST Bozorth3 [ 56 ] matcher: distorted images are unwarped via the predicted fields, minutiae are extracted from the aligned images, and matching scores form genuine and imposter distributions whose separability is summarized by the decidability index d ′ = | μ g ​ e ​ n − μ i ​ m ​ p | / 1 2 ​ ( σ g ​ e ​ n 2 + σ i ​ m ​ p 2 ) d^{\prime}=|\mu_{gen}-\mu_{imp}|\,/\,\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma^{2}_{gen}+\sigma^{2}_{imp})} , where μ \mu and σ 2 \sigma^{2} denote the means and variances of the genuine and imposter score distributions. As summarized in Tables 1 and 3 , across both synthetic and real-world domains Gum-Net substantially increases genuine-pair scores ( Δ ​ μ g ​ e ​ n ≫ 0 \Delta\mu_{gen}\gg 0 ) with negligible perturbation of imposter pairs ( Δ ​ μ i ​ m ​ p ≈ 0 \Delta\mu_{imp}\approx 0 ), outperforming the unaligned baseline and the AP/MP variants. This asymmetry is what one would expect if the warps recover real structure: alignment increases similarity only where genuine correspondence exists, indicating that the predicted fields do not fabricate matches. These are distribution-level summaries rather than operating characteristics; full ROC/DET behavior of a complete recognition pipeline is beyond our pre-matching scope and left to future work. ORB in fact degrades baseline alignment on every dataset, which indicates that the deformation in these pairs lies outside the linear transformation class. 5 Conclusion We introduced 2D Gum-Net, an end-to-end unsupervised framework for dense non-rigid 2D image registration, obtained by transferring the original 3D Gum-Net [ 4 ] from volumetric cryo-ET alignment to two dimensions and replacing its rigid transformation module with a Dense Spatial Transformer, so that a smooth dense deformation field is predicted without ground-truth displacement annotations while DCT spectral pooling and control-point parameterization preserve the fine structure on which correspondence depends. Trained purely on synthetic fingerprint data and evaluated zero-shot, the method improves structural correlation by 10.5% (synthetic) and up to 15.7% (FVC2004), and on Tsinghua raises genuine match scores while leaving imposter scores essentially unchanged, confirming deformation quality through an external matcher rather than the training objective alone. Evaluation is restricted to a single domain by design and reports distribution-level summaries rather than operating characteristics; future work includes extending to other imagery where fine quasi-periodic structure meets elastic deformation, operational metrics on larger datasets, and coupling with fixed-length embeddings [ 33 ] for retrieval at scale.
