3 Materials and Methods 3.1 Task and framework Let X ∈ ℝ H × W × 3 X\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3} be an H&E image on a pixel domain Ω \Omega . The annotations consist of an instance map I I and a nuclear type map Y ∈ { 0 , 1 , … , C } H × W Y\in\{0,1,\ldots,C\}^{H\times W} , where zero denotes background. The required outputs are an instance map I ^ \widehat{I} and one type label for each predicted nucleus. During training, the network additionally predicts nuclear foreground probabilities N ^ \widehat{N} , horizontal–vertical displacement maps V ^ \widehat{V} , nuclear type probabilities P ^ \widehat{P} , and an auxiliary spatial target T ^ \widehat{T} . An encoder produces multi-resolution features E ⁡ ( X ) E(X) . Shared upsampling stages combine these features before the decoder separates them into two groups: a geometry group for foreground and displacement, and a semantic group for nuclear type and CSS. Figure 1 summarizes the computation. The CSS target is generated from the training annotations. At test time, the network takes the image alone; foreground and displacement predictions produce instances through HoVer-style post-processing, and type predictions assign a label to each instance [ 4 ] . Nuclear foreground (NP), horizontal–vertical displacement (HV), and nuclear type (TP) predictions are used for typed instance assembly. Class-conditioned spatial supervision (CSS) provides an annotation-derived auxiliary training target, and its head is omitted at inference. Encoder dilation and decoder self-attention form the local–global context module (LGCM), while squeeze-and-excitation (SE) supplies separate channel recalibration. (b) Dilated residual block with a shortcut connection. (c) Spatial self-attention with a learnable residual gate. Residual learning and atrous convolution provide the basis for its convolutional context extraction [ 26 , 27 ] , while dense connections support feature reuse within the decoder [ 28 ] . Together, these operations provide local and non-local image context. SE remains a separate channel-recalibration component. For a residual unit with input Z k − 1 Z_{k-1} , write Z k = 𝒜 k ​ ( 𝒢 k ​ ( Z k − 1 , r k ) ) + 𝒫 k ​ ( Z k − 1 ) , Z_{k}=\mathcal{A}_{k}\!\left(\mathcal{G}_{k}(Z_{k-1};r_{k})\right)+\mathcal{P}_{k}(Z_{k-1}), (5) where 𝒢 k \mathcal{G}_{k} is a bottleneck convolutional transformation with dilation r k r_{k} , 𝒫 k \mathcal{P}_{k} aligns shortcut channels and resolution, and 𝒜 k \mathcal{A}_{k} denotes the spatial alignment used before addition. Dilation increases the spatial sampling extent while retaining the convolutional kernel’s parameter count. R_{l}=R_{l-1}+(k_{l}-1)r_{l}j_{l-1},\qquad j_{l}=s_{l}j_{l-1}. (6) This relationship describes theoretical convolutional support. Self-attention then enables feature exchange across the spatial locations represented at the attention stage. Squeeze-and-excitation (SE) applies channel recalibration [ 30 ] and is controlled separately from LGCM in the experiments. This separation allows the contribution of dilation and spatial attention to be examined without conflating it with channel gating. 3.4 Grouped task decoder (GTD) GTD uses a shared decoder representation followed by group-specific transformations and separate prediction heads. PanMix is a private pancreatic histopathology collection with annotations for tumor, lymphocyte, fibroblast, acinar, and islet nuclear categories. Access is organized through an application procedure described on the associated GitHub page. CoNSeP provides colorectal H&E images with nuclear instance annotations; the analysis uses the four-category grouping associated with joint nuclei analysis [ 4 ] . PanNuke contains diverse tissue types and five nuclear classes: neoplastic, inflammatory, connective, dead, and epithelial [ 33 , 34 ] . Figure 3 summarizes the annotated nuclear class counts over each entire dataset. PanMix has similar counts across its five categories, whereas PanNuke contains a much smaller dead-cell population than its neoplastic and connective populations. The grouped CoNSeP labels also have unequal counts. This composition provides context for interpreting overall overlap and the class-specific results. These controls distinguish the use of class information from the mere presence of an auxiliary distance task. Removing class identity changes what the model is asked to encode, while class weights and Gaussian accumulation alter the spatial assignments. Their observed scores support the particular EDT construction in this control setting. The alternatives trained with one seed provide descriptive comparisons rather than a statistical ranking.
