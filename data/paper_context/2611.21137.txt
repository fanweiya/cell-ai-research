2.2 Vision Foundation Models and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation Large-scale self-supervised pretraining has produced vision backbones with transferable representations that can be reused across downstream tasks with limited task-specific supervision [ 11 ] . DINOv3 extends this capability through large-scale self-supervised pretraining, providing spatial representations that can be transferred to domains where labeled data are scarce. We use a general-purpose DINOv3 rather than an echocardiography-specific foundation model so that the supervision scheme remains modality-agnostic rather than tied to a single domain. Adapting large pretrained backbones to a target medical domain introduces a second challenge. Full fine-tuning of all backbone parameters increases computational cost and risk of overfitting [ 17 ] . Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods instead preserve most of the pretrained representation while restricting task-specific adaptation to a small number of parameters. LoRA introduces trainable low-rank updates into selected linear projections while keeping the original pretrained weights fixed, substantially reducing the number of trainable parameters. 3 Methodology 3.1 Overview Figure 1 : Overview of the proposed weakly-supervised temporal segmentation framework. A mean-teacher architecture with LoRA-adapted frozen DINOv3 encoders generates mixed connectivity-filtered pseudo-labels for dense Dice supervision. Partial cross-entropy supervises scribbles on the anchor frame, gated-CRF refines boundaries and an anchor-relative temporal loss enforces bidirectional consistency while allowing cardiac deformation As illustrated in Fig. 1 , we propose a weakly-supervised framework for segmenting echocardiographic sequences from scribble annotations on the anchor frame. The following sections describe each component in detail. 3.2 Segmentation of Echocardiographic Sequences from a Single Scribble Let X = { x 1 , x 2 , … , x T } X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{T}\} denote an echocardiographic sequence of T T frames. The model predicts a segmentation mask for every frame with supervision limited to a single scribble map S S defined on the anchor frame x m x_{m} while all remaining frames are unlabeled. The annotation map contains both foreground and background scribbles and the scribble assigns each pixel i i of x m x_{m} one of three values S ⁡ ( i ) = { 0 , background , 1 , foreground , 255 , ignore S(i)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{background},\\ 1,&\text{foreground},\\ 255,&\text{ignore}\end{cases} (1) The framework allows any frame in the sequence to be selected as the annotated anchor frame. The middle frame x m x_{m} provides a natural default by reducing the maximum propagation distance to approximately T / 2 T/2 frames. However, keyframes such as end-systole (ES) or end-diastole (ED) frames may also be selected. Supervision is subsequently propagated bidirectionally from the selected anchor to the remaining unlabeled frames. 3.3 Parameter-Efficient Encoding and Multi-Scale Decoding A frozen DINOv3 encoder is adapted using LoRA on the query and value projections of three transformer blocks, introducing 27.6 27.6 K trainable parameters corresponding to 0.03 % 0.03\% of the 85.7 85.7 M while preserving the pretrained representation under sparse supervision. Patch tokens are extracted from the three adapted blocks. Each token corresponds to a 16 × 16 16\times 16 pixel patch so a 512 × 512 512\times 512 input yields a grid of 32 × 32 32\times 32 tokens. Since the transformer block applies no spatial downsampling, all three LoRA block readouts share this resolution and differ only in semantic depth. They are resampled to strides 4 4 , 8 8 and 16 16 , producing 128 × 128 128\times 128 , 64 × 64 64\times 64 and 32 × 32 32\times 32 spatial feature maps. A fourth level at stride 32 32 is obtained by average pooling the stride- 16 16 map to 16 × 16 16\times 16 , which provides the decoder a coarser representation and a larger effective receptive field. Each pyramid level is independently projected using a 1 × 1 1\times 1 convolution followed by group normalization and ReLU. The projected features are then passed to a U-Net style decoder, beginning at stride- 32 32 level and progressively upsampled through three stages. At each stage, the upsampled decoder features are concatenated with the corresponding projected feature map from the pyramid. The final decoder output is converted to segmentation logits and bilinearly resized to the original input resolution. 3.4 Dense Supervision from Single Scribble-annotated Frame 3.4.1 Mean Teacher Construction and Scribble Supervision The framework maintains structurally identical student and teacher branches. The student is optimized directly through gradient descent while the teacher parameters are updated as an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student parameters. Since the DINOv3 backbone is frozen throughout training, its pretrained weights are shared between the two branches, while the LoRA adapters, feature projections and segmentation decoder are maintained separately and updated through the corresponding student and teacher mechanisms. For each training sequence, the student receives a weakly augmented view generated using brightness and contrast perturbations together with additive noise. These transformations modify image appearance while preserving spatial geometry, ensuring pixel-wise alignment between the student predictions and the supervision generated from the unaugmented sequence. Direct supervision is restricted to the annotated frame x m x_{m} , which is the only frame containing scribble annotations. All datasets are divided into training, validation and test sets using a 70:10:20 ratio. CAMUS contains echocardiographic sequences from 500 patients, from which we use the two-chamber (2CH) half-sequence subset. Ground-truth masks are provided for all frames, so the mask-derived scribble annotations is on the middle frame of the sequence. Performance is evaluated on three anatomical structures namely the left-ventricular endocardium ( L ​ V e ​ n ​ d ​ o LV_{endo} ), left-ventricular epicardium ( L ​ V e ​ p ​ i LV_{epi} ) and left atrium. CardiacNet comprises two pathology-specific cohorts: atrial septal defect (ASD) and pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). Segmentation masks are released for only a subset of the acquired sequences and a small number of the released files could not be read. We therefore use every sequence for which a usable annotation exists, giving 135 ASD and 299 PAH sequences. Unlike CAMUS, ground-truth masks are provided for approximately five frames within each sequence, so the scribble is placed on the middle annotated frame rather than the middle frame of the sequence. Both cohorts are evaluated on four cardiac structures: left ventricle (LV), left atrium (LA), right ventricle (RV) and right atrium (RA). Across both datasets, training supervision is restricted to one background and one foreground scribble with all remaining frames unlabeled. 4.2 Implementation Details All experiments were implemented in PyTorch with Python 3.12 and CUDA 13.2 and conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell GPU. We use DINOv3 ViT-B/16 as the vision foundation model backbone, with rank-3 LoRA adapters applied to the query and value projections of transformer blocks 3, 6 and 9. Single-channel ultrasound frames are used across three channels, resized to 512 × 512 512\times 512 , and normalized using the ImageNet channel-wise mean and standard deviation. Models are trained for 40 epochs using AdamW with a learning rate of 1 × 10 − 4 1\times 10^{-4} , weight decay of 1 × 10 − 4 1\times 10^{-4} and mixed-precision training. The partial cross-entropy, Dice, temporal consistency and gated-CRF losses are weighted by 1.0 1.0 , 0.5 0.5 , 0.1 0.1 and 0.1 0.1 respectively. The weights of the three unsupervised objectives are linearly ramped from zero to their respective values over the first five epochs. The teacher parameters are updated using an EMA decay of 0.99 0.99 and the connectivity-filtered teacher and student predictions are mixed using a pixel-wise Bernoulli mask with β = 0.5 \beta=0.5 . 4.3 Baseline and Evaluation Metrics We compare our method against a U-Net trained with pCE alone and four scribble-supervised methods: CutMix, CycleMix, ScribFormer and HELPNet. A consistent evaluation protocol is applied across all methods, with performance measured on all frames with available ground-truth annotations to assess generalization beyond the annotated frame. By combining a mean-teacher framework, a LoRA-adapted DINOv3 encoder and connectivity-filtered pseudo-labels with spatial and temporal losses, supervision is propagated across unlabeled sequences. Experiments on CAMUS and CardiacNet demonstrate consistent improvements over existing scribble-supervised methods in Dice and HD95 while substantially reducing false-positive predictions. These results show that annotation cost can be decoupled from sequence length without sacrificing reliable temporal segmentation.
