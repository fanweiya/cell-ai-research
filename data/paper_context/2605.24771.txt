Our study illustrates a productive feedback loop between learning theory and benchmark design. Classical noisy-label bounds [ 17 , 18 , 19 ] predict that a weak-supervision crossover exists and who governs the ceiling (the teacher rather than the student); our three-benchmark, six-architecture calibration locates where that crossover falls for modern biomedical VLMs ( n g ⋆ ≈ 100 n_{g}^{\star}\approx 100 on PCAM, 20 20 – 50 50 on ISIC 2019, and 250 250 – 500 500 on NIH ChestX-ray14) and confirms the 0.024 0.024 -AUC architecture-invariance band. Conversely, the structured-versus-random sign flip on NIH ChestX-ray14 (Table 4 ) and the > 20 × >20\times per-pathology crossover spread (Table 5 ) demonstrate that scalar rate-only noisy-label bounds are insufficient on multi-label clinical benchmarks, motivating our bias–variance decomposition (Theorem 3.3 ), toxic-class triage (Definition 3.4 ), and label-space projection refinement (Proposition 3.5 ). Recommendations for Clinical Benchmark Design and Reporting. Because downstream AUC under weak supervision is bounded by the VLM teacher’s bias floor δ \delta independently of student capacity (Section 6 ), clinical weak-supervision benchmarks should adopt three reporting standards: (i) report gold-only and weak-augmented scaling curves jointly rather than at a single arbitrary ( n g , n w ) (n_{g},n_{w}) operating point; (ii) report the VLM’s validation ceiling AUC ^ FM \widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\mathrm{FM}} alongside per-class toxicity flags ( AUC ^ FM c < 0.50 \widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}^{c}_{\mathrm{FM}}<0.50 ) so safety regressions on healthy-patient classes such as No Finding cannot hide behind macro-averages; and (iii) condition evaluations of noise-mitigation interventions (confidence filtering, GCE, Co-teaching) on whether the operating point lies below or above n g ⋆ n_{g}^{\star} . Limitations and Future Directions. First, our empirical grids evaluate in-distribution train/test splits within each benchmark; characterizing how cross-hospital distribution shift between the gold validation set and the unlabeled clinical pool perturbs the decision margin M ⁡ ( n g ) M(n_{g}) is an important next step. Second, while we evaluate two VLM labelers (BiomedCLIP across four prompt templates and PubMedCLIP) and six student architectures, extending the multi-VLM comparison to pathology-specialized foundation models such as PLIP [ 9 ] and CONCH [ 10 ] or multi-VLM consensus ensembles [ 2 , 16 ] will test how ensemble disagreement reduces error correlation ρ err \rho_{\mathrm{err}} . Third, our student experiments train decoupled classifiers; extending the crossover analysis to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA or linear probing) of the VLM backbone itself remains an open direction. Conclusion. VLM-generated weak labels are a powerful variance-reduction scaffold when expert annotations are scarce, but they impose a rigid, architecture-invariant bias ceiling once gold supervision suffices to match the VLM. By calibrating this noisy-label crossover across three clinical imaging modalities, six student architectures, and 15 thoracic pathologies—and distilling the theory into an audited, zero-fitting decision rule operable from a small validation split—this work provides clinical AI practitioners with a rigorous, actionable criterion for when to leverage foundation-model pseudo-labels and when to invest in expert clinical annotation.
