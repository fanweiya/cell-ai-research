4.1 Three closures and a shared experimental history The three closures of Section 3 share a campaign history H t H_{t} : proposed experiments, commands, recorded realized conditions, sensor observations, audit decisions and human interventions before trial t t . Each closure reads from this history and writes to it. Selection reads H t H_{t} and writes the next command. Execution writes the realized condition, which enters the record. The record then updates two quantities: the scientific belief and the estimate of execution reliability. Claim assessment reads the record and writes the claim state σ k \sigma_{k} . The loop closes only if the record stores the realized condition; otherwise the belief is updated from what was commanded rather than from what occurred (Figure 1 ). An experiment specification states the question, intended intervention or acquisition, specimen identity, measurement method, preconditions and interpretation criterion. Its record adds timing, delivered quantities, instrument settings, sample transformations and exceptions. Response models depend on realized conditions, making the distinction between intent and recorded execution necessary [ 34 , 67 , 68 ] . Figure 4 connects these layers to specimen and apparatus dynamics: the command acts through a latent realized condition, the trial record stores what was commanded, requested, observed and audited, and a remade specimen receives a new identifier so that lineage survives interruption. Operational observations must constrain the variable being interpreted: a balance measures delivery, whereas a camera view of a robot near a vessel need not. An observation informs a decision only through the variable it measures, so a correlated but different measurement moves the belief about the wrong quantity. Epistemic updates depend on the task—response-surface estimation, mechanism discrimination or acquisition allocation. The formulation fixes three interfaces: state ( s t , κ t ) (s_{t},\kappa_{t}) , observation ( o t , m t ) (o_{t},m_{t}) and decision a t a_{t} . Each downstream quantity is defined only through them. The belief depends on the observation interface, and the feasible set depends on the decision interface, so a change in either changes what the optimizer can learn or choose. Transition and measurement models must be estimated, and practical policies replace the exact filter with approximate inference and planning; that approximation is a property of the method and must be reported with it. 4.3 Partial observability and the experimental data-generating process The variables needed to control an apparatus need not be those needed to interpret its result. Such comparisons evaluate whether state estimation improves scientific decisions while retaining execution, calibration and confirmation records. 5 Epistemic layer: experiment selection and scientific reasoning 5.1 Terminal decision criteria governing method selection The epistemic layer compares Select and Acquire methods by their terminal decisions: recommend a design, predict a response over a domain, discriminate hypotheses, or choose a state-dependent action sequence. These decisions have different losses; comparisons therefore fix the scientific endpoint and resource boundary. Each method must also support the evidence cycle: traceable records, fault diagnosis, calibration and claim-specific follow-up (Section 3 ). Embodiment constrains the experiments a selector can deliver. Programmable synthesis supplies an execution interface [ 34 , 77 ] ; robot scientists and adaptive microscopy use its observations differently [ 7 , 53 ] . Matched comparisons specify five attributes: scientific objective; action type (preparation, intervention, acquisition or diagnosis); feedback timescale (within-operation, between-trial or between-campaign); state information (commands, telemetry, specimen history or latent estimates); and evidential endpoint. Table 3 connects each terminal decision to the assumptions required by a method family and to evidence from cited studies. Bayesian optimization. BO recommends a feasible design maximizing a measured response or Pareto trade-off. It requires qualified preparation and measurement, a bounded design space, and noise and feasibility models. Independent verification distinguishes an optimum from a noise-favored winner. Multi-fidelity BO has no automatic advantage over single-fidelity search: its value depends on approximate-fidelity cost and informativeness [ 78 ] . Anubis handles unknown feasibility and failed experiments explicitly [ 20 ] . Proxy misalignment, validity errors and unmodeled feasibility change the response functional represented by the fitted surface. Noisy expected hypervolume improvement targets batch multiobjective trade-offs [ 79 ] . Olympus compares selectors on shared noisy-response problems, including mixed-parameter and multiobjective extensions, while Atlas connects planning to laboratory execution [ 80 , 81 , 82 ] . These comparisons screen selectors; prospective campaigns must additionally test physical feasibility and whether the assay measures the application property. Active learning. Active learning minimizes predictive or representation loss over a declared target distribution specified independently of acquisition. Materials mapping, biological optimization and interpretable latent models illustrate coverage, response-guided sampling and low-dimensional discovery representations [ 76 , 83 , 75 ] . Each chooses where to measure next, but their terminal claims differ; acquisition score alone is not scientific utility. High variance can reflect irreducible noise or regions outside the target distribution, not reducible uncertainty. BO can find a good design without learning a global response map; active learning can improve prediction without improving the best response. Accordingly, verify a BO recommendation independently and assess an active-learning model on observations from the declared target distribution. Bayesian experimental design. Hypothesis-directed selection asks which explicit alternatives should govern the terminal decision. With h h the hypothesis variable, e e a feasible intervention or measurement design, Y Y the resulting observation and H t H_{t} the recorded history, the information-gain criterion is IG ( e ) = I ( h ; Y ∣ e , H t ) , \mathrm{IG}(e)=I(h;Y\mid e,H_{t}), (16) where I ( ⋅ ; ⋅ ∣ ⋅ ) I(\cdot;\cdot\mid\cdot) is conditional mutual information [ 32 ] . Information gain is an intermediate objective. A measurement can distinguish two models yet leave the decision unchanged if both imply the same intervention; a less informative measurement can be decisive because it changes which claim or experiment is selected. The terminal loss ℒ ⁡ ( θ , δ T ) \mathcal{L}(\theta,\delta_{T}) in Eq. 15 makes this distinction explicit. BED is appropriate when alternatives predict distinct observable distributions under feasible interventions and measurements; robot-scientist and biological model-development campaigns provide experimentally connected examples [ 33 , 74 ] . Observational equivalence under the feasible design class leaves the mechanism unresolved (Section 4.3 ); posterior concentration among misspecified alternatives therefore requires an external validation target. Report the alternatives considered and the decision changed by the selected observation. Reinforcement learning and model-predictive control. RL and MPC choose action sequences when intermediate specimen or apparatus states change later options. They require a sufficient state or belief, endpoint-aligned rewards or dynamics, and adequate interaction and reset budgets. AlphaFlow demonstrates sequential nanoparticle-processing decisions with spectroscopic feedback [ 63 ] . Proxy rewards can favor behavior unrelated to the final claim, and model-based safety guarantees depend on the model [ 19 , 84 ] . Under partial observability, model shift or reward mismatch, control return can diverge from scientific loss. Multi-fidelity value is a bias–cost–correlation trade-off. An approximate fidelity helps when it is cheaper than the terminal assay and remains informative about it once its bias is modeled. If it is cheap but weakly related to the endpoint, it consumes budget without moving the posterior about G resp G_{\mathrm{resp}} ; if its bias varies with design beyond the correlation model, it can mislead selection. Setup, cleaning, plate capacity and assay delay determine whether ten parallel designs cost less than ten sequential trials. Report costs per attempt, valid observation and elapsed campaign time. Pending outcomes enter acquisition through predictive distributions, not realized values. Delays change information, dependence and cost without changing a fixed terminal estimand; outcome-dependent completion, clock cutoffs or selective inclusion of pending results can instead change the observed target population and require explicit modeling. Resets, execution parameters and belief-sequential control. Scientific campaigns do not restart from identical states. A failed trial can consume a rare sample, dirty an apparatus or leave a specimen changed, so the reset assumption is a modeling choice. When preparation is expensive, irreversible or state-dependent, selection should value keeping options alive and act on the belief state of Eq. 12 , not on a fresh-start approximation. BO remains useful when intermediate observations leave later feasible actions unchanged, because a recipe can then be represented as one structured design. Execution parameters can themselves be optimized: above 100 cP, air-displacement pipettes fail to perform accurate and precise transfers, and multi-objective Bayesian optimization of aspiration and dispense rates reached transfers within 5% percentage transfer error for viscosities up to 1275 cP on the tested pipettes [ 59 ] , a result that depends on transfer volume. Such tuning changes which nominal designs are realizable. Diagnosis is a selection decision. Blanks, delivery measurements, references and orthogonal assays diagnose contamination, execution, drift and identity, respectively. Equation 5 values diagnosis by its cost-adjusted continuation relative to the best alternative first action, including stopping. It credits both improved decisions and direct claim resolution. Charge attempts, diagnosis and resets to the same resource boundary, and state whether missing outcomes represent infeasibility, censoring or unresolved evidence. Language models supply literature-grounded priors and proposals. BioDiscoveryAgent combines perturbation reasoning with numerical selection and reports improved prediction on evaluated datasets [ 93 ] . When proposals and evaluations reuse model-derived evidence, represent their dependence. Record the provenance of variables, constraints and candidates, including proposals originating outside the fitted model. Two further routes place learned models inside the numerical selector. LLAMBO frames BO in natural language so that a language model proposes and evaluates candidates conditioned on past evaluations, with results reported mainly on hyperparameter-tuning problems [ 14 ] ; PFNs4BO trains a network by in-context learning on a sampleable prior, allowing user hints about optimum location and learned acquisition functions [ 15 ] . In both cases the prior is encoded in weights or a prompt, not a stated covariance, so a campaign should record the prior and test it against a conventional Gaussian-process surrogate on its own data. Amortized design moves part of online design computation into offline training; language-model proposals provide an additional source of candidate structure. LIRA couples vision-language models to localization, inspection and reasoning for self-driving-lab workflows, adding closed-loop error detection where workflows otherwise run open-loop [ 72 ] . Its laboratory setting makes the distinction concrete: localization supplies an enabling state estimate, while an experimental measurement additionally requires a qualified physical readout. Contact sensing provides the direct observation of the realized condition for contact-dominated steps, extending perception beyond visual geometry. Vessel pose alone may leave immersion depth or contact force unresolved; the step requires a validated readout of its relevant physical variable, whether optical, tactile or force-based. Section 6.2 examines the criteria under which emerging tactile and force foundation models can serve as calibrated laboratory instruments, not task-specific policy embeddings; a model that has not been calibrated against a reference cannot yet be cited as a measurement. Transparent or reflective labware and gripper or lid occlusion obscure transfer completion. Pose estimates identify location, not contents or preparation history. Tip immersion, cap torque and septum-piercing force require contact measurements, and glass imposes tighter safety tolerances than many manipulation benchmarks. The consequence is an evaluation rule. Perception should be scored against the protocol-dependent quantity, with an independent measurement as reference: a balance for delivered mass, a force–torque sensor for contact, an orthogonal assay for identity. Error under the relevant conditions—occlusion, reflection, transparent labware, wet surfaces—and calibration drift over an extended campaign belong in the record, because cumulative thermal and mechanical drift over multi-day operation can misattribute an execution deviation to the specimen. Localization and inspection are enabling capabilities; they become scientific evidence when their error is stated on the variable the protocol tolerates. 6.2 Tactile and force foundation models as laboratory instruments The question a foundation model for contact has to answer in a laboratory is which physical quantity its readout supports and which decisions it informs. Tactile and force–torque sensing serve two complementary roles. As execution-governance signals they reveal slip, collision, seal integrity, vial seating and other contact events that determine whether a protocol step remains within tolerance. As primary scientific channels they can measure contact mechanics, rheology, modulus maps and wetting or adhesion responses. A representation used only for policy control need not itself be a force value, but a claim about a measured force or displacement requires traceability to a calibrated physical reference. Chemical or structural identity generally requires orthogonal spectroscopic or diffraction assays because contact signals do not uniquely identify composition or phase. Three capabilities therefore matter: representations that transfer across sensor units and wear, a readout calibrated to the quantity required by the named operation, and a drift record that makes the calibration interval a property of the record rather than an assumption. The available evidence establishes transferable representations and parts of the control-oriented readout. Self-supervised touch representations pre-trained on large tactile image collections transfer across vision-based tactile sensors and downstream tasks, and unified static–dynamic representations span four visuo-tactile sensors [ 69 , 70 ] . ForceVLA treats six-axis force as a first-class modality through a force-aware mixture of experts [ 71 ] . These results are reported on contact-rich manipulation benchmarks, where the endpoint is task success. They support tactile sensing for execution governance; a scientific contact-mechanics claim still requires calibrated measurements. A contact model should therefore be evaluated in two tracks. For execution governance, the test reports slip, collision, seal-integrity or seating detection against event labels and measures the resulting intervention rate, sample loss and protocol-valid rate. For contact as a scientific measurement, the test reports force, displacement or material-response error against an independent reference across sensor units, wear and temperature. Representation capacity and calibrated physical readout are separate evaluation targets. The drift record should state when the physical readout exceeds the protocol tolerance; without that record, a learned representation may support control while the scientific measurement remains unqualified. 6.3 Language planning and protocol compilation Language is the practical interface for intent, but a scientific protocol must reach hardware as a typed, checkable artifact before motion can mean the intended experiment. Several complementary mechanisms have emerged. Affordance-grounded planning scores proposed steps against estimated executability; runtime checks must still assess whether the selected step meets physical preconditions [ 5 ] . Code generation makes the plan’s assumptions explicit and executable: generated policy code composes perception APIs and parameterized control primitives, including feedback loops and logic [ 6 ] . The Embodied Agent Interface formalizes where such systems fail—goal interpretation, subgoal decomposition, action sequencing and transition modeling—and reports fine-grained errors, not final success only [ 98 ] . Laboratory systems bind these interfaces to domain tools. Coscientist and ORGANA connect language planning to hardware and tool calls [ 99 , 36 ] ; ChemAgents reports executed multistep synthesis-and-characterization tasks, including one in a different laboratory [ 92 ] . Earlier work already treats protocol as a first-class object through digitized synthesis and chemical programming languages [ 77 , 67 ] . The application synthesis distinguishes physical execution from software-led design assistance (Section 7 ). Language-mediated laboratory control needs an explicit reliability boundary. Empirical evaluations of autonomous chemistry agents, ORGANA and LLM agents for atomic-force microscopy report task- and benchmark-specific failures in planning, tool use and instrument operation [ 99 , 36 , 100 ] . An observed error rate describes one task, tool set and escalation policy, so it transfers only to that configuration, and a low rate leaves structural failure modes unchecked. A model can hallucinate a parameter absent from the protocol, express uncalibrated confidence in an uncertain interpretation, or emit a syntax-valid tool call whose physical preconditions or safety envelope is violated. Parameter provenance, confidence calibration, typed preconditions and runtime interlocks are therefore separate checks, and successful parsing alone is not evidence of a safe or scientifically valid operation. The mechanism of the gap is underspecification. A natural-language instruction such as “add the reagent and heat gently” leaves open identity, quantity, units, order, preconditions, acceptance thresholds and safety envelopes; neither affordance filtering nor fluent code generation resolves them, because the resolution is a domain decision, not a linguistic one. A protocol compiler—the function Γ \Gamma of Section 4 in its operational role—must therefore emit the parsed protocol together with resolved parameters, rejected alternatives, preconditions, safety authorization and compilation errors, and link that version to the realized effects it produced. Compilation errors and human disambiguations are evidence about the feasible experiment space, not noise to be hidden; a reader who can inspect the compiled protocol and its execution trace can distinguish a valid assay from a fluent plan. The compiled configuration m t cfg m_{t}^{\mathrm{cfg}} and the metadata packet m t m_{t} recorded afterwards are separate objects, and the compiler’s output belongs to the first while timing, lineage, calibration version and telemetry belong to the second; a comparison across runs is only meaningful when both are present and the realized condition x t x_{t} is measured independently of the command that produced it. 6.4 Visuomotor policies: tokenized, diffusion and flow action generation Many generalist policies share a skeleton of vision and language encoders feeding a transformer trunk, while the action decoder determines important precision and latency trade-offs. ACT introduced action chunking for fine bimanual tasks such as threading cable ties and slotting a battery: predicting a sequence of future actions reduces compounding error from per-step prediction [ 1 ] . Diffusion Policy models the action distribution with a denoising process, capturing multimodal continuous trajectories [ 37 ] ; π 0 \pi_{0} formulates the action expert as a flow-matching model for general robot control [ 2 ] . Autoregressive VLAs such as OpenVLA emit discrete action tokens and reuse language-model machinery [ 101 ] . These choices trade precision, multimodality and latency.
